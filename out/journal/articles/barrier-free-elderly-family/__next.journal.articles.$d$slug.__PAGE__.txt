11:T1ee6,<p>"Barrier-free costs money." Many inns stop thinking at that one line. True enough — renovating rooms or adding an elevator is not a realistic expense for a small inn. But the essence of barrier-free is not equipment; it is <strong>the design of flow</strong>. Make the signage clear, reduce the steps, build routes where a staff member can walk one step alongside. Without changing equipment, an inn becomes one where the elderly, the disabled, and families can all move. And today, this way of thinking is drawn as a line in law, not only in ethics.</p>

<h2>The line of the law — what cannot be refused, and what to tell in advance</h2>

<p>Since its enactment in 1948, the Ryokan Business Act has provided that a person seeking lodging must not, in principle, be turned away. The revision that took effect on December 13, 2023 — a response to customer harassment — added "specified demanding acts" (unreasonable demands for discounts, repeated demands for apologies, and the like) to the grounds for refusal, while at the same time making the drawing of the line explicit: <strong>"requests to remove social barriers" were clearly excluded from the grounds for refusal.</strong> What the government's materials list are: a wheelchair user asking for assistance transferring to the bed; a guest asking for written communication at the front desk; a guest with a visual impairment asking to be guided to the room; and the lodging itself of severely disabled guests and wheelchair users who need medical assistance. In other words, there is no path in law for refusing these as "excessive demands." With this line known, what the inn should do comes into view. Before the choice of refusing or accepting, <strong>tell correctly, in advance</strong> — in the pre-arrival email and the listing, write honestly what you can and cannot do. Preparation is not the inn's work alone; it becomes joint work with the guest.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>The Ryokan Business Act in principle prohibits refusing accommodation, and the revision effective December 13, 2023 explicitly excluded "requests to remove social barriers" (assistance with bed transfers for wheelchair users, written communication, guiding of visually impaired guests, accompaniment by assistance dogs, and the lodging of severely disabled guests and wheelchair users requiring medical assistance) from the grounds for refusal (Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare / government publicity)</strong> — Barrier-free response is not a domain of goodwill; it stands on the line drawn by law.</div>

<h2>The three segments, and one move for each</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The elderly — fall prevention and rest</strong> — Non-slip surfaces, night lighting at the feet, grab bars. And "a chair along the way." One chair in the middle of a long corridor is a rest point cheaper than a renovation and more certain than a floor guide.</li>
<li><strong>The disabled — width, transfer, information</strong> — The corridor width a wheelchair can pass, the transfer height to bed and toilet, the presence of an accessible toilet. And how information arrives — how to contact them in an emergency (vibration, a lamp), written communication at the ready. Physical barriers and information barriers are different jobs.</li>
<li><strong>Families — children’s safety</strong> — Outlet covers, where the kettle sits, the balcony lock. Plus extra beds and cribs. The family's barriers are, in fact, the easiest to find — crouch down and look around the room, and they appear.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The design of flow — signage, steps, "one step"</h2>

<p>Three moves that do not lean on equipment. (1) <strong>Make the signage clear</strong> — what is where becomes instantly apparent (work shared with the multilingual-signage minimum-line article). (2) <strong>Reduce the steps</strong> — a ramp at the corner, thresholds made visible, routes legible even to someone carrying luggage. (3) <strong>Routes where staff can add "one step"</strong> — from the front desk to the room, can you walk side by side with a wheelchair or a stroller? Adding a step is not assistance; it is the supply of ease. One more thing: do not forget the power of the pre-arrival email. "There is no elevator (the room is on the second floor)." "The accessible toilet is only in Room 1." "We can store one wheelchair." With these honest three lines alone, the guest can design their own trip.</p>

<h2>Writing the listing honestly — what you cannot do, together with why</h2>

<p>The reason to write honestly what you can and cannot do is the flip side of the law's line. If it cannot be refused, all the more reason to say so in advance. The form to write is "the fact you cannot + the alternative you can." "There is no elevator (the room is on the second floor). Our staff will carry your luggage." "The accessible toilet is only in Room 1. If you prefer another room, we can guide you to the nearest multipurpose toilet (a one-minute walk)." And photographs — the corridor's width, the grab bar's position, inside the accessible toilet. Photographs are believed before text.</p>

<h2>The other side of barrier-free</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The basis of "we can accommodate"</strong> — Write only what you have actually handled. An inn that writes "wheelchair accessible" without ever having received a wheelchair guest fails at its very first such stay. Build the receptacle before writing.</li>
<li><strong>The line between assistance and medicine</strong> — Assisting a transfer can be one of the inn's moves, but medical procedures such as suctioning or tube feeding belong to the qualified. Confirm the line, and keep contacts ready — the nearby clinic, home-care support.</li>
<li><strong>Staff bodies</strong> — Transfer assistance is also work that breaks backs. Learning how to assist (training courses, consulting local welfare facilities) is for the staff's safety, not only the guest's.</li>
<li><strong>The diversity of the elderly</strong> — Do not equate "elderly-friendly" with grab bars. There is a 75-year-old who walked the mountains here, and an 80-year-old who needs no rail. The standard is the person you asked, not the attribute.</li>
<li><strong>The fantasy of full renovation</strong> — Better than dreaming of a fully barrier-free building and doing nothing: removing one step and hanging one honest photograph reaches today's guest.</li>
<li><strong>Assistance dogs</strong> — The accompaniment of guide dogs and service dogs is covered by a separate law (the Act on Assistance Dogs for Persons with Physical Disabilities). Decide in advance the arrangements for their stay, including where they eat.</li>
</ul>

<p>The line of the law, I believe, should be read not as "do not refuse" but as "be prepared." The minimum unit of preparation: today, walk once from the entrance to a room at the width of a wheelchair. Wherever it will not pass — that is the first job. The equipment can wait; the design of flow can begin tonight.</p>

12:T19c1,<p>无障碍<strong>“流程设计”而不是“设备”</strong>。很多客栈把无障碍住宿视为“资金投入”，认为“无障碍住宿要花钱”。但从不同的角度来看，无障碍是“流程设计”。即使没有新设备，您也可以通过使标志清晰、减少步骤并创建员工可以向前迈出一步的流程来使设施无障碍。本文从流程设计而非设备角度来组织老年人、残疾人、家庭这三个细分市场以及应对的优先顺序。</p>

<h2>1. 老年人、残疾人、家庭三个细分领域和重点</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>老年人——防跌倒（防滑、照明、扶手）、休息点：</strong>老年人无障碍护理最重要的是预防跌倒。三件事很重要：防滑、照明、扶手。提供防滑浴室和走廊、夜间照明以及楼梯和卫生间的扶手。沿途设有休息点（椅子、长凳），让老年人可以安心出行。</li>
<li><strong>残疾人士 - 轮椅（宽度、转运、兼容厕所），如何接收信息：</strong>对于残疾人来说，最重要的无障碍设施是轮椅住宿。三点很重要：宽度（轮椅可以通过吗？）、转移（可以转移到床上或厕所吗？）、无障碍厕所（有轮椅无障碍厕所吗？）。信息传递方式（针对视力障碍人士的音频指导，针对听力障碍人士的书面指导）也很重要。</li>
<li><strong>家庭-儿童安全（插座、热水、阳台）、加床：</strong>家庭无障碍最重要的方面是儿童的安全。三样东西很重要：插座、水壶和阳台。我们管理插座盖、热水器位置和阳台钥匙。加床（加床和婴儿床）对于家庭来说也是一个重要的无障碍选择。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2.流程设计（从设备）——显示、步骤、流程线</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>澄清显示并减少步骤：</strong>无障碍流程设计最重要的方面是清晰的标记和减少台阶。清晰的显示屏让客人知道物品所在位置。减少台阶将使轮椅、婴儿车和老年人更容易走动。展示和台阶都是无障碍的，无需任何资金投入即可完成。</li>
<li><strong>让员工迈出“一步”的流程：</strong>在设计无障碍流程时，重要的是要创建允许员工向前迈出“一步”的流程线。示例：让工作人员引导您逐步从登记柜台到客房。工作人员一步步走来，让客人感觉可以安心前行。</li>
<li><strong>首先告诉“什么在哪里”（到达前电子邮件）：</strong>在设计无障碍流程时，首先传达“什么在哪里”非常重要。通过在客人抵达前向他们发送电子邮件，并诚实地告知他们可以做什么和不能做什么，例如“没有电梯（二楼）”或“只有 1 号房间可以使用厕所”，客人会觉得自己可以提前做好准备。</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. 写在清单中——诚实地说明你能做什么和不能做什么</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>诚实地说明你能做什么和不能做什么——示例：“没有电梯（二楼）”“唯一可用的厕所是 1 号房间”：</strong>在房源中撰写有关无障碍住宿的内容时，请诚实地说明您可以做什么和不能做什么。通过诚实地写下“没有电梯（2楼）”、“只有1号房间有无障碍厕所”、“只能容纳一辆轮椅”等内容，客人将能够了解该房间是否适合自己。</li>
<li><strong>诚实的陈述可以提高询问的质量：</strong>诚实展示无障碍住宿将提高查询质量。如果你写“能做”一些不能做的事情，客人会不满意并说：“我来这里以为我能做到。”通过诚实地写下您可以做什么和不能做什么，客人可以选择适合他们的住宿。</li>
<li><strong>用照片传达无障碍住宿：</strong>通过照片传达无障碍住宿也很重要。准备无障碍厕所的照片、轮椅可以通过的走廊宽度、扶手的照片等，并将其发布在房源上。照片可帮助客人直观地了解您的酒店设有残疾人通道。</li>
</ul>

<h2>行动清单</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>第一周：分3段绕宿舍走动（我们自己）</strong> — 围绕旅舍走三圈：老人、残疾人、家庭。列出您可能跌倒的地方、轮椅无法通过的地方以及您担心孩子安全的地方。</li>
<li><strong>第2周：优先维护防跌倒、宽度、陈列</strong> — 优先考虑三点：防跌倒（防滑、照明、扶手）、宽度（轮椅能否通过）、标识（什么在哪里？）。从不需要资本投资就能做什么开始。</li>
<li><strong>第 3 周：在清单中诚实地写下您可以做什么和不能做什么</strong> — 在房源中诚实地写出无障碍住宿的信息。明确说明您可以做什么和不能做什么，例如“没有电梯（二楼）”和“只有 1 号房间有无障碍厕所。”</li>
<li><strong>第 4 周：我们将向您发送一封抵达前电子邮件，告诉您什么在哪里。</strong> — 在您抵达之前发送一封电子邮件，告诉他们有关无障碍住宿的信息以及哪里可以提供无障碍住宿。通过诚实地告知自己能做什么和不能做什么，客人可以提前做好准备。</li>
<li><strong>续：将无障碍设计改进为流程设计</strong> ——不断完善无障碍设计，将其视为“流动设计”而非“设施”。我们将继续使标志更加清晰，减少步骤，并建立流程线，让工作人员能够遵循。</li>
