16:T203a,<p>1996年、アムステルダムの一つの小さな会社が、ホテルの予約をインターネットで受け始めました。それが、いまや世界227の国と地域の宿が並ぶ、巨大なマーケットになっている。あなたの宿も、そのマーケットの一角に店を出しています。問題は、その店がいつも開いていて、客の目に留まり、選ばれているか。予約サイトで選ばれるまでの道のりは、実は4つの段に分かれています。検索されて、順位が上がって、クリックされて、予約される。この記事は、4段それぞれの一手を整理する話です。</p><div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Booking.comは1996年にアムステルダムで創業(当初はBookings.nl、2000年に現名称)。現在は世界227の国と地域に宿を掲載し、日本では2016年2月から楽天トラベルとの提携も始まっている(会社公表・業界メディアによる)</strong> — 宿が店を出すマーケットは、まだ30年ほどの歴史しかない。</div><h2>段の1 — 検索 — 見つかること</h2><p>旅人が検索窓に打つのは、たいてい「地名」と「条件」です。そして結果を絞るのは、フィルターの属性——朝食付き、Wi-Fi、駐車場、ペット可。ここでの一手は2つ。①タイトルに「地域+属性」を入れる(○○駅から徒歩5分、海の見える温泉宿)。②フィルターの属性を正確に設定する。朝食を出すのに「朝食なし」のままでは、朝食を探している旅人は、永遠にあなたの宿にたどり着きません。フィルターは宿の索引です。間違った索引の本は、書架の奥に消えます。</p><h2>段の2 — 順位 — 上に載ること</h2><p>予約サイトの順位は、いちばん気になる不思議の一つです。Booking.com自身はこれを完全には明かしていませんが、パートナー向けに「順位は単一の要因では決まらない」と説明した上で、なかでも最も重いのが、閲覧されたページが実際に予約につながる割合——コンバージョンであることを公表しています。</p><div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Booking.comはパートナー向けに、検索順位は単一の要因では決まらないこと、なかでも「閲覧から予約への転換(コンバージョン)」が最も重い指標であることを説明している(パートナー向け情報・複数の業界解説による)</strong> — 順位は努力でなく成果で評価される。予約を取る宿がさらに上に載る構造である。</div><p>この事実が意味するのは、順位対策とは「頑張りを見せること」ではなく「選ばれるListingを作ること」——そういうことです。4つの段は一本道ではなく、循環しています。選ばれる宿は順位が上がり、順位が上がった宿はさらに選ばれる。逆に言えば、最初の一歩(見つかり、クリックされ、予約される)さえ踏めば、輪は回り始めます。順位を動かす素材として挙げられるのは、空き室の充実(掲載率)、問い合わせへの返信の速さと率、レビューのスコアと件数、そして価格の競争力です。加えて、宿側の都合でのキャンセルは、順位への打撃として重く扱われるとされています。</p><h2>段の3 — クリック — リストで押されること</h2><p>検索結果には、何十件もの宿がサムネイルと価格とスコアを並べて並びます。ここで押されるのは、最初の1枚の写真です。写真が選ばれる理由と撮り方は「写真が9割」の記事に譲りますが、予約サイトの2枚目以降の写真は、1枚目に興味を持った人にしか見られない——この原則は、地図の話(ローカルSEOの記事)と同じです。もう一つは価格とスコアの見え方。総額がいくらか(税・サービス料込みか)は、クリックの前に読まれます。</p><h2>段の4 詳細ページで決められること</h2><p>クリックした旅人が次に読むのは、写真の続き(おおむね8〜10枚は欲しい)、説明文、そしてポリシーです。キャンセルの条件、チェックインの時間、子どもやペットの扱い。曖昧な部分があると、旅人は不安になって戻ります。説明文を英語で書く話(どんな宿を一文で伝えるか)は英語Listingの記事に、そして料金の見え方(隠れたコストを作らない)は総額の話として別の記事に譲ります。ここで押さえるべきは、詳細ページの仕事は「魅力を足すこと」ではなく「予約の邪魔を減らすこと」にある、ということです。</p><h2>開始からの90日</h2><p>新しい 1b:T1c7c,<p>In 1996, one small company in Amsterdam began taking hotel bookings over the internet. It has since become a giant marketplace where properties in 227 countries and regions stand side by side. Your inn, too, has opened a shop in one corner of that market. The question is whether that shop is always open, catching the customer's eye, and being chosen. The road to being chosen on a booking site in fact divides into four stages: be searched, rank high, be clicked, be booked. This article organizes the one move for each of the four.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Booking.com was founded in Amsterdam in 1996 (initially Bookings.nl; the present name since 2000). It now lists properties across 227 countries and regions, and in Japan it began a partnership with Rakuten Travel in February 2016 (per company disclosures and industry media)</strong> — The marketplace where inns open their shops has a history of barely thirty years.</div>

<h2>Stage 1 — Search — being found</h2>

<p>What travelers type into the search box is usually "place name" and "conditions." And what narrows the results is the filter attributes — breakfast included, Wi-Fi, parking, pets allowed. Two moves here. (1) Put "region + attribute" into the title (a five-minute walk from the station; a hot-spring inn with ocean views). (2) Set the filter attributes accurately. If you serve breakfast but remain listed as "no breakfast," the traveler searching for breakfast will never, ever reach your inn. Filters are the inn's index. A book with the wrong index disappears into the back of the shelves.</p>

<h2>Stage 2 — Rank — placing high</h2>

<p>The ranking on a booking site is one of its greatest mysteries. Booking.com does not fully disclose it, but for partners it explains that rank is not determined by any single factor — and discloses that the heaviest of them is conversion: the share of page views that actually lead to bookings.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Booking.com explains to partners that search ranking is not decided by any single factor, and that "conversion from view to booking" is the heaviest metric (per partner-facing information and multiple industry analyses)</strong> — Rank is judged not by effort but by results. It is a structure in which the inns that take bookings rise further.</div>

<p>What this fact means is that rank measures are not "showing how hard you try" but "making a listing that gets chosen" — that is what it comes to. The four stages are not a one-way road; they circulate. Chosen inns rise in rank; inns that rise in rank get chosen more. Put the other way: once the first step is taken — found, clicked, booked — the wheel starts turning. The materials cited for moving rank are completeness of availability, the speed and rate of replies to inquiries, review score and count, and price competitiveness. On top of that, cancellations made at the property's own convenience are treated as a heavy blow to rank.</p>

<h2>Stage 3 — Click — being pushed in the list</h2>

<p>Search results line up dozens of inns with thumbnails, prices, and scores. What gets pushed here is the first photo. Why photos get chosen, and how to take them, is left to "The Photo Is 90%" — the second photo onward is seen only by people the first one interested, a principle shared with the map story (the local SEO article). The other factor is how price and score read. What the total comes to (whether tax and service charge are included) is read before the click.</p>

<h2>Stage 4 — Book — being decided on the detail page</h2>

<p>What the traveler who clicked reads next: more photos (you want roughly eight to ten), the description, and then the policies. Cancellation terms, check-in times, the handling of children and pets. Any ambiguity makes the traveler anxious and sends them back. Writing descriptions in English (conveying what kind of inn in one sentence) belongs to the English-listing article, and how prices look (creating no hidden costs) to the total-price story in another article. What to hold onto here is this: the detail page's job is not "adding appeal" but "removing obstacles to booking."</p>

<h2>The first ninety days</h2>

<p>A new listing has its first ninety days of laying the foundation. Two things to lay. (1) Speed and rate of replies (answer inquiries the same day; this is itself material for rank). (2) The early reviews. Ask the people who stayed whether they might write a few words. Count grows only by accumulation — but the first ten weigh more than the hundred that follow. How these ninety days are built becomes the foundation of the rank afterward.</p>

<h2>The other side of the four stages</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>What the commission pays for</strong> — Booking sites take a commission of roughly 15–25% (a reference value). An infuriating number — but it is the cost of bringing in "people who do not yet know your inn." It has meaning only as the cost of new acquisition, set inside a design that grows repeats into direct.</li>
<li><strong>Price alignment</strong> — Offering the same room higher than on other booking sites or on direct is said to lower your rank. The reverse — cheaper than direct — erases the meaning of direct. Align prices across the waterways.</li>
<li><strong>The risk of holding back rooms</strong> — Narrowing availability "because we don't know if bookings will come" lowers your listed availability and reduces the material of rank with your own hands. A strategy of listing only in high season demands double caution.</li>
<li><strong>Over-softening policies</strong> — Free cancellation increases bookings — and no-shows too. Flexibility is a material to be used differently in seasons that fill easily and seasons that do not.</li>
<li><strong>Chasing rank alone</strong> — Rank is a result. Make rank the purpose and the listing becomes discounts and cramming; the disappointment of the stay returns as a review. The substance is the experience of staying.</li>
</ul>

<p>A listing is "a sales representative who works twenty-four hours." While you sleep, it stands before travelers on the far side of the globe. What to do this week is small: walk your inn's page through the four stages — search, rank, click, book — one at a time, and write down, in one line, the cause of the weakest stage. The wheel starts turning from there.</p>
