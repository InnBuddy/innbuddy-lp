11:T1d43,<p>Sleep is <strong>not "the bed" but "a choosable experience."</strong> The mountain quiet, the sound of the waves, the darkness of night — the environment is good, and yet failing to sleep because the pillow does not fit is a waste. The most common complaint about sleep is, in fact, "the pillow did not suit me." Too hard, too low, a smell that bothers. With one pillow, the satisfaction of sleep swings widely. This article organizes the pillow menu as "experience design" — a foundation edition.</p>

<h2>Kusamakura — travel was sung through the pillow</h2>

<p>In old Japanese song there is the word <strong>kusamakura</strong> — the grass pillow. Since the age of the Man’yōshū, it has been one of the <strong>makurakotoba</strong> — the set phrases placed before certain words when singing of travel. Begin with "kusamakura, tabi ni shi yukeba…" ("on a journey of grass pillows…"), and it is a travel poem. Strangely, in this country’s world of words, <strong>travel has been sung bound to the pillow</strong>. That the fixed decorative phrases that crown a word are themselves called "pillow words" (makurakotoba) is, you could say, a vestige of the pillow being what supported "the beginning of things."</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>"Kusamakura" has been used repeatedly in waka poetry since the Man’yōshū as the makurakotoba (a set modifier phrase placed before certain words) attached to "travel" (tabi). Expressions binding travel and pillow — "the pillow of a journey," "not changing one’s pillow" — survive in numbers in the Japanese classics</strong> — People of old entrusted the verdict on a night’s lodging to the pillow. (Traditional expression of waka poetry since the Man’yōshū; general knowledge of the classics.)</div>

<p>This is not metaphor but the very origin of the inn’s work. The traveler’s day ends at the moment the head is laid on the pillow. Superb meals, a magnificent bath — <strong>if sleep fails, the trip is remembered as a failure</strong>. As the Man’yō people sang travel through the pillow, the modern guest remembers the property in the words of sleep. "I slept well." Stacking that one phrase is the work of the property’s sleep experience.</p>

<h2>Designing the pillow menu — height, material, display</h2>

<p>A pillow menu is a mechanism of preparing pillow options and letting the guest choose. The mountain quiet belongs to the location; the darkness, to the room. The remaining piece — the pillow — is the last component of sleep the property can control.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Height — 3 kinds: low, standard, high</strong> — Pillow preference divides by person. Three heights cover most tastes. Start with three. Before adding a fourth and fifth, record which height is chosen.</li>
<li><strong>Material — 2 standards, and 1 regional material</strong> — Alongside the standards like feather and low-resilience foam, one material of the land — buckwheat hulls, for instance. Near buckwheat fields, "local soba hulls" — with one line of story on the material, the pillow turns from equipment into experience.</li>
<li><strong>Display — one card in the room</strong> — Hide the pillows deep in the closet, and the menu does not exist. Place one photo card in the room with the line "we lend pillows to your preference — please ask the front desk." Half of any option is its announcement.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Operation — cleanliness, records, one question</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Cleanliness — double covers</strong> — These touch skin. Covers in two layers (one waterproof), the outer exchanged at every lending, the body aired in the shade regularly. A property that writes "lending pillows get a fresh cover every time" is trusted more than one that does not.</li>
<li><strong>Records — one line per lending</strong> — Which pillow, chosen how often. One line of record becomes the grounds for purchasing and rotation (the manners of recording are shared with <a href="#guest-data-basics">Guest Data Basics: What to Record, and How to Use It</a>).</li>
<li><strong>One question at check-out</strong> — "Did you sleep well last night?" This single question yields material for both pillow improvement and reviews (review operations are handled by <a href="#review-management-3-numbers">Review Management: The Basics of Score, Count, and Recency</a>).</li>
</ul>

<h2>The environment of sleep — reviewing quiet and darkness</h2>

<p>Once guests can choose pillows, the next thing that works is reviewing the environment. The thinking on the room’s sound (the design of unbroken quiet, insect voices, wind chimes) and on scent is handled by <a href="#scent-music-five-senses">Scent & Sound — A Stay the Five Senses Remember</a>. The pillow fits, it is quiet, it is dark — a room with the three points of sleep becomes the most retold equipment in the property. "The sleep I slept at that inn" is word of mouth stronger than any photograph.</p>

<h2>The other side — when the menu exhausts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Too many options</strong> — Past five kinds, guests tire before choosing. The joy of choosing plateaus "around three."</li>
<li><strong>A hole in hygiene</strong> — Lax cleaning frequency on lent pillows turns the pillow into the most intimate and most dangerous equipment. Decide the exchange and airing frequency in advance.</li>
<li><strong>Pushing the menu</strong> — Asking "how about a pillow?" to everyone at check-in turns the pillow into a persistent sales item. Place the card, and wait. The one who chooses is always the guest.</li>
<li><strong>Loss and compensation</strong> — Lent pillows do occasionally get taken. Decide the policy on compensation and write it in one line of the card (not a design that charges heavily — a design that does not panic when it happens).</li>
<li><strong>The real cause of "could not sleep"</strong> — If "could not sleep" continues even after changing pillows, the cause is elsewhere. The neighboring room’s sound, the corridor light, the morning cleaning noise — in the check-out question, listen for words other than pillows.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is three pillows and one card. Low, standard, high. This week, line up the three behind the front desk and place the card in the rooms. From the age when Man’yō travelers slept in the open on grass pillows, the pillow has decided the outcome of a journey — <strong>the choosable pillow</strong> is the most modern form of that origin.</p>

12:T174b,<p>睡眠是<strong>“选择一种体验”而不是“选择一张床”</strong>。山的寂静、海浪的声音、日本乡村夜晚的黑暗——即使环境再好，但因为枕头不合适而无法入睡也是一种浪费。客人最常见的睡眠问题是枕头不合适。它太硬、太低、太高，而且我担心气味。只需一个枕头就可以极大地提高您的睡眠满意度。本文将枕头菜单的设计整理为一种“体验设计”，而不仅仅是一种便利设施，它在不产生成本的情况下创造了“选择的睡眠体验”。</p>

<h2>1. 为什么选择枕头菜单 — 3 项睡眠投诉</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>最常见的抱怨是枕头不合适：</strong>评论说他们睡不好的首要原因是枕头。即使床和被褥相配，如果枕头不相配，你也睡不着。另一方面，如果枕头合适，说“我睡得很好”的评论就会增加。枕头是睡眠满意度的关键。</li>
<li><strong>能够选择适合您的住宿：</strong>一家只有一种枕头的酒店就是一场关于它是否合适的赌博。您可以选择三种类型的住宿。通过选择，客人会觉得住宿是适合他们的。能够选择是待客之道的基础。</li>
<li><strong>费用每间房几千日元，效果回顾：</strong>准备三种枕头的费用为每个房间数千日元。然而，随着“我睡得很好”的评论数量增加，OTA 评分也会上升，预订数量也会增加。枕头不是“成本”，而是“增加点评的投资”。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2.设计三点：高度、材质、呈现</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>高度 — 3 种类型：低、正常和高：</strong>枕头的高度因人而异。有的人喜欢低枕头，有的人喜欢高枕头。通过提供三种不同的高度，我们可以满足 80% 客人的喜好。高度将显示为“低、正常或高”，您可以通过触摸进行选择。</li>
<li><strong>材料 — 羽毛、记忆海绵、荞麦壳和其他当地材料：</strong>有羽绒枕、记忆棉枕、荞麦壳枕三种。如果你加入一种当地食材，它就会成为一个故事。例如：来自山上的荞麦壳，如果您住在海边，则在海滩微风中干燥的荞麦壳，如果您在日本农村，则来自当地农民的荞麦壳。如果材料有故事，工作人员就会想讲述它。</li>
<li><strong>如何呈现 — 房间里一张卡片上印有照片的“枕头菜单”：</strong>不要将枕头藏在衣柜里，而是将它们展示在客房的一张卡片上，并附上它们的照片作为“枕头菜单”。照片让客人更容易明白他们有选择。该卡应包含以下信息：“如果您睡得很好，请在前台告诉我们。我们还借给你枕头。”</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 运营——清洁、管理以及如何连接评论</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>清洁度 — 始终双层盖：</strong>枕头是直接与皮肤接触的东西。确保有双层盖，其中一个防水盖。每月一次将枕头放在阳光下或阴凉处晾干。清洁度应该是可见的。通过更换枕套向客人展示您的枕套有多干净。</li>
<li><strong>管理——在一张纸上记录贷款：</strong>在一张纸上记录租金，以跟踪哪些枕头受欢迎。办理入住手续时，询问“有枕头可以租吗？”如果借用，请记录下来。我们每月一次分析哪些枕头最受欢迎。购买额外受欢迎的枕头并更换不受欢迎的枕头。</li>
<li><strong>如何连接到评论：</strong>退房时问：“昨晚睡得好吗？枕头怎么样？”对于晚上睡得好的客人，我说：“如果您写评论说：“我睡得很好。”我会很高兴。睡眠满意度与评论直接相关。</li>
</ul>
