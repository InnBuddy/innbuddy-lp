豊かに見せる組み合わせの知恵</h2><p>日本の朝の定番、<strong>一汁三菜</strong>。ご飯と汁と香の物、そして主菜が1品。栄養と美学の設計とされますが、コストの面から見ると、これは見事な組み合わせです。ご飯・汁・香の物は安くお腹を満たし、原価を<strong>主菜の1品に集中</strong>させる。皿数は多いのに、高い素材は1つ。原価を守りながら卓を豊かに見せる、江戸の食卓のアセンブリ設計だった、と考えることもできます。</p><p>この「基本は安く豊かに、原価は1点に集中」という構造は、そのまま現代の朝食の単価設計に使えます。</p><h2>計算式 — 4つの数字</h2><p><strong>朝食売上=宿泊人数×付帯率×単価+追加売上</strong>。この式に現れる数字は、4つだけです。</p><ul><li><strong>宿泊人数</strong> — 前泊の人数。朝食の入口となる数字。</li><li><strong>付帯率</strong> — 宿泊客のうち朝食を食べる人の割合。写真と案内で動く数字。</li><li><strong>単価</strong> — 朝食の価格。部屋代に込む含み値か、単品かで、自由度が変わります。</li><li><strong>追加売上</strong> — 地元牛乳、果物、1品追加。朝食の中の小さな売り場。</li></ul><h2>計算を歩く — 1b:T1d7f,<p>Breakfast is <strong>not "a service" but "revenue that can be calculated."</strong> Satoyama vegetables, the sea’s bounty, local eggs — the more a property has the story of its ingredients, the more breakfast becomes an account of "revenue + experience." This article organizes the formula of that account — a foundation edition (all figures are reference values).</p>

<h2>Ichiju-sansai — the wisdom of combination that looks abundant</h2>

<p>Japan’s standard morning, <strong>ichiju-sansai</strong> — one soup, three dishes. Rice, soup, pickles, and one main dish. It is regarded as a design of nutrition and aesthetics, but seen from the cost side, it is a splendid combination. Rice, soup, and pickles fill the stomach cheaply, and <strong>concentrate the food cost on the single main dish</strong>. Many plates on the table, but only one expensive ingredient. You can read it as the assembly design of the Edo dining table — keeping the cost while making the table look abundant.</p>

<p>This structure — "the base, cheap and abundant; the cost, concentrated on one point" — transfers, as is, to the modern design of breakfast pricing.</p>

<h2>The formula — 4 numbers</h2>

<p><strong>Breakfast revenue = guests × attach rate × price + add-on revenue.</strong> Only four numbers appear in this formula.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Guests</strong> — The previous night’s headcount. The number at the entrance of breakfast.</li>
<li><strong>Attach rate</strong> — The share of staying guests who eat breakfast. A number that photographs and guidance move.</li>
<li><strong>Price</strong> — The price of breakfast. Whether it rides inside the room rate as an embedded value or stands alone changes the degrees of freedom.</li>
<li><strong>Add-on revenue</strong> — Local milk, fruit, one extra dish. The small marketplace inside breakfast.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Walking the calculation — a month in reference values</h2>

<p>Take a property where 20 guests in 10 rooms stay, with an attach rate of 50% and a price of ¥1,500 (all reference values). The diners: 20 × 50% = 10. A day’s breakfast revenue: 10 × ¥1,500 = <strong>¥15,000</strong>. Over 30 days, <strong>¥450,000</strong>. Add "+¥500 for a small bowl of the sea’s bounty," and if 4 guests order a day, that is ¥2,000 — over 30 days, <strong>¥60,000</strong>. At a food-cost rate of 35%, the gross profit on ¥450,000 is about ¥290,000. The places a number can move are just four. Every breakfast discussion comes down to which of the four to move.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">SIMULATION</span><strong>At a property of 10 rooms / 20 guests, attach rate 50%, price ¥1,500 (all reference values), a day’s breakfast revenue is ¥15,000 — ¥450,000 over 30 days. A +¥500 add-on dish ordered by 4 guests a day adds ¥60,000 a month. At a food-cost rate of 35%, the gross profit on ¥450,000 is about ¥290,000</strong> — Move the four numbers one at a time, and you can see which one moves the result best.</div>

<h2>The 3 levers</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Raise the attach rate</strong> — One line at check-in: "Tomorrow’s breakfast stars the satoyama vegetables." The only lever that moves at zero cost. One breakfast photograph works the same way (the craft of photographs is in <a href="#hotel-photos-90">"The Photo Is 90%": What Bookable Hotel Photos Have in Common</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Design the price</strong> — Make it two tiers: a base plus optional add-ons. The base, cheap and abundant (the ichiju-sansai structure); on the add-ons, concentrate the cost and the story.</li>
<li><strong>Voice the add-ons</strong> — One sentence at serving: "We also have coffee made with local beans, if you’d like it." The voice, once only. Pushing lowers not the price but the rating.</li>
</ol>

<p>Watching <strong>gross profit</strong>, not revenue — that is the formula’s hidden promise. Record prep volumes and cut food loss (<a href="#food-loss-kitchen">Food Loss: Reducing Prep in the Kitchen</a>), write the producers’ names on the menu (<a href="#idea-breakfast-local-story">Convey Region with Breakfast - Ideas for Breakfast Putting Regional Story on One Dish</a>) — gross profit and story rise on the same line.</p>

<h2>Raising the price through form and stage</h2>

<p>The design of the breakfast’s form (how it is laid out and shown) is handled by <a href="#breakfast-buffet-media">Turning Breakfast Into Media: Buffet Design</a>. And the idea of moving the breakfast’s stage outside the dining room — breakfast eaten in the forest, for instance — is another direction for raising the price (<a href="#forest-picnic-breakfast">Forest Picnic: Designing Breakfast Eaten in Nature</a>). The "price" inside the formula is a number that can be pushed from three directions: taste, form, and stage.</p>

<h2>The other side — when the numbers exhaust the breakfast</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The fantasy of a 100% attach rate</strong> — Some guests want to skip breakfast. Force it up, and wasted prep and lowered satisfaction arrive together. The attach rate is less something to raise than something to know first.</li>
<li><strong>Raising the price without a story</strong> — Price is the last lever. An unexplained ¥300 is a number that can drop a review star (the foundations of price-setting are handled by <a href="#pricing-fundamentals">How to Set Room Rates: Pricing Fundamentals</a>).</li>
<li><strong>The freedom of the embedded value</strong> — Breakfast folded into the room rate cannot be re-priced. If you want to design the price, it starts by moving it back onto "a visible price."</li>
<li><strong>Pushing the add-ons</strong> — The voice, once; on the menu, one line. The calm of the morning hour has no need of the air of selling.</li>
<li><strong>Being thrown by the numbers</strong> — Do not change policy in a month when the attach rate moved two points. Breakfast numbers are read on the slope of three months.</li>
</ul>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">DID YOU KNOW?</span><strong>Of the four numbers, the only one that moves without spending is the "attach rate." Guest count is set by occupancy; price and add-ons require cost and courage. Precisely because it moves with one line of voice and one photograph, it is chosen as the first lever</strong></div>

<p>The first step is to write this month’s four numbers (guests, attach rate, price, add-ons) on one sheet of paper. The moment you write them out, you notice which number has been left "undecided." Monetizing breakfast can begin without harming its deliciousness — the formula is already turning in your dining room.</p>
