11:T1dc8,<p>An earthquake-response manual is <strong>not "a thick booklet" but "the first 30 minutes."</strong> In the 30 minutes right after the shaking, almost all of the guests’ safety and trust is decided. However splendid the booklet, a manual not opened in the first 30 minutes might as well not exist. This article is the foundation for those 30 minutes on one sheet.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>In the Great Kantō Earthquake (M7.9) of September 1, 1923, the dead and missing numbered about 105,000, of which about 90% (about 92,000) were lost to fire (Cabinet Office Disaster Management White Paper figures). The quake struck at lunchtime, overlapping with the use of fire — one cause of the great conflagration. So the day is never forgotten, September 1 was designated "Disaster Prevention Day" in 1960</strong> — The first line of Japan’s earthquake response was decided by statistics a hundred years old.</div>

<h2>The one-line lesson — "fire before shaking"</h2>

<p>90% of the Great Kantō Earthquake’s victims were lost not to collapse but to fire. Lunchtime flames, and fire spread by wind. This fact decides the first line of a property’s earthquake response. <strong>While it shakes, your body’s safety; once it stops, immediately, fire.</strong> For a property with a kitchen, whether everyone knows this one line in their body — that is the quality of the first 30 minutes.</p>

<p>September 1, "Disaster Prevention Day," was established in 1960 from the lessons of the Great Kantō Earthquake and the Ise Bay Typhoon (1959), and has become a day of drills across Japan. Properties are no exception. Writing <strong>a once-a-year whole-team drill day</strong> into the calendar in advance — that alone means the property’s disaster preparedness has begun.</p>

<h2>The first 5 minutes — safety and fire</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Your own safety</strong> — Under a desk, distance from falling objects. Do not run while it shakes. The manual’s first line is always here (if you are injured, no one moves).</li>
<li><strong>Cut the fire</strong> — When the shaking stops, the kitchen’s main gas valve. Let the nightly patrol teach "where the valve is" to the body (<a href="#safety-patrol-night">Safety Patrol: The Basics of Watching When No One Watches</a> links in here).</li>
<li><strong>Call out</strong> — Over the PA, or by voice: "Are you all right? We will now check the building." The words’ content belongs to the next section.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The next 10 minutes — information and gathering</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Information</strong> — By radio or smartphone: the scale of the shaking, and whether there is a tsunami. Properties near the sea, without waiting for tsunami information, execute <strong>"to high ground, on foot, not by car"</strong> first. Walk the evacuation route once, all together, in a drill.</li>
<li><strong>Securing the routes</strong> — Unlock the entrance and emergency exits. Clear the paths of glass shards and fallen objects. Expecting a blackout, check the flashlights’ positions too.</li>
<li><strong>Announce one gathering place</strong> — "Everyone, please gather in the lobby (parking)." The gathering place, and one line of what is known, repeated in a calm voice.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The last 15 minutes — records and contact</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Records</strong> — Time, how the shaking felt, the number of guests, presence of injuries, on one sheet of paper. A few minutes’ record eases everything in the later reports and insurance.</li>
<li><strong>Contact</strong> — The owner (in their absence, the deputy), families, and the neighbors. Neighbors you work with daily are the true allies of a disaster (<a href="#local-business-collaboration">Local Business Collaboration: Basics of Reservation-Driving Partnerships</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Water and food</strong> — Confirm the place and amount of emergency water and stockpiled food. Keep in mind, too, what happens to the refrigerator’s contents in a blackout.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Write the words first — the broadcast script</h2>

<p>What most shapes the first 30 minutes is, in fact, <strong>the words</strong>. Write the in-building announcement script now, in peacetime. "Regarding the earthquake just now, we are checking the building. Please gather in the lobby. All sources of fire have been confirmed. The next announcement comes at ○ o’clock" — these few lines, broadcast in a calm voice, divide panic from trust. The script needs two lines: Japanese and English (the thinking of conveying with pictures and photographs is the same craft as <a href="#idea-onsen-picture-guide">Tell How to Enjoy Onsen with Pictures - Ideas for Onsen Conveyed with Pictures and Photos Without Relying on Words</a>).</p>

<h2>The other side — when the manual endangers the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The thick booklet</strong> — A 50-page manual cannot be opened in an emergency. The first 30 minutes: one sheet, one sheet per station (front desk / kitchen / rooms). The booklet is for afterwards.</li>
<li><strong>A single channel</strong> — Do not rely on one means against blackouts and downed lines. List radio, charged smartphones, and a paper contact tree together.</li>
<li><strong>Blind spots in stockpiles</strong> — How many days of water per head? Infant formula, allergic guests, those on regular medication. Make the emergency shelf visible.</li>
<li><strong>Over-telling and under-telling</strong> — Cutting information too thin grows anxiety; a list of guesses invites panic. Say only the three: "fact + response + time of next update." The principles of telling a group are handled in the group-response article.</li>
<li><strong>Abandoning stations</strong> — With everyone running to the lobby, no one watches the kitchen fire. The one sheet per station is the device for exactly that.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is tonight: from the existing manual (or blank paper if none), <strong>extract only the first 30 minutes onto one sheet</strong>. Line one, "your own safety"; line two, "cut the fire"; line three, "announce the gathering place." Written? Then on the coming September 1, run one 30-minute drill with the whole team. A hundred years from the Great Kantō Earthquake — the first 30 minutes this country’s inns have stacked up; now it is your turn to add to your property’s sheet.</p>

Please design emergency plans to your property’s structure and location (tsunami and landslide risks, etc.), together with fire-department and municipal guidance.</div>
