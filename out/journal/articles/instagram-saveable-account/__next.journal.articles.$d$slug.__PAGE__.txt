11:T1a52,<p>In running a property’s Instagram, what is truly close to bookings is not "likes" but <strong>"saves" (bookmarks)</strong>. A post with 100 likes and 0 saves is "a post that was seen"; a post with 20 likes and 30 saves is "part of a travel plan." This article organizes the account design of "being saved = entering the shortlist." And Instagram’s own explanation backs this way of thinking.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>In its official explanation of ranking (published 2023, as of writing), the photo-sharing app Instagram states that no single algorithm exists, and that Feed, Stories, Explore, and Reels each run on different mechanisms. As the first signal determining the order of Feed posts it names "your own activity" (posts you liked, shared, saved, commented on), and on the Explore screen, a post’s popularity (including likes, comments, shares, and saves) is said to carry more weight than in the Feed</strong> — Saves, as evidence of "worth looking back at," work both on the follower’s screen and on discovery by strangers.</div>

<h2>Why "saves" are the KPI</h2>

<p>Picture the booking path of the guests who come to your property. <strong>Discovered on social in the pre-trip stage → added to the shortlist (a save) → searching and comparing pre-trip → booking.</strong> The "save" is the act of "entering the shortlist." The moment it is saved, your property has become a component of that person’s travel plan — the contest for the booking starts there. Two numbers to watch monthly: saves per post, and the share of reviews containing the words "saw it on Instagram."</p>

<h2>Account design — the 9-grid is "the storefront"</h2>

<p>The nine tiles lined up on the profile screen are not a random stream of posts but <strong>the property’s storefront</strong>. If a first-time visitor cannot tell "this is what kind of property it is" in three seconds, it fails as a storefront. For that, design fixed ratios across five content pillars.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Signature space</strong> (1 in 3 posts) — the symbol of the brand</li>
<li><strong>Guest rooms</strong> (1 in 4) — the shape of the stay</li>
<li><strong>Food</strong> (1 in 4) — the most-photographed content</li>
<li><strong>Surroundings and access</strong> (1 in 4) — practical value; the wellspring of saves</li>
<li><strong>People</strong> (1 in 6) — the warmth of staff and owner</li>
</ul>

<p>That "surroundings and access" is in the list is deliberate. The content of a saved post is not property publicity but <strong>a tool for the trip</strong> — "what time do I need to arrive to see the sunrise here" is information that the less someone knows your property, the more they save. Add, for the moment the saver next opens your profile, highlights (the permanent collections) with "Access," "How to spend your time," and "Rooms" lined up. First impression on the 9-grid; the second visit received by the highlights. That is the design of the storefront.</p>

<h2>The 4 post formats that get saved</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>The "how to use it" type</strong> — "The three best times to see the sunrise here." Practical value is the wellspring of saves.</li>
<li><strong>The "before / after" type</strong> — The process of styling, the process of renovation. "Invisible labor" moves people.</li>
<li><strong>The "one recommendation" type</strong> — A single recommendation of one dish, one view, one amenity. It comes through because everything else is cut.</li>
<li><strong>The "a staff member’s day" type</strong> — The backstage that conveys "I want to work here / I want to come here." A wellspring of trust.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Posting rhythm and the CTA</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>3–4 posts a week</strong> and weekly Stories (the backstage). Continuity over volume — 2 a week for a year beats 5 a week ending in two months.</li>
<li>Write <strong>"Save this for your trip planning"</strong> in the caption. Asking for the save explicitly is the simplest way to raise the rate.</li>
<li>Pin the booking link in the profile. Since posts cannot carry links directly, the profile becomes "the corridor to booking."</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other side — when chasing saves exhausts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Becoming an information-only account</strong> — Pile up information to be saved, and the "air of the property" — the very thing — drains out. Surroundings and access is one pillar, not the whole.</li>
<li><strong>The stamina of updating</strong> — 3–4 a week is an "ideal," not a "norm." If it doesn’t continue, drop to 2 and make it sustainable. Seven tiles that continue beat nine with gaps.</li>
<li><strong>The CTA wearing out</strong> — The same "please save" on every post becomes the same as not writing it. Restate it, with a reason.</li>
<li><strong>A save is not "a booking now"</strong> — A pre-trip save can take months to turn into a booking. Don’t throw discounts at it in impatience; keep the design (profile and highlights) that holds the saved shortlist.</li>
<li><strong>Saves from overseas guests</strong> — For posts you want saved by visitors to Japan, add a line of English to the caption. A translation app is fine. Saves work across borders.</li>
<li><strong>How to read the metric</strong> — Save counts cannot be compared with other properties (a non-public metric). The only counterpart to compare with is "yourself last month."</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is, tonight, to open your own account’s 9-grid. Look at it for three seconds with a first-timer’s eyes — if "what kind of property is this?" does not come through, redesign from the pillar ratios. An account that gets saved is a signboard working 24 hours. Building the signboard begins not with more posts but with designing the storefront.</p>

<li><strong>签名空间</strong>（3 篇帖子中的 1 篇）——品牌象征意义</li>
<li><strong>客房</strong>（4 条帖子中的 1 条）——“留下来”</li>
<li><strong>一顿饭</strong>（4 条帖子中的 1 条）— 被拍摄最多的内容</li>
<li><strong>周边环境/交通</strong>（4 个帖子中的 1 个）——实用价值（保存来源）</li>
<li><strong>人们</strong>（6 个职位中的 1 个）——员工和管理层的热情</li>
</ul>
<p>关键是“整个简介可以看成一个故事”。第一次来的人3秒就能看出这就是旅馆的类型。</p>
<h2>3. 保存4种帖子格式</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>“入门指南/掌握”类型</strong> — “这里看日出的最佳时间有3个。”实用价值=保存来源。</li>
<li><strong>“之前/之后”类型</strong> ——造型的过程，改造的过程。 “看不见的努力”让人感动。</li>
<li><strong>“一次推荐”型</strong> — 推荐一道菜、一种景色、一种设施。您可以删除其他的。</li>
<li><strong>“员工节”类型</strong> — 在幕后传达“我想在这里工作/我想来这里”。信任的来源。</li>
</ol>
<h2>4. 节律后和 CTA</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>每周 3-4 个帖子</strong>+ 每周故事（背面）。延续而不是数量。</li>
<li>在标题中<strong>它说“请保存到您的旅行计划”</strong>。明确的 CTA 可以提高保留率。</li>
<li>如需预订链接，请转到个人资料+生物工具中的链接。</li>
</ul>
