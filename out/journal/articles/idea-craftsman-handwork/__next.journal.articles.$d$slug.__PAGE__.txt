9:T246f,<p>宿の建物を案内するとき、多くの宿は「築何年」を言います。ですが、建物の本当の説明文は、年数ではなく<strong>職人の判断の跡</strong>にあります。柱の面取り、漆喰の塗り跡、建具の組み、畳の目——それらは、誰かがその場所で何かを判断した記録です。この記事は、宿の建物や道具に残る手仕事を「読んで、見せる」ための、見方の配り方を扱います。</p>

<h2>1. 建物より先に、技が遺産になります</h2>
<p>2020年12月、ユネスコの無形文化遺産の代表一覧表に、「伝統建築工匠の技——木造建造物を受け継ぐための伝統技術」が登録されました。檜皮葺（ひわだぶき）など、国の選定保存技術に選ばれた17件の伝統建築技術で構成される登録です。木・草・土といった自然素材を生かす知恵、保存修理を見据えた材料の採取と再利用、木工・屋根葺き・左官・装飾・畳などの高度な技——そして登録の評価には、熟練の職人が弟子を育ててきた系譜と、かやぶき屋根の補修に地元住民も参画してきたことが含まれていました（日本経済新聞報道）。</p>
<p>同じ2020年、地球の反対側でも似た登録がありました。中世以来、大聖堂の修復を担ってきた欧州の「バウヒュッテ（大聖堂工房）」の技術と慣習が、独・墺・仏・ノルウェー・スイスの共同提案で、ユネスコの「よき保護実践の登録簿」に記載されたのです。国境を越えた職人のネットワークが、口承と文書で知識を伝え、大聖堂を何百年も修理し続けてきた——その仕組みそのものが遺産と認められました。</p>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>2020年、日欧で「技の継承」が相次いで遺産になった</strong> — 日本の代表一覧表登録と欧州の大聖堂工房の登録簿記載。どちらも登録の対象は建物ではなく、建物を直し続ける<strong>技術と人と組織</strong>である。大聖堂は石の記録だが、それを保っているのは工房の記録だ。宿の建物も同じで——建物の物語とは、職人の系譜の物語である。</div>

<h2>2. 職人の読み方——『木のいのち木のこころ』</h2>
<p>手仕事を読むための最良の教科書のひとつが、聞き書き『木のいのち木のこころ』です。法隆寺の宮大工の最後の棟梁・西岡常一と、その唯一の内弟子・小川三夫、そして弟子たちの三代にわたる語りを、塩野米松が書き取った本です。西岡はここで、木にはふたつの命があると言いました——ひとつは樹齢、もうひとつは<strong>材としての耐用年数</strong>。改修で瓦を外すと、重荷から解放された垂木が跳ね返り、鉋（かんな）を当てれば香りが立ちます。材になっても木は生き続けている、という眼です。西岡は寸法ではなく木のクセで組んだとも言われています。自然石の上に立つ柱の底は、方向がまちまちで、それぞれの違った「遊び」が地震の揺れを吸収する——法隆寺1300年の歴史が証明する知恵です。</p>
<p>この眼を、宿の建物に借りてくると、見どころのリストが一気に増えます。</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>面取り</strong> — 角を落とすのは美観だけでなく、割れと傷を防ぐ判断。柱や鴨居の角に手を当ててみましょう。</li>
<li><strong>墨字（すみじ）</strong> — 部材に書かれた組立の記号。大工の「取扱説明書」が建物に残っています（詳しくは<a href="#idea-scrap-wood-signboard">廃材の看板の記事</a>で扱いました）。</li>
<li><strong>漆喰の塗り跡</strong> — 左官の手の跡。塗り直しの層が重なっている壁は、建物の診療録といえます（土壁の話は<a href="#idea-touch-soil-stay">土に触れる滞在</a>で）。</li>
<li><strong>建具の組み</strong> — 釘を使わず木と木で組み上げる建具。開け閉めの滑らかさは、組みの精度そのものです。</li>
<li><strong>畳の目</strong> — 畳表の目の詰まり方と縁の柄。部屋ごとに違う理由を尋ねてみると、畳屋さんの判断が聞こえてきます。</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. 見せる——「この建物の手仕事」カード</h2>
<p>読み方が決まったら、次は配るだけです。客室や廊下に小さなカードを1枚——「この部屋の手仕事」——を置きます。書くのは3行でいいのです。<strong>何が（面取り）／誰の技法か（大工・左官・建具士・畳職人）／いつ頃の判断か</strong>。建てた職人の名前が分かる建物は多くありませんが、直した職人の名前は、まだ聞けることがあります。修理に出会ったら、職人の名前と仕事の内容を書き留めておきましょう——宿の建物の「修理史」は、こうして1行ずつ貯まります。</p>
<p>道具も、手仕事を見せる素材です。鉋、鑿（のみ）、墨壺、指金——使われなくなった大工道具を廊下に並べるときは、名札をつけましょう。「鉋（かんな）— 木を削る。刃の調整が職人の腕」という1行があるだけで、道具は飾りから資料になります。地域に現役の職人がいる宿なら、道具は借り物でもいいでしょう。職人の仕事を宿で紹介することは、地域の手仕事の産地への小さな送客にもなります。</p>

<h2>4. 触れる——木工体験の現実的な作り方</h2>
<p>手仕事は見るだけでは完結しません。削る、組む、磨く——身体で覚える面があるからです。現実的なのは、端材を使う小さな木工といえます。地域の大工さんや工務店から出る端材をいただき、鉋で削ってコースターを作ったり、麻紐で組んで小さな棚を作ったりしましょう。職人に来てもらえるなら、鉋の使い方を10分だけ教わる体験は、子どもの記憶に長く残ります。工作の完成度より、<strong>本物の道具と本物の手</strong>に触れたことが体験の核となるのです。</p>
<p>ただし、道具を扱う体験には線が要るのです。刃物の数は管理し、使い終わったら戻す習慣を最初に教え、幼い子どもには補助がつきます。職人に依頼するときは「体験の先生」としての対価をきちんと設定します——教えることは、職人の仕事のうちです。</p>

<h2>5. 見学——修理の日は、建物が開く日</h2>
<p>宿の建物が修理に入るとき——屋根の葺き替え、壁の塗り替え、建具の直し——は、普段は見えない手仕事が一堂に公開される日です。修理の見学を「工事中につき立入禁止」ではなく、安全な範囲を見せる日にできないでしょうか。職人との段取りを事前に相談し、見学の時間帯と場所を決めて、ゲストに「今日はうちの建物の修理の日です」と伝えます。完成した建物より、建物が直されている現場の方が、遥かに珍しいものです。</p>
<div class="note"><span class="tag">InnBuddy VIEW</span>手仕事を見せる営みで忘れがちなのは、職人の仕事は「見せるため」ではなく<strong>本番</strong>だということです。見学者の安全確保、職人の段取りを邪魔しない距離、撮影の可否——その気配りができた宿だけが、修理の日を体験にできます。そしてもうひとつ。後継者の減少は、多くの伝統技術に共通した現実です。宿が職人の名前を書き、仕事を紹介することは、需要の見える化という、地味ですが実のある応援です。</div>

11:T24a3,<p>When guiding guests through an inn’s building, most inns state its age. But the building’s true description lies not in years but in <strong>the traces of craftsmen’s judgments</strong>. The chamfering of a pillar, the trowel marks of the plaster, the joinery of the fittings, the weave of the tatami — these are records of someone deciding something, in that place. This article treats how to distribute the seeing — to read, and to show, the handwork that remains in the inn’s building and tools.</p>

<h2>1. Skill becomes heritage before buildings do</h2>

<p>In December 2020, "Traditional skills, techniques and knowledge for the conservation and transmission of timber architecture in Japan" was inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. The inscription is composed of seventeen traditional building techniques selected as the nation’s Selected Conservation Techniques, including hiwada-buki cypress-bark roofing. The wisdom that draws on natural materials — wood, grass, earth; the harvesting and reuse of materials with repair in view; the advanced skills of woodworking, roofing, plastering, decoration, and tatami — and among the grounds for the inscription were the lineage of master craftsmen raising apprentices, and the participation of local residents in re-thatching roofs (Nikkei reporting).</p>

<p>In the same year 2020, on the opposite side of the globe, a similar inscription took place. The craft and customs of Europe’s Bauhütten — the cathedral workshops that have carried the restoration of cathedrals since the Middle Ages — were entered in UNESCO’s Register of Good Safeguarding Practices, under a joint nomination of Germany, Austria, France, Norway, and Switzerland. A network of craftsmen crossing borders, transmitting knowledge by word of mouth and in documents, repairing cathedrals for centuries — that very mechanism was recognized as heritage.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>In 2020, in Japan and Europe, "the passing on of skill" became heritage, one after the other</strong> — Japan’s inscription on the Representative List, and Europe’s cathedral workshops entered on the Register. In both, what was inscribed is not the building but the <strong>skill, the people, and the organization</strong> that keep repairing it. A cathedral is a record in stone, but what keeps it standing is the workshop’s record. The same is true of an inn’s building — the story of a building is the story of a lineage of craftsmen.</div>

<h2>2. How to read the craftsman — "The Genius of Japanese Carpentry"</h2>

<p>One of the best textbooks for reading handwork is the oral record "Ki no Inochi Ki no Kokoro" (The Life and Heart of Wood). It is the book in which Shiina Osamu transcribed the tellings of three generations: Nishioka Tsunekazu, the last master carpenter of the Hōryūji temple works; his sole apprentice, Ogawa Mitsuo; and the apprentices after them. Nishioka said here that wood has two lives — one is its age as a tree, the other its <strong>service life as timber</strong>. Remove the roof tiles in a repair, and the rafters, freed of their load, spring back; set a plane to them and the scent rises. Timber, too, keeps living — that was his eye. Nishioka is also said to have joined wood by its grain, not by its dimensions. The bases of the pillars standing on natural stone face various directions, each with a different "play" that absorbs the shaking of earthquakes — wisdom proven by Hōryūji’s thirteen hundred years.</p>

<p>Borrow this eye for the inn’s building, and the list of things to see grows at a stroke.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Chamfering</strong> — Dropping the corner of an edge is a judgment that prevents splitting and injury, not just looks. Lay a hand on the corner of a pillar or a lintel.</li>
<li><strong>Sumiji ink marks</strong> — The assembly symbols written on members. The carpenter’s "instruction manual" remains in the building (treated in <a href="#idea-scrap-wood-signboard">Signboard Made from Scrap Wood - Ideas for Inn Signboard Made from Reclaimed Wood and Old Tools</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Trowel marks in the plaster</strong> — The trace of the plasterer’s hand. A wall where layers of re-coating overlap is the building’s clinical record (for earthen walls, see <a href="#idea-touch-soil-stay">Stay Touching Soil - Material Ideas for Inn from Earthen Wall, Dirt Floor, Soil of Field</a>).</li>
<li><strong>The joinery of fittings</strong> — Doors assembled of wood on wood, without nails. The smoothness of the opening and closing is the precision of the joinery itself.</li>
<li><strong>The tatami weave</strong> — The tightness of the mat’s weave and the pattern of the binding. Ask why it differs room by room, and the tatami-maker’s judgment comes into earshot.</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. Showing — the "handwork of this building" card</h2>

<p>Once the way of reading is set, all that remains is to distribute it. In the rooms and corridors, place one small card — "the handwork of this room." Three lines are enough: <strong>what it is (the chamfer) / whose technique (carpenter, plasterer, joiner, tatami-maker) / the period of the judgment</strong>. Few buildings know the name of the carpenter who built them, but the name of the one who repaired them can still, at times, be heard. Whenever repair comes, write down the craftsman’s name and the content of the work — the building’s "repair history" accumulates, one line at a time.</p>

<p>Tools, too, are material for showing handwork. Planes, chisels, the ink pot, the try square — when you line up retired carpenter’s tools in a corridor, give each a name tag. One line — "Kanna, the plane: it shaves wood; the tuning of the blade is the craftsman’s wrist" — turns the tool from ornament into document. Where local craftsmen are active, the tools may even be borrowed. Introducing a craftsman’s work at the inn is also a small sending of guests to the home of the region’s handwork.</p>

<h2>4. Touching — the realistic making of a woodworking experience</h2>

<p>Handwork does not complete in watching alone. Shaving, joining, polishing — there is a side the body learns. What is realistic is small woodworking with offcuts: receive the offcuts that come from local carpenters and builders, shave them with a plane into coasters, or tie them with hemp cord into a small shelf. If a craftsman can come, ten minutes of learning to use a plane stays long in a child’s memory. Less than the polish of the finished piece, <strong>touching real tools and real hands</strong> is the core of the experience.</p>

<p>But an experience that handles tools needs lines. Count the blades, teach the habit of returning each tool when done, and attach support for small children. When asking a craftsman, set a proper fee for the role of "teacher of the experience" — teaching is part of the craftsman’s work.</p>

<h2>5. Watching — repair day is the day the building opens</h2>

<p>When the inn’s building enters repair — re-roofing, re-plastering, fixing fittings — it is the day the normally invisible handwork is displayed all at once. Instead of "Keep out: work in progress," can the safe range not be shown? Arrange the schedule with the craftsmen in advance, fix the viewing hours and place, and tell the guests, "Today is the day our building is repaired." A building being repaired is far rarer than a finished building.</p>

<div class="note"><span class="tag">InnBuddy VIEW</span>What is easily forgotten in showing handwork is that the craftsman’s work is not "for showing" — it is <strong>the real performance</strong>. Securing the viewers’ safety, keeping a distance that does not disturb the craftsman’s rhythm, asking about photography — only the inn that manages this care can turn repair day into experience. And one more thing: the decline of successors is a reality shared by many traditional skills. For an inn to write the craftsman’s name and introduce the work is a plain but substantial support — making the demand visible.</div>

In implementing, design under each inn’s own judgment, including safety, laws and regulations, and consideration for the neighborhood. When handling buildings, old timber, and tools, confirm structural and work safety with specialists.</div>
