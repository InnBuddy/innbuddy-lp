11:T1b3c,<p>"For now, let's list on every OTA we can." — Born of anxiety about occupancy, plenty of channel operations begin with that one line. A year later, that inn's channels are "halfhearted everywhere." The photos are old on every site, the replies have stopped somewhere, and no channel has become the inn's main force. This article's claim is one thing from the start: OTAs are not for "listing everywhere" but for <strong>"digging deep into the two or three that work"</strong> — a story of deciding not the number you list on, but the number you dig into.</p>

<h2>What happened in Europe in 2024</h2>

<p>On May 13, 2024, the European Commission designated Booking.com — one of the world's largest booking sites — a "gatekeeper" under the Digital Markets Act (DMA): a rulebook that presumes giant platforms hold dominant positions as the market's thoroughfares and disciplines their conduct. Compliance obligations began November 14 that year, and among them was a change that matters to inns: <strong>the prohibition of parity clauses</strong> (clauses that prevent properties from offering better prices or conditions on other channels than on the site itself).</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>On May 13, 2024, the European Commission designated Booking.com a gatekeeper under the EU Digital Markets Act (DMA). Compliance obligations began November 14, 2024, and parity clauses were prohibited — properties using the site became free to offer different (better) prices and conditions on their own sites and other channels, and blocking this through commission raises or delisting was also prohibited. Violations can draw fines of up to 10% of worldwide revenue (European Commission announcement)</strong> — An event in which the booking site was officially recognized as the market's "gatekeeper."</div>

<p>What is interesting in this story is less the content of the rules than their placement. When the regulator itself says "gatekeeper," the world's inns' acquisition has concentrated on a mere handful of booking sites — that became the world's common sense of 2024. Note, though: this is European regulation, not a story that immediately changes the contract terms between Japanese inns and each company. Even so, what this structure teaches remains, as-is, for Japanese inns. <strong>The OTA is a powerful thoroughfare — and at the same time, a tenancy.</strong> The reviews, the ratings, the display ranking stacked on top of a channel are assets belonging to that channel, not things the inn can carry home. Concentration that entrusts everything to one channel makes acquisition fast — and quietly pools risk.</p>

<h2>Criterion 1 — the match of segments</h2>

<p>Each OTA has a different strong segment. As a general tendency: Booking.com is strong for inbound independent travelers; Rakuten Travel for domestic and family layers; Agoda for bookings from Asia-Pacific. First, <strong>"who comes to your inn"</strong> narrows the candidates. Put the other way: an OTA where you cannot imagine your desired guest searching for inns drops in priority.</p>

<h2>Criteria 2–4 — cost, operation, data</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Commissions and payment terms</strong> — The fee structure (flat or variable), the payout cycle, the treatment of cancellations and no-shows. Rates change with contract and season, so compare with the numbers your inn actually contracts.</li>
<li><strong>Operational load</strong> — Ease of updating the listing (prices, availability, photos), support quality and language, report readability. Each channel added does not neatly double this load. It grows in the shape of incidents.</li>
<li><strong>Data and value for direct</strong> — Lead data from inquiries and unfinished bookings, the quality of reports. Ultimately: is it a channel that can guide guests toward direct bookings?</li>
</ul>

<h2>How to decide — score it, then "one primary + one or two secondary"</h2>

<p>Score the four criteria from 1 to 5. The highest-scoring channel becomes the "primary," and you concentrate operating resources on it. The next one or two become "secondary," held at a level of "no loss in existing." <strong>Running every channel at the same level is the worst cost-performance operation there is.</strong> Rather than running four channels at 70 points, polishing one to 95 and holding two at 70 changes how bookings appear.</p>

<h2>The other side — behind the digging</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>"Listing everywhere" early has its meaning</strong> — Narrowing down at the stage where you do not know where demand lies is guesswork. The right order: set a period (say three months), collect the data from what you can list, then narrow.</li>
<li><strong>Abandoning the secondary backfires</strong> — Even a channel you do not dig into cannot be left exposed once you list on it. Old photos and unanswered reviews read, to the guest who sees that channel, as "an inn whose being open is uncertain."</li>
<li><strong>The risk of depending on the primary</strong> — A commission revision, rule change, or outage at the primary channel is, directly, an event in your inn's revenue. The one-primary + one-or-two-secondary composition is an acquisition strategy and, at once, a design for spreading risk.</li>
<li><strong>Multiple management of inventory</strong> — Manually updating availability across channels is the entrance to the worst failure: double-selling. Add channels only after building a way to manage inventory in one place.</li>
<li><strong>The cost of switching primaries</strong> — The ratings and reviews stacked on a channel do not move with you. Replacing the primary is a decision to "leave the asset of trust behind," so do not make it a casual item of periodic review.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step requires no new contract. Tonight, count the past three months of the booking ledger by channel. One primary and one or two secondaries — and is everything else at its proper level? The numbers are already in your hands. Digging in begins not with "listing" but with "deciding."</p>

<li>支持质量/语言</li>
<li>轻松获取报告</li>
</ul>
<h2>标准 4：数据和直接连接的价值</h2>
<ul>
<li>线索数据的利用（查询/不完整的预订）</li>
<li>点击率/排名报告的质量</li>
</ul>
<h2>判定方式：“主1+次1-2”评分</h2>
<p>4个标准得分1-5分，<strong>最高分是“主赛”，亚军是“副赛1-2”</strong>。主要集中运营资源，将次要资源保持在现有无害的水平。将所有渠道都放在同一层级上进行运营是成本效益最低的运营方式。</p>

<h2>为什么现在值得关注？</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>客人期待的变化：</strong>当谈到“优先考虑哪个OTA？ - 渠道选择标准”时，客人的期望与五年前相比发生了变化。过去“有了更好”的服务，如今已变成“没有就难以被选择”。适应这些变化，是住宿设施持续经营的前提。</li>
<li><strong>与地区关系：</strong>在网上吸引顾客的努力增加了整个地区的价值，而不仅仅是旅馆本身的价值。通过与当地生产者和周边商户合作，旅馆的行动会成为“当地故事”。社区共同参与，才更容易长期持续。</li>
<li><strong>不增加员工负担：</strong>新举措不应增加员工负担。通过建立机制，用一张表或每次15分钟的学习来运转，就能在不增加员工负担的情况下持续改进。</li>
</ul>

<h2>避免失败的3个要点</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>从小处开始——先做好一件事：</strong>一开始就想全部改变，往往难以持续。先从一件事开始。可以从一间客房、一个货架或一句话开始；即使效果不理想，影响也小，更容易调整。</li>
<li><strong>记录——用一张表看清楚：</strong>把行动记录在一张表里，效果就能被看见。你会知道哪些做法有效、哪些需要调整，记录也会成为下一轮改进的依据。</li>
<li><strong>持续——每周一次，每次15分钟：</strong>新行动需要持续。每周留出15分钟，逐渐形成习惯，最后才会成为团队文化。</li>
</ol>
