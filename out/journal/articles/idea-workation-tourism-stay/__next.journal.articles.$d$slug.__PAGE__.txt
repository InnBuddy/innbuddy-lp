11:T1b70,<p>“Workation” combines “work” and “vacation,” and is said to have originated in the United States in the 2000s. In Japan, the term spread rapidly after the Chief Cabinet Secretary used it in summer 2020. Yet many workations slide into being nothing more than working in a different place. Good Wi-Fi, a desk, and then the usual work: the vacation disappears. This article considers how an inn can avoid that trap.</p><h2>A country opening its doors for work</h2><p>In summer 2020, the Caribbean nation of Barbados began accepting applications for the “12-Month Barbados Welcome Stamp.” This was among the world’s earliest remote-work residence visas, allowing people employed by companies outside the island to live and work there for up to 12 months. It made the blend of work and vacation into an offering at a national scale.</p><div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>In summer 2020, Barbados began accepting applications for the “12-Month Barbados Welcome Stamp”</strong> — Among the world’s earliest remote-work residence visas, it permits people working for employers outside the island to stay for up to 12 months. It represents a shift toward opening a country as a place to work.</div><h2>Kamiyama: offices arrived, then everyday life followed</h2><p>One Japanese town had already been following its own path: Kamiyama in Tokushima Prefecture. Its starting point was not work. The nonprofit Green Valley began inviting artists through an artist-in-residence program in 1999, then moved in 2008 toward “work-in-residence,” welcoming people who relocated while bringing their work with them. From around 2010, satellite offices of IT companies in renovated traditional houses began to increase. Six companies arrived in two years; by 2018, there were 16. Fiber-optic connections across Tokushima Prefecture and the quiet of a mountain valley drew companies and people to a place where the usual assumptions about central-city locations and proximity to train stations did not apply.</p><p>What matters is what followed. After the offices came cafés, pizza restaurants, and accommodation. When working people settle, businesses supporting everyday life follow. Put that side at the center of your design.</p><ul><li><strong>One seat is enough</strong> — Identify one desk by a window, with power and lighting, as a place to work.
