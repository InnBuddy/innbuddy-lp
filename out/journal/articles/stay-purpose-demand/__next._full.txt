16:T1e23,<p>同じ宿に、同じ部屋に泊まっても、求められているものは別物です。出張の客が求めるのは「朝6時に出られて、駅まで5分、Wi-Fiが速い」こと。家族連れが求めるのは「子どもが走り回れる庭と、写真に残る朝食」。祭りに来た客が求めるのは「会場まで歩いて10分、遅くまでチェックインできる柔軟さ」。1つのListing、1つの写真、1つの説明文で、これら全部を満たそうとすると——誰にも刺さらないページが出来上がります。この記事は、需要を「目的」という刃で切り分け、それぞれに入口を設計する話です。</p><div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>2024年の全国の延べ宿泊者数は6億5,906万人泊(過去最高)で、うち外国人は1億6,446万人泊(比率25.2%)。同年の訪日客で「観光・レジャー」目的の割合は84.1%(2019年は77.8%)だった(観光庁の宿泊旅行統計調査・インバウンド消費動向調査)</strong> — 観光・レジャーが主役の市場で、ビジネス客は貴重な少数派である。</div><p>この数字の読み方は2つあります。一つは、市場の主役がレジャーであること。もう一つは、<strong>少数派の需要の価値</strong>です。ビジネス客は少ない——しかし、レジャー需要が谷に沈む平日に、直前予約で埋まっていくのがビジネス需要です。少数派だからこそ、宿の平準化に効く。では、3つの目的を順に見ます。</p><h2>ビジネス — 「時間と効率」の需要</h2><ul><li><strong>重視</strong> — 立地(駅・会場・取引先への近さ)、Wi-Fiの速度と安定、ランドリー、早朝チェックアウトと深夜チェックインの柔軟さ、静かに仕事ができるデスクと照明。</li><li><strong>行動</strong> — 直前予約が多く、1泊が基本。ただし常連になると通年で安定する(出張は季節の波が少ない)。領収書・請求書対応を重視。リピート時は「同じ部屋・同じ対応」を求める。</li><li><strong>設計</strong> — 「ビジネスプラン」を分ける。写真はデスク、コンセントの位置、駅からの道順。説明は「駅から徒歩4分、6時チェックアウト可、Wi-Fi実測(数字)」と、具体的に。</li></ul><h2>レジャー — 「体験と記憶」の需要</h2><ul><li><strong>重視</strong> — 体験(何ができるか)、眺望、食事、写真映え、子ども・ペット対応、連泊できる広さと収納。</li><li><strong>行動</strong> — 週末と季節に山ができ、2〜3ヶ月前から計画し、レビューと写真で比較する。連泊で満足度が上がる。</li><li><strong>設計</strong> — 「体験パッケージ」を作る。写真は朝食、庭、夕景、地域の体験の風景。説明は「森のピクニック付き2連泊」など、何ができるかの言葉で。</li></ul><h2>行事・イベント — 「近さと柔軟さ」の需要</h2><ul><li><strong>重視</strong> — 会場への近さ、イベントに合わせた早朝・深夜対応、遅めチェックアウト、グループ対応、大荷物・衣装の収納。</li><li><strong>行動</strong> — 短期で需要が山になる。ただし告知から半年〜1年前に計画が動く(マラソンや大規模な祭りは前年から)。キャンセルポリシーの柔軟性が選ばれる理由になる。</li><li><strong>設計</strong> — 「行事カレンダー」を作る。地域の祭り、マラソン、受験、コンサートを12ヶ月分洗い出し、3ヶ月前に行事プランを公開。写真は会場までの距離地図と、グループで泊まれる部屋。</li></ul><h2>入口を分ける — プラン名・写真の順番・キーワード</h2><p>3目的への対応は、部屋を増やすことでも、全員に対応することでもありません。<strong>入口を分ける</strong>ことです。予約サイトでも自社サイトでも、ゲストが最初に読むのはプラン名と最初の1枚。ビジネス客にはデスクの写真から、レジャー客には朝食の写真から始まる同じ宿。説明文の冒頭一文も、目的ごとに変える(「6時チェックアウト可」か「森の朝で目覚める」か)。キーワード(出張・テレワーク・家族・体験・祭り)も、目的の言葉で埋まります。宿は1つでも、扉は3つで良い。</p><h2>予約記録に「目的」の列を</h2><p>最後に、設計の土台になる記録の話。予約時に「今回のご旅行は、どのようなご予定ですか」と一言聞く(任意で、選択肢は軽く)。ビジネス/レジャー/行事の月別比率が分かるだけで、来年の価格設計とプラン更新の根拠が変わります。なお、地域によって目的の構成は全国平均と違います——ビジネス需要が厚い地域もあれば、レジャー一色の地域もある。全国の数字は物差しの一部として、自宿の記録を本命に。</p><h2>目的別設計の反対側</h2><ul><li><strong>入口の乱立</strong> 1b:T1dae,<p>The same inn, the same room — but what is being asked for is a different thing. The business traveler asks for "out by six in the morning, five minutes to the station, fast Wi-Fi." The family asks for "a garden children can run around in, and a breakfast worth photographing." The festival-goer asks for "ten minutes on foot to the venue, and flexibility to check in late." Try to satisfy all of these with one listing, one photo, one description, and what takes shape is a page that pierces no one. This article slices demand with the blade of "purpose," and designs an entrance for each.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Nationwide guest-nights in 2024 totaled 659.06 million (an all-time high), of which foreign guests accounted for 164.46 million (25.2%). Among that year's visitors to Japan, the share traveling for "tourism and leisure" was 84.1% (77.8% in 2019) (Japan Tourism Agency's Lodging Statistics Survey and survey of foreign-visitor consumption trends)</strong> — In a market led by tourism and leisure, business travelers are a valuable minority.</div>

<p>There are two readings of these numbers. One: the market's lead belongs to leisure. The other: <strong>the value of minority demand</strong>. Business travelers are few — but it is business demand that fills the weekdays, with last-minute bookings, exactly when leisure demand sinks into the valley. Precisely because it is a minority, it works to level the inn. Now, the three purposes in order.</p>

<h2>Business — the demand of "time and efficiency"</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>What they value</strong> — Location (closeness to the station, the venue, the client), Wi-Fi speed and stability, laundry, flexibility for early checkouts and late-night check-ins, a desk and lighting for quiet work.</li>
<li><strong>How they behave</strong> — Many last-minute bookings, one night as the base. But once regulars, they steady the year round (business travel has few seasonal waves). They value receipts and invoicing. On repeats, they ask for "the same room, the same treatment."</li>
<li><strong>Design</strong> — Split off a "business plan." Photos: the desk, the outlet positions, the route from the station. Description: "a 4-minute walk from the station, checkout at 6 a.m. available, Wi-Fi measured at (the number)" — concretely.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Leisure — the demand of "experience and memory"</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>What they value</strong> — Experience (what can be done), the view, the food, photogenic-ness, support for children and pets, the space and storage for consecutive nights.</li>
<li><strong>How they behave</strong> — Peaks form on weekends and in season; planning starts two to three months out; they compare by reviews and photographs. Satisfaction rises with consecutive stays.</li>
<li><strong>Design</strong> — Build "experience packages." Photos: breakfast, the garden, the evening scene, local experiences. Description: in words of what can be done — "two nights with a forest picnic," and so on.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Events — the demand of "closeness and flexibility"</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>What they value</strong> — Closeness to the venue, early-morning and late-night arrangements matched to the event, late checkout, group handling, storage for big luggage and costumes.</li>
<li><strong>How they behave</strong> — Demand spikes short and tall. But planning starts half a year to a year after the announcement (marathons and major festivals move from the year before). The flexibility of the cancellation policy becomes a reason to be chosen.</li>
<li><strong>Design</strong> — Build an "event calendar." List twelve months of local festivals, marathons, exam seasons, concerts, and publish the event plan three months ahead. Photos: a distance map to the venue, and rooms where a group can stay.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Separate the entrances — plan names, photo order, keywords</h2>

<p>Answering the three purposes is neither adding rooms nor accommodating everyone. It is <strong>separating the entrances</strong>. On booking sites and your own site, what guests read first is the plan name and the first photo. The same inn, beginning with the desk photo for business guests and the breakfast photo for leisure guests. The opening sentence of the description changes by purpose too ("checkout at 6 a.m. available," or "wake to a morning in the forest"). The keywords (business trip, remote work, family, experience, festival) also fill up with the words of each purpose. The inn can be one; three doors are fine.</p>

<h2>Add a "purpose" column to the booking records</h2>

<p>Finally, the records that ground the design. At booking, ask one line — "What are your plans for this trip?" (optional, the choices kept light). Just knowing the monthly ratios of business/leisure/events changes the basis of next year's pricing design and plan updates. Note that the purpose mix differs from the national average by region — some regions run thick with business demand, others are leisure through and through. Take the national numbers as one part of the ruler, and make your own records the favorite.</p>

<h2>The other side of purpose-based design</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>A proliferation of entrances</strong> — Too many plans raise the costs of management and commissions, and chooser's paralysis. Whether the three purposes truly exist at your inn — first confirm in the booking records (the past three months).</li>
<li><strong>The unit price of business demand</strong> — It fills the weekdays, but the rate stays modest, and the paperwork of receipts and early rising grows. Build the habit of viewing it in gross profit.</li>
<li><strong>Event cancellations</strong> — Event demand vanishes with the event's own cancellation or postponement (in recent years, the whole world learned this). A flexible cancellation policy is revenue insurance.</li>
<li><strong>The appeal of "everything on board"</strong> — A listing claiming to be good for every purpose ranks third or lower in every search. The reason to be chosen is born on the far side of narrowing down.</li>
<li><strong>How to ask the purpose</strong> — Asking too much is prying. "What are your plans?" stays light; never press those who decline. The record goes in an optional column.</li>
<li><strong>Awareness of tilt</strong> — At an inn of mostly business guests, breakfast makes efficiency, not leisure, its justice. Which face the inn shows is decided by the purpose mix.</li>
</ul>

<p>Start small: next week, sort the bookings into three colors by purpose (colored pens on the past booking sheet are fine). The tilt of the colors will tell you where to build your inn's entrances.</p>
