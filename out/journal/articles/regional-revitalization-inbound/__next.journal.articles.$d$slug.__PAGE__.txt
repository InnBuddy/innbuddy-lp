11:T1c30,<p>"Regional revitalization is the government's job; inns have a business to run" — this sorting keeps the most important participant away from many regions. Look at it from the other side. No other profession meets the region's producers, guides visitors through its sights, and passes among its residents every day. <strong>The inn is the region's only daily route connecting it with travelers.</strong> This article organizes, in form and in use, how that inn takes part in regional revitalization × inbound.</p>

<h2>The region already has a "management organization" — the fact of the DMO</h2>

<p>When thinking about regional revitalization × inbound, the first fact to know is this: in nearly every Japanese destination, an organization serving as the command tower of destination management already exists. It is the registration system of the "Japan DMO," begun in November 2015.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>In November 2015, the Japan Tourism Agency opened the registration system for "Japan DMOs" (destination management organizations). As the command tower of a destination's management, a DMO works with local residents, transport operators, lodging facilities, restaurants, and other stakeholders to formulate and coordinate strategy. Registrations come in wide-area, prefectural, and local categories; as of April 2026, 326 are registered nationwide (10 wide-area, 38 prefectural, 280 local)</strong> — The entrance by which a property joins "the management of its region's tourism" is, in most cases, here.</div>

<p>What these numbers mean is a change of era: from "waiting for someone to fix the region" to <strong>"an organization running the region's tourism is already moving nearby."</strong> DMOs register in the wide-area, prefectural, and local categories, and local DMOs are often at the municipal level. In other words, if the inn decides to join, that meeting is already in session — at the town hall next door, or the tourist association by the station.</p>

<h2>The inn’s three roles — gateway, hub, ambassador</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The gateway</strong> — For the guest, the inn is the first window into the region. Place the morning market's map; tell the producers' names. What the inn at the gateway hands over changes the time and the money spent in the region.</li>
<li><strong>The hub</strong> — A node joining producers, sights, and residents. With the inn in between, the fisher's morning market connects with the traveler's morning. The circuits of a regional economy turn on connections like these, outside the sights themselves.</li>
<li><strong>The ambassador</strong> — One line from the staff conveys the region's appeal better than any brochure. "This rice is from the farm next door" turns a guest into a fan of the region.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The three forms of participation — what to join</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Working with producers — products and experiences</strong> — Put the local farmers', fishers', and brewers' goods on the breakfast table and in the shop. The details of designing them as experience programs are left to "Regional Experience Programs." What the inn carries here is the place to sell and the words to introduce.</li>
<li><strong>Working with the sights — guiding and transfers</strong> — Connect the region's mountains, sea, hot springs, and historic buildings with maps and transfers. There is "a way to walk this land" that only the inn can draw.</li>
<li><strong>Working with residents — events and exchange</strong> — Guiding guests to festivals, watching the mochi-pounding, joining the neighborhood's gatherings. Touching daily life can become the single biggest reason for a repeat visit.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Three points of use — connecting to inbound</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Shape it as an experience</strong> — Turn the work with producers into experience programs that become reasons for consecutive nights (see ID106). The fruits of collaboration become the inn's revenue only when converted into "a reason to stay."</li>
<li><strong>Lower the language barrier</strong> — Put the region's guide on one sheet, multilingual. Not a perfect translation — with names, times, places, and pictures in place, it gets through (see the multilingual signage article).</li>
<li><strong>Hand it over as a story</strong> — "This fish was caught by the fisher this morning." One line of fact turns the food and the scenery into the region's story. Only a story keeps being told after the flight home.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other side — when participation exhausts the inn</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Meetings-only participation</strong> — The pattern of listing your name among the officers and the meetings while nothing changes on the ground. The time an inn can pay the region is limited. If you join, choose the committee that connects directly to your inn's own work.</li>
<li><strong>Demands for goodwill discounts</strong> — "It's for the region" — the moment when free rooms or discounts are asked of the inn always comes. "Contribution to the region exists only where the inn survives" — prepare this one line in advance.</li>
<li><strong>Backlash against tourism</strong> — Not every resident welcomes tourists. The inn stands in the position of protecting both the travelers' enjoyment and the residents' daily life.</li>
<li><strong>The subsidy-built sandcastle</strong> — Exchange programs that begin on subsidies can vanish when the term ends. What you want to continue, place — however small — inside the ordinary operations of the inn and the region.</li>
<li><strong>Inbound is not the only answer</strong> — For some regions, domestic repeaters or new residents work better than inbound. Choose the answer that fits the region, on both the numbers and the lived sense.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is small and concrete. Tell the town hall or the tourist association, in one line, "I would like to sit in on the region's tourism meetings." And in the lobby, place one sheet gathering the region's producers, sights, and events. Participation does not begin with lending your name to a large project; it begins with <strong>keeping your inn's entrance open as the region's entrance</strong>.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. DMO registration counts and the like are published values as of writing. Regional circumstances differ greatly; for each area's initiatives, check information from those involved.</div>

<li><strong>旅馆是该地区的“入口”：</strong>入境旅客将旅馆作为体验该地区的门户。当旅馆了解当地生产商和旅游目的地时，客人就可以“体验该地区”。通过使旅馆成为该地区的门户，整个地区的旅游业将得到振兴。</li>
<li><strong>该旅馆是一个区域“中心”：</strong>旅馆成为连接当地生产者、旅游景点和居民的枢纽。整个地区由出售生产商产品、提供旅游景点信息以及连接居民和客人的旅馆连接起来。通过将客栈变成枢纽，整个地区的经济将得到重振。</li>
<li><strong>客栈是当地的“大使”：</strong>旅馆作为该地区的大使，可以向客人传达该地区的魅力。通过让旅馆的工作人员讲述该地区的魅力，客人可以“爱上该地区”。通过成为大使，该地区的粉丝数量将会增加。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. 参与形式——三种参与方式</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>与生产商合作——产品销售和体验提供：</strong>我们与当地生产商（农民、渔民、啤酒厂等）合作销售产品并提供体验。通过在旅馆中销售生产者的产品，并向旅馆的客人提供生产者的体验，生产者的销售额增加，旅馆的吸引力增加。</li>
<li><strong>与旅游目的地的合作——信息、交通、套餐：</strong>我们与当地旅游目的地（山脉、海滩、温泉、历史建筑等）合作，提供指导、交通和套餐。通过引导旅游目的地、提供交通、销售住宿+旅游目的地套餐，客人可以“体验当地”。</li>
<li><strong>与居民的合作——活动、交流和学习：</strong>我们与当地居民合作提供活动、互动和学习机会。通过引导客人参加当地的节日和活动，创造居民与客人互动的机会，提供向居民学习的机会，让客人体验“社区生活”。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 将其用于入境旅游——3种利用方式</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>作为体验计划提供（请参阅当地体验计划文章）：</strong>我们与当地生产商、旅游景点和居民合作提供体验项目。例如：农场收获体验、与渔民一起体验早市、与当地居民一起捣麻糬体验等。体验项目为入境旅客提供了“体验当地”的宝贵机会。</li>
<li><strong>多语言信息（见外籍人员文章）：</strong>我们提供与当地生产商、旅游景点和居民合作的多语言指导。通过提供英语、汉语、韩语等多种语言的引导，入境客人也可以“体验当地”。将使用外籍员工和翻译工具提供多语言指导。</li>
<li><strong>将其作为当地故事讲述（请参阅品牌文章）：</strong>我们将讲述该地区与当地生产商、旅游景点和居民合作的故事。通过讲述诸如“这种蔬菜是隔壁农民种植的”或“这条鱼是今天早上渔夫捕获的”等故事，客人可以“品尝该地区的味道”。</li>
</ul>
