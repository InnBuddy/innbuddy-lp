隠しコスト(総額が違う)</h2><p>チェックアウトの瞬間に爆発する型です。日本の宿の料金構造——税・サービス料が後から乗る、朝食が別、入湯税がある——は、総額で表示する文化で育ったゲストには「後出し」に見えます。</p><ul><li><strong>対応の順</strong> — 1b:T1db9,<p>Handling complaints from inbound guests is made difficult not so much by the content of the complaint as by <strong>the language barrier</strong>. It takes time to grasp what is wrong; you cannot tell whether the apology landed; confirming the resolution is hard to do. Seen from the other side, though, there is hope. The content of the dissatisfaction <strong>concentrates into four patterns: "gap from the photos," "hidden costs," "communication," and "facilities and safety"</strong> — in other words, even without sharing a language, the templates can be prepared in advance. This article organizes the response principles and the first-response template for each of the four patterns. The general theory of complaint handling (listening, empathy, the next move) is left to a separate article; here we stay on the front line with overseas guests.</p>

<h2>Pattern 1 — gap from the photos</h2>

<p>The most common type. The difference between the booking site’s photos and the actual room — size, view, aging equipment. The anger’s true identity is "the feeling of having been deceived," so opening with an explanation pours oil on the fire.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The order of response</strong> — Accept → explain the facts → offer concrete compensation (a room change, a discount, amenities). Do not change the order. Insert an excuse first, and nothing after it is heard.</li>
<li><strong>The first line</strong> — "We are sorry the room did not match the photos. Let us move you to a room that matches your expectations."</li>
<li><strong>The fundamental fix</strong> — Managing the photographs. An embellished photo buys the best acquisition and the worst complaint at the same time (see the room-photography article).</li>
</ul>

<h2>Pattern 2 — hidden costs (the total is different)</h2>

<p>The type that erupts at the moment of checkout. Japanese properties’ rate structure — tax and service charge added afterward, breakfast separate, an onsen bathing tax — looks like "disclosure at the end" to guests raised in a culture of total-price display.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The order of response</strong> — Accept → explain the rate structure (in writing) → the first time, a one-time accommodation. After that, eliminate it at the root with total-price display.</li>
<li><strong>The fundamental fix</strong> — A design that shows the total from the start, on both the booking-site page and the direct site (see the international cards and direct-site articles).</li>
</ul>

<h2>Pattern 3 — communication</h2>

<p>The type where "it didn’t get through" itself becomes the complaint. Asked the breakfast time three times over; the requested towels never came — most of it begins with a mismatch between "I told them" and "it came through."</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The order of response</strong> — Confirm "understood" → follow up in writing. Anything important is written on a card or memo and handed over. Numbers and dates are read aloud and confirmed on the spot (the readback pattern introduced in the front-desk English article).</li>
<li><strong>Where the translation app belongs</strong> — Explanations of fact (the breakdown of a charge, the location of a facility) may be delivered by app. But apologies and feelings come in your own voice. Receiving an apology in a machine’s voice creates a dissatisfaction separate from the problem itself.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Pattern 4 — facilities and safety</h2>

<p>The heaviest type. Injuries, equipment failures, safety-related events. Here, more than elegance of response, speed and records are everything.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The order of response</strong> — Securing safety comes first → a formal apology → a record of the fix, and a report. To management within 24 hours. The mere existence of a record becomes material for the next trust.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Questions beyond the property’s knowledge — the 24-hour multilingual hotline</h2>

<p>Questions that fit none of the four patterns sometimes arrive. "I feel ill — where is a hospital where English works?" "A disaster has happened — what information should I pass to my family?" "I’m in trouble with this contract." A property does not have to answer all of these itself. In Japan, public hotlines exist for people on the road.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>The Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) operates the Japan Visitor Hotline (050-3816-2787), a multilingual call center open 24 hours a day, year-round. In English, Chinese, Korean, and Japanese, it handles support for emergencies such as illness and disaster as well as general tourist information (the service is for direct consultations from visitors to Japan; three-way interpretation and booking agency are in principle not provided)</strong> — Being able to hand over the number on a single card is, in itself, part of a property’s response capability.</div>

<p>Whether you know this hotline exists changes the first response. Instead of ending an unanswerable question with "I don’t know," receive it with "let’s find out together," and add the hotline card. For an overseas guest, it becomes the memory that "this property knows the way."</p>

<h2>The other side — the limits of templates</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Anger moves faster than templates</strong> — A template guards the first thirty seconds; it does not process all the emotion. Before the template, first hear the other person out without interrupting. The order begins with "listen."</li>
<li><strong>Reciting the template</strong> — Reading an English pattern flatly gets the words right and the care nowhere. The template is the skeleton; the flesh is your own words and expression.</li>
<li><strong>The habit of compensation</strong> — Stack discounts with every apology, and complaints learn to be "a means of price reduction." A one-time accommodation the first time; after that, hold the line of the proper response.</li>
<li><strong>"Polite but never fixed" is the most dangerous</strong> — A property whose handling is praised while the same dissatisfaction recurs three times will eventually be written up as "good at handling, not good as a property." A record of complaints is a forecast of the next complaint. Do not rest satisfied with the response — turn toward eliminating the cause (see the review-management article’s three numbers).</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is, tonight, to make a single card of the four first-response lines. All it needs is the English lines and the JNTO hotline number. Complaints come from beyond the wall of language. The templates can be prepared, ahead of time, on this side of the wall.</p>
