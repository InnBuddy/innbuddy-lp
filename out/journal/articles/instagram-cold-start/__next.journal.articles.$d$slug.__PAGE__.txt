11:T1cdd,<p>"I know we should be on Instagram. But I don't know what to photograph." — Most consultations about a property's social media come down to that one line. The wall at zero is neither technique nor time; it is that the first photo never gets decided. So this article is not about account management — it is a plan for deciding the first ten posts in advance. Even with zero followers, the content of the posts can be designed starting today.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>In the Japan Tourism Agency's survey of foreign-visitor consumption trends (January–March 2024), the most useful pre-trip information source was social media (Instagram, TikTok, X, and the like) at 39.1%, followed by video sites at 36.0% and personal blogs at 26.0%</strong> — Travel plans are being assembled not on official channels but in the places where individuals publish.</div>

<p>The place first seen differs by country. In the same survey's breakdown, video sites (YouTube and others) exceed 50% for travelers from South Korea, while social media ranks high for those from Malaysia and Indonesia. This is not a story of reaching the whole world through Instagram alone. Even so, in an era when travel shortlists are assembled in the places where individuals publish, the meaning of a property turning publisher has not disappeared. So — toward what goal?</p>

<h2>The goal that comes first — not followers, but "saves"</h2>

<p>The first goal is not follower count but whether a post gets saved. A follower is "a person who watches"; a save is "the moment the property enters a travel shortlist." A like vanishes on the spot, but a saved post is reopened on a night of trip-planning weeks or months later. A save, in other words, is the bud of a future booking. The design of the account as a whole (profile, highlights, the arrangement of posts) is left to the "Designing an Instagram Account for Saves" article; this one narrows to its content — what to photograph.</p>

<h2>The first ten posts — once decided, the wall disappears</h2>

<p>Decide what to photograph in advance, and you are freed from "what should I shoot today?" The first ten posts are not the property's full portrait — they are a test. Which of your property's appeals actually gets saved? To find out, here are ten subjects that eight properties out of ten can photograph.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>The guest room in morning light</strong> — The room in its softest hour, just after the curtains are opened.</li>
<li><strong>A close-up of the bed</strong> — The texture of the linen. The distance that makes people want to stay comes from texture, not size.</li>
<li><strong>The bathroom</strong> — Bright and clean. A place that can be a reason to choose you, or a reason to cross you off.</li>
<li><strong>One dish from breakfast</strong> — Not the whole menu; only the single plate you are most confident in.</li>
<li><strong>The view from the window</strong> — Sell not the room but "the world visible from the room."</li>
<li><strong>A signature space</strong> — The lounge, the irori hearth, the engawa porch. The place that becomes the property's face.</li>
<li><strong>A staff member with one line</strong> — A face and a sentence. The warmth of "welcome" gets saved as what makes you different.</li>
<li><strong>An area guide</strong> — What to do nearby. The property is the base camp of the trip.</li>
<li><strong>Before/after</strong> — The styling, before and after. Posts that show the effort become trust.</li>
<li><strong>Seasonal notes</strong> — The dish that exists only now, the scenery that exists only now. An account that has a "now" is alive.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The weekly run — one post plus two Stories a week, for ten weeks</h2>

<p>The pace: one post a week, with two Stories a week showing the backstage (the prep, the staff's ordinary day, today's sky). Run the ten posts over ten weeks. Don't rush; don't stockpile. If in week two you shoot about three posts in one batch and keep them in reserve, you break through the first wall — "no time to shoot" — at a stroke. Ease of continuing beats precision of shooting. The first ten weeks are not a period for getting good; they are a period for keeping it running.</p>

<h2>Week ten — reviewing the saves</h2>

<p>When the ten weeks are done, look at the saves. The standard is simple: identify the three with the most saves, and double down in that direction. Stop the three that didn't grow. Leave no room for second-guessing. The property whose "one dish from breakfast" won keeps shooting breakfast, then the next season's breakfast. The property whose "view from the window" won keeps shooting the window through the seasons. The ten posts are not an introduction of the property's full portrait; they are a test of which appeal gets saved. In ten weeks, one winning line of your property comes into view.</p>

<h2>The other side of starting from zero</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>An account that stops updating can be worse than no account</strong> — An account whose last post is two years old plants the worry "are they even open?" Before starting, check the calendar for whether ten weeks of posts are sustainable.</li>
<li><strong>Copyright and permission</strong> — Reposting another photographer's pictures, or photos a guest has posted, requires permission. When receiving a photo to repost, ask first, in words.</li>
<li><strong>Staff portraits</strong> — Get the consent of staff who appear in photos before publishing. The decision to appear or not belongs, in principle, to the person.</li>
<li><strong>Frames without other guests in them</strong> — When shooting guest experiences, watch for guests' faces in the background. Privacy is the foundation of a property's trust.</li>
<li><strong>Consistency between editing and reality</strong> — Over-saturated photos turn into disappointment on arrival, and come back as reviews. Correct only as far as "what it actually looked like."</li>
<li><strong>Don't buy numbers</strong> — Buying followers or likes shaves away the account's credibility itself. The ten weeks from zero are the period for building the thing that cannot be bought.</li>
</ul>

<p>After ten weeks, you will know "the one photo that gets saved." It probably sits somewhere other than what you consider your "selling point." Learning that is the greatest reward of starting from zero. Start small: this week, write the list of ten posts on paper. The first can be the guest room in morning light.</p>

<h2>为什么现在值得关注？</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>客人期待的变化：</strong>“酒店 Instagram - 计划现在从 0 条帖子增加到 10 条帖子” 客人的期望与五年前相比发生了变化。过去“有了更好”的服务，如今已变成“没有就难以被选择”。适应这些变化，是住宿设施持续经营的前提。</li>
<li><strong>与地区关系：</strong>在网上吸引顾客的努力增加了整个地区的价值，而不仅仅是旅馆本身的价值。通过与当地生产者和周边商户合作，旅馆的行动会成为“当地故事”。社区共同参与，才更容易长期持续。</li>
<li><strong>不增加员工负担：</strong>新举措不应增加员工负担。通过建立机制，用一张表或每次15分钟的学习来运转，就能在不增加员工负担的情况下持续改进。</li>
</ul>

<h2>避免失败的3个要点</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>从小处开始——先做好一件事：</strong>一开始就想全部改变，往往难以持续。先从一件事开始。可以从一间客房、一个货架或一句话开始；即使效果不理想，影响也小，更容易调整。</li>
<li><strong>记录——用一张表看清楚：</strong>把行动记录在一张表里，效果就能被看见。你会知道哪些做法有效、哪些需要调整，记录也会成为下一轮改进的依据。</li>
<li><strong>持续——每周一次，每次15分钟：</strong>新行动需要持续。每周留出15分钟，逐渐形成习惯，最后才会成为团队文化。</li>
</ol>
