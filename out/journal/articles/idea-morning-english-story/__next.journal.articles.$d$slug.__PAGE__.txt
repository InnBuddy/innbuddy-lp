That is 15.8% more than the previous year (36.87 million). It is no longer an era of “What do we do if overseas guests come?” but of “How do we design a breakfast room on the assumption that they will come?” And the morning is also the time of day when guests are most tense at an inn. On the first morning, it is the inn’s guidance that lets guests, not yet used to the air of the place, open the door of the breakfast room.</p><h2>Miso soup, and the tempura dipping-sauce bowl</h2><p>In a 2024 survey (325 respondents) by the travel media outlet Tokyo Cheapo, the top answer to “What was the most difficult thing about traveling in Japan?” was “the language barrier,” at 43%. Among the true stories submitted to this survey is this one. At a ryokan breakfast, a woman guest from South Africa said that “the miso soup was delicious,” but the bowl she had emptied was the dipping sauce for the tempura. She had not touched the miso soup.</p><div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>“The language barrier” was the top travel difficulty, at 43% (Tokyo Cheapo survey, 325 respondents, 2024)</strong> — Among the responses is an account of “eating the tempura dipping sauce by mistake for miso soup.” This mistake is not the guest’s problem but a missing explanation.</div><p>The reason this story hits home is that no one is at fault. The guest ate in earnest, and the inn served the meal as usual. All that was missing was one line of explanation between one bowl and the next. Breakfast is, as it is, a small museum. Without captions, it cannot be appreciated correctly. Conveying how to spend the morning in English is, in short, the job of writing captions for an exhibition.</p><h2>Nouns and numbers are enough for English</h2><p>The English on a breakfast card does not need to be sentences. It is the time, the items, and where they come from. <strong>7:30–9:00 / rice / miso soup / grilled fish / egg / pickles / coffee or tea</strong>. This conveys the floor plan of breakfast. There are four tips. Write times in numerals (7:30–9:00). Use simple nouns for words (grilled fish is enough). Place the card next to the actual food (conversation works by pointing). Make it something guests can point to even if they cannot pronounce it. Greetings begin with a single “Good morning.” Conversely, it is also good to place a card that teaches guests “ohayō.” Learning greetings from each other is a device by which greetings arise from the guests’ side even before the inn speaks to them.</p><h2>The B&B is a form of inn with breakfast in its name</h2><p>Turning our gaze abroad, the “B&B,” a form of lodging born in Britain, stands for Bed and Breakfast — half of its name is breakfast. A form of lodging that pairs a room with breakfast became, as it was, the name of the business. In Europe, breakfast has a history of being fought over as the inn’s main battleground. Japanese inns have been sitting in a special seat on that battleground from the start, because a Japanese breakfast is a prime example of a breakfast worth explaining. Turned around, the story of the miso soup and the dipping sauce means that a Japanese breakfast, once explained, stands head and shoulders above the world’s breakfasts in interest.</p><h2>A morning that needs no words, and a line that concerns life</h2><p>Some morning experiences need no words. Radio calisthenics (rajio taisō) is a culture of morning sound in Japan that began broadcasting on November 1, 1928 (Shōwa 3), as “National Health Exercises” by the Postal Life Insurance Bureau of the Ministry of Communications (it was proposed as a project to celebrate the enthronement of Emperor Shōwa, modeled on the efforts of an American life insurance company). Invite guests to join a local morning exercise session in their exercise clothes — a shared experience of moving the body closes distance even before translation.</p><p>And the top-priority line is allergies. Show an <strong>Any allergies?</strong> card at check-in, and always confirm, even if only with a translation app. Even if the story of a dish can miss, the allergy check is only complete once you have confirmed “Did it get across?” Information that concerns life is received not with nouns and numbers but through two-way exchange. This alone is the exception.</p><h2>Before “adding more” English</h2><ul><li><strong>Inns it suits</strong> — Inns where the share of overseas guests is growing.
