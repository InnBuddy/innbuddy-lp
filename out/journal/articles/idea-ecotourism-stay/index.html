「エコ」の語源は、ギリシャ語のオイコス — 「家」です。エコロジー（生態学）もエコノミー（経済）も、同じ「家」の言葉から始まっています。つまりエコツーリズムとは、自然を壊さずに楽しむことと、自然で生計を立てることが一つの家に住むことなのです。この記事は、その両立を宿の小さな体験に落とす話です。

言葉は1983年、メキシコで生まれました

エコツーリズムという言葉は、1983年7月、メキシコの環境建築家ヘクター・セバロス＝ラスクラインが造ったとされます。彼はユカタン半島の湿地を守る活動をしており、自然地域へ「学び、鑑賞し、楽しむ」ために旅する形態をこう呼びました。1990年には国際エコツーリズム学会（TIES）が設立され、今日よく引かれる定義が示されます。「自然地域への責任ある旅であり、環境を保全し、地域住民の福祉を高めるもの」。注目すべきは、この定義の3分の2が「環境」ではなく「保全と地域」であることです。エコツーリズムの本体は自然ではなく、守る仕組みです。

FACTエコツーリズムの言葉は1983年、メキシコのヘクター・セバロス＝ラスクラインが造語し、1990年設立の国際エコツーリズム学会（TIES）は「環境を保全し、地域住民の福祉を高める、自然地域への責任ある旅」と定義した — 定義の核心は「楽しむ自然」ではなく「守り、地域に届く仕組み」である。

日本は2007年、観光を「制限する」法律を作りました

日本にも独自の歩みがあります。2007年6月、エコツーリズム推進法（平成19年法律第105号）が成立し、翌2008年4月に施行されました。法律が定めるのは、自然観光資源を持続的に保護すること、地域の多様な主体が連携すること。そして受け入れ側には、入域者数や区域を制限する工夫や、専門ガイドの養成までが求められています。観光振興の法律でありながら、中身が「増やさない工夫」です。この逆説こそがエコツーリズムの本質で、宿が体験を設計するときも同じ問いが立ちます。この体験は、この土地にとって増やして良い量なのでしょうか。

世界で最も厳しい「見学のルール」が守る島

仕組みの極限が、ガラパゴス諸島です。諸島の陸地の約97%は国立公園。国立公園区域内を観光客が歩くには、国立公園局が養成・免許を発行する公認ナチュラリストガイドの同行が義務で、1人のガイドが案内できるのは最大16人とされます。持ち出せるのは写真だけで、石一本ですら持ち帰れません。動物との距離、触れないこと、ルートの厳守。1978年、世界遺産の最初の12件の一角に選ばれたこの島は、「勝手に歩かせない」ことで自然を守り、ガイドという職と入園料という財源で地域を成立させてきました。エコツーリズムの定義 — 守る・学ぶ・地域に届く — が、そのまま制度になった場所です。

  • 有料ガイドの境界 — 宿の体験を「案内」として提供する場合は、旅行業の登録や地域のガイドとの役割分担を確認してください。

The root of “eco” is the Greek oikos — “house.” Ecology and economy both start from the same word, “house.” In other words, ecotourism means that enjoying nature without destroying it and making a living from nature live in a single house. This article is about bringing that balance down to small experiences at an inn.

The word was born in Mexico in 1983

The word “ecotourism” is said to have been coined in July 1983 by the Mexican environmental architect Héctor Ceballos-Lascuráin. He was working to protect wetlands on the Yucatán Peninsula, and used the word for a form of travel to natural areas in order to “study, admire, and enjoy” them. In 1990, The International Ecotourism Society (TIES) was founded, and the definition often cited today was set out: “responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people.” What deserves attention is that two-thirds of this definition is not about “the environment” but about “conservation and the local community.” The core of ecotourism is not nature but the system that protects it.

FACTThe word “ecotourism” was coined in 1983 by Mexico’s Héctor Ceballos-Lascuráin, and The International Ecotourism Society (TIES), founded in 1990, defined it as “responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment and improves the well-being of local people” — The heart of the definition is not “nature to enjoy” but “a system that protects it and reaches the local community.”

In 2007, Japan made a law that “limits” tourism

Japan has its own path as well. In June 2007, the Ecotourism Promotion Act (Act No. 105 of 2007) was enacted, and it came into force in April 2008. What the law sets out is the sustainable protection of natural tourism resources and cooperation among the region’s diverse actors. And the host side is asked for measures that limit the number of visitors and the areas they enter, and even for training specialist guides. It is a law for promoting tourism, yet its content is “measures for not increasing.” This paradox is the very essence of ecotourism, and the same question arises when an inn designs an experience: is this experience an amount this land can afford to increase?

An island protected by the world’s strictest “rules for visiting”

The extreme of such a system is the Galápagos Islands. About 97% of the islands’ land area is national park. For tourists to walk within the national park area, they are required to be accompanied by a certified naturalist guide trained and licensed by the national park service, and one guide is said to be able to lead at most 16 people. The only things you can take away are photos; you cannot take home so much as a single stone. Distance from animals, not touching, strictly keeping to routes. Chosen in 1978 as one of the first 12 World Heritage Sites, these islands have protected their nature by “not letting people walk wherever they like,” and have sustained the local community through the occupation of guide and the revenue of entrance fees. It is a place where the definition of ecotourism — protect, learn, reach the local community — became a system as it is.

CASEAbout 97% of the Galápagos Islands’ land area is national park; being accompanied by a certified naturalist guide is mandatory (up to 16 people per guide), and the only thing you can take away is photos — The extreme of a system in which “nature remains because there are guides.” The three conditions — protect, learn, reach the local community — are realized as a system.

What a small inn can learn from the Galápagos is the weight of the presence of a guide. A place where people are guided preserves both nature and the quality of the experience better than a place where people walk freely. If the innkeeper can name ten of the land’s birds and wildflowers, that is already a kind of qualification.

Small ecotourism within the inn

  • A “this week you can see” board — Update three lines every week: flowers in bloom now, birds you can hear now, stars you can see now. Freshness is the life of nature guidance.
  • An observation list — Place a single checklist of birds, insects, flowers, stars, and sounds. For children it becomes a treasure hunt; adults become observers.
  • Lending binoculars and field guides — When the tools change, the world you can see changes. Choose regional field guides. Rather than a guide to the world, “one book for this land.”
  • The inn’s observation notebook — A notebook in which guests write down what they found. The previous guest’s record becomes the next guest’s guide.
  • A 15-minute observation session a day — In the morning, count birds in front of the inn for just 15 minutes. Rather than a long tour, a smallness you can keep up is ecotourism.

The responsibility of those who trade on nature

  • Laws on collecting — In national parks, quasi-national parks, and prefectural nature conservation areas, collecting plants and animals is prohibited by law. “The only thing you may take is photos” is a manner to keep not only in the Galápagos.
  • Don’t feed — Feeding birds or fish shortens their lives. Do not call the subjects of observation with food.
  • The rarer it is, the less you write its location — Writing the location of precious flowers or creatures in detail on social media can invite poaching and overharvesting. Keep the record, but blur the location.
  • The boundary of paid guiding — If you offer the inn’s experience as “guiding,” check travel-agency registration and the division of roles with local guides.
  • Don’t let it be all signboard — Once you call yourself “eco,” the reality of your detergents, wastewater, and garbage is called into question. If words run ahead, trust collapses later.

The word “ecotourism” was born in 1983, Japan set out “tourism that limits” by law in 2007, and the Galápagos protects its islands with certified guides alone. What they have in common is designing not nature itself but “the system that protects it.” Start small, by writing three lines about “this week” in the nature in front of the inn. If you write what you saw this week, that is already the beginning of guiding.
