11:T1b19,<p>OTA commissions, depending on the contract, are generally put at 15–25%. Paid every time a booking comes in — something like a "tax" on the room revenue. Taking a 20-room property as an example, this article organizes, down to the amounts, the three mechanisms that raised the direct booking ratio from 22% to 42% (with ADR held flat) — with the declaration up front that <strong>every number in it is a reference value and simulation for showing the approach</strong>. As a yardstick: in the example of 20 rooms (ADR ¥15,000, commission 15%, 180 additional direct guest-nights a year), the annual commission saved comes to about ¥8.1 million (a reference value).</p>

<p>But first, a premise. OTAs are not the enemy; they are "channels." The current 22% is demand the OTAs delivered to your property. The goal is to convert the "second night onward" of that demand to direct. The rule to mind here is price. Cutting the OTA-side price to make a gap is, under many contracts, the road that touches parity (same-price) clauses — the losing road. But the world around this rule is moving. In Europe in 2024, it shifted toward banning those parity clauses themselves (the details of that event are in the "Which OTA First?" article). It is not a story that changes Japanese properties’ contracts with each company tomorrow — but <strong>if you cannot make the difference with price, make it with benefits</strong> — that idea is right in any era.</p>

<h2>Mechanism 1 — making direct "the rational choice"</h2>

<p>The design principle is <strong>"same price, better benefits."</strong> Keep the direct price identical to the OTA’s, and add two or three small benefits.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Breakfast included</strong> (cost ¥400–600, felt value around ¥1,500 — reference values)</li>
<li><strong>A welcome drink</strong> (cost under ¥200)</li>
<li><strong>Late checkout on off-peak days</strong> (at the front desk’s discretion)</li>
</ul>

<p>Show this "total value" on the booking screen, and the direct page looks effectively 5–10% cheaper. What matters is <strong>not lowering the room rate itself</strong>. Simply converting the commission budget into "benefits" keeps the margin per night intact.</p>

<h2>Mechanism 2 — the 4 touchpoints of guidance</h2>

<p>Direct bookings do not occur naturally. Place the guidance at the four moments when the guest’s interest in your property is at its highest.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>The QR code in the room</strong> — At check-in, when the room is put in order in the evening. In the hours of highest satisfaction, add one line: "For your next stay, book direct with breakfast included."</li>
<li><strong>The booking-confirmation email</strong> — Layer the direct link onto the one or two sentences of "Thank you for choosing XX."</li>
<li><strong>The signature on review replies</strong> — Put one line with the direct booking page’s URL on every reply. Replies are read as material for the next booking.</li>
<li><strong>The bio on your social profiles</strong> — Keep the direct link pinned there. Posts flow past; the bio is read every time.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Mechanism 3 — CRM that does not end at the booking</h2>

<p>Repeaters are, in the end, the cheapest channel. A repeater’s lifetime value can be estimated at about 2.3 times a first-time guest’s (an illustrative figure) — with no OTA commission on top. Two moves.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The "next season" email, 30 days after checkout</strong> — With a seasonal campaign attached. Frequency: at most once a month. More than that shaves trust (the legal points to check before sending emails are in the Email and CRM article).</li>
<li><strong>Instant response to inquiries</strong> — Replying within one hour to "Any availability this weekend?" is the golden time of direct booking. Miss it, and that booking goes back through an OTA.</li>
</ul>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">SIMULATION</span><strong>At a property of 20 rooms, ADR ¥15,000, commission 15%, suppose the direct ratio shifts from 22% to 42% (over 10 months, ADR held flat): the annual commission saved is about ¥8.1 million (a reference value). All of this is a simulation example for showing the approach; actual results differ by the property’s scale, contract terms, and execution</strong> — To judge the effect, record the direct ratio, repeat rate, and ADR before and after with the same counting method, and compare.</div>

<h2>The other side — when direct exhausts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The failed trade-off</strong> — The most common failure is the move to "shrink the OTAs and grow direct." OTAs capture new demand; direct captures continuing demand — grow both, and revenue and profit turn up together. Shrinking the total is not a winning line.</li>
<li><strong>Benefits left to pickle</strong> — Benefits are "a reason to book direct," not "everyone’s vested right." A benefit once given is hard to withdraw — review cost and effect monthly.</li>
<li><strong>Pushing the guidance</strong> — The QR guidance belongs only in moments of high satisfaction. Said in front of a dissatisfied guest, "next time, book direct" is a phrase that gets written into word of mouth.</li>
<li><strong>The illusion of zero commission</strong> — Direct costs money too. Maintaining the site, payment fees, the labor of handling it. The misconception "direct = free" breeds a shoddy direct experience (see the direct-site minimum-setup article).</li>
<li><strong>An unchecked contract</strong> — Before designing benefits, reread the price-and-benefits clauses of your current OTA contracts. Parity conditions differ contract by contract.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is, tonight, to decide on two benefits. Under ¥1,000 in cost, carrying the character of your property — add them to the booking page and the confirmation email. The number 42% is only someone’s example; but the structure — "at the same price, a slightly better choice exists" — can be built at any property, starting today.</p>

<h2>窍门一：让直连成为“理性选择”</h2>
<p>设计原则是“同等价格，更好效益”。直接价格与 OTA 相同，但有 2-3 个小额外费用：</p>
<ul>
<li>含早餐（成本价400-600日元，体验值1,500日元）</li>
<li>迎宾饮料（成本价200日元以下）</li>
<li>非繁忙日的延迟退房选项（由前台自行决定）</li>
</ul>
<p>如果您在预订屏幕上显示“总价值”，直接页面会显得便宜 5-10%。重要的是不要降低房价本身。只需将您的佣金预算转换为“奖励”并花费它，您的每晚利润就保持不变。</p>
<h2>技巧2：4个接触点指导</h2>
<p>直接预订不会自然发生。 “引导”您的客人对您最感兴趣的四个时刻：</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>房间二维码</strong> — 安排在黄金时间以获得满意：办理入住时和晚上房间准备好时。 “在这个季节，有直达航班，并且包含早餐，”他补充道。</li>
<li><strong>预订确认电子邮件</strong> — 1-2 句话“感谢您选择[旅馆名称]”+直接链接。</li>
<li><strong>审核回复签名</strong> — 在每个回复中包含一行“直接预订页面”URL。</li>
<li><strong>社交网络简介</strong> — 直接链接到 bar-io，每次都会附上故事。</li>
</ol>
<h2>技巧 3：CRM 不以预订结束</h2>
<p>中继器最终是最便宜的渠道。假设回头客的LTV约为首次客人的2.3倍（示例值），并且不收取OTA费用。两项动议：</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>结账后 30 天发送“下一季”电子邮件</strong> — 包括季节性活动（例如秋叶季节 15% 折扣）。频率最多每月一次。超过这个数字就会削弱信任。</li>
<li><strong>即时聊天回复</strong> — “这个周末你有空吗？”一小时内回复是预订的黄金时间。如果您错过了，您的预订将通过 OTA 进行。</li>
</ul>
