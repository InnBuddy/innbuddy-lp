11:T20ef,<p>"Foreign staff? Impossible for a regional property like ours" — that premise is already old. The foreign nationals working in Japan grow year by year, and the front lines of lodging are no exception. This article’s position is this: hiring foreign staff in a regional city is not a downgraded copy of the urban version — it is <strong>an independent strategy designed around two strengths (cost-performance and depth of local connection) and two challenges (the missing colleague community and the missing multilingual infrastructure)</strong>. First, let us confirm the fact underneath.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare’s summary of notifications on foreign employment, foreign workers numbered 2,302,587 as of the end of October 2024 (up 12.4% year on year), updating the record high for the twelfth consecutive year since 2013. By industry, accommodation and food services account for about 12% of the total; by nationality, Vietnam (24.8%) comes first, followed by China and the Philippines. The number of establishments employing foreign nationals (342,087) is also a record</strong> — Foreign staff are not "people who will come someday" — they are already beside us in this industry.</div>

<h2>The framework of the system — the road called Specified Skilled Worker "Accommodation"</h2>

<p>When thinking about hiring, having the minimum map of the status-of-residence world speeds up the conversation. In April 2019, lodging became usable as one of the fields covered by "Specified Skilled Worker," a status of residence created as a measure for labor shortages.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>The "Accommodation" field of the Specified Skilled Worker status was created in April 2019. Workers may engage in lodging services in general — front desk, guest service, planning and publicity, food-and-beverage service — including incidental simple tasks. Category (i) carries a total period of stay of five years with no family accompaniment, but in August 2023 accommodation was added to the fields eligible for Category (ii), which has no upper limit on the period of stay, allows family accompaniment, and opens a path to permanent residence. Acceptance is limited to operators licensed for ryokan or hotel business; simple lodging houses (minshuku, guesthouses, and the like) are not eligible</strong> — The direction the system faces is not short-term, disposable labor but people to raise over the long term.</div>

<p>This fact has the power to change the design of hiring. Category (i)’s five-year cap and Category (ii)’s lack of one — in other words, <strong>a path has been opened, as a matter of system, to keep employing a person you have spent five years training</strong>. For a small regional property, this is the option of hiring not "labor that leaves after a few years" but "a colleague who puts down roots in the region."</p>

<h2>The two strengths — materials only the regions have</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Cost-performance</strong> — While salary levels sit below the cities, appeal can be built with the living environment (rent, nature, commuting). A design that fills the gap with the cities using "ease of living" is the regions’ exclusive franchise.</li>
<li><strong>The depth of local connection</strong> — Foreign staff working at a regional property can become, for guests, <strong>"a bridge to the region."</strong> Multilingual guidance, cultural mediation, the empathy of a fellow foreigner. The degree to which one staff member at a small property stays in a guest’s memory is worth dozens at a large one.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The two challenges and their countermeasures</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>The missing colleague community</strong> — The circle of "friends and seniors from the same country" that forms naturally in the cities does not exist in the regions. The countermeasure is deliberate design — an online community with same-nationality, same-language staff or nearby businesses, regular video calls, a "staff-only gathering" in the off-season. Isolation is one of the biggest causes of resignation six months after hiring.</li>
<li><strong>The missing multilingual infrastructure</strong> — Nearby, there are no offices that handle government, medical, and daily-life procedures in foreign languages. The countermeasure is <strong>a "procedures guide"</strong> — the procedures needed in the first 30 days (resident registration, banking, insurance, a mobile phone) gathered into one booklet, with dates, counters, and what to bring. It is a self-help tool for the property, one that runs without depending on someone’s spare hands. The burden of training is distributed by combining it with the buddy system from the teaching article.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The design of retention — 3 items</h2>

<ol start="3">
<li><strong>Housing support</strong> — In the regions, "securing a place to live" is itself a condition of hiring. Keep at hand a real-estate agent you can refer to, staff housing, a floor plan of a boarding house. Almost no urban hiring gets this far. This is where the difference is made.</li>
<li><strong>A career path tied to the region</strong> — Regional ambassador, multilingual guide, buyer of local products — make "the meaning of working here" concrete in job titles. The more a region has a salary ceiling, the more the language of career becomes the foundation of retention.</li>
<li><strong>Seasonal rotation (an optional benefit)</strong> — Short-term rotation with an urban sister property or a partner inn. Not compulsory; offered as a benefit to those who want it, it becomes a vent for feelings of confinement.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The other side — when hiring exhausts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The principle of direct employment</strong> — Acceptance under Specified Skilled Worker (i) is direct employment only; dispatch is not permitted. When a staffing agency says "it’s easy to take them on," check the framework of the system.</li>
<li><strong>Where housing support ends</strong> — Introducing a house and managing a life are different things. Leave the rent subsidy and the name on the lease ambiguous, and you build a structure where, on resignation, the person loses both.</li>
<li><strong>Workplace Japanese</strong> — The Japanese used at work is far narrower and deeper than everyday conversation. Do not hand over entirely the training that starts from twenty on-the-job phrases (see the Japanese-training article).</li>
<li><strong>The receiving side’s homework</strong> — Managing the types and expiry dates of statuses of residence is the property’s duty. Leaving an expiry alone becomes a risk not only for the staff but for the property itself. Stay connected with a specialist.</li>
<li><strong>The danger of "bundled" displays</strong> — Designs such as showing a hired staff member’s country beside their name on the front-desk name tag should be confirmed with the person. Do not turn a bridge-builder into a symbol.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is to write your property’s "regional package" on one sheet — housing (how much, where they can live), salary, the 30-day procedures guide, the buddy’s name. A hiring interview with that one sheet is a different conversation from one without it. If the cities win with money, the regions win with the design of living. And only your land can draw that blueprint.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. Systems of status of residence and employment change. For practice, please check the latest publications of the Immigration Services Agency and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, and consult specialists.</div>

12:Te8d,<p>在当地城市招募外国人，应围绕<strong>两大优势（性价比/本地联系）和两大挑战（缺乏同事社区/缺乏多语言基础设施）</strong>进行设计。本文是一篇总结与城市差异以及农村设施设计的基础文章。</p>
<h2>1.两大优势</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>性价比</strong> ——工资低于城市，居住环境对移民有吸引力。</li>
<li><strong>本地连接深度</strong> — 接待客人的工作人员<strong>“通往该地区的桥梁”</strong>（多语言指导和文化中介）。</li>
</ul>
<h2>2、两大挑战及对策</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>缺乏同侪社区</strong> → 在线同事社区+定期视频通话+“仅限员工聚会”。</li>
<li><strong>缺乏多语言基础设施（行政、医疗、程序）</strong> → <strong>《流程指南》</strong>（前 30 天有记录）+ 好友系统（参见“如何教学”文章）。</li>
</ol>
<h2>3、地方城市保留设计（3项）</h2>
<ol start="3">
<li><strong>住房支持</strong> ——在农村地区，“居住地”是就业的一个条件。</li>
<li><strong>与该地区相关的职业道路</strong> — 营造一种在这里工作意味着什么的感觉，例如担任区域大使或多语言导游。</li>
<li><strong>「季节轮换」</strong> — 与城市地区的短期轮换（可选福利）。</li>
</ol>

<h2>为什么现在值得关注？</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>客人期待的变化：</strong>对于“地方城市的外籍员工——与城市地区的差异及保留设计”，客人的期望与五年前相比发生了变化。过去“有了更好”的服务，如今已变成“没有就难以被选择”。适应这些变化，是住宿设施持续经营的前提。</li>
<li><strong>与地区关系：</strong>人力资源和招聘工作增加了整个地区的价值，而不仅仅是旅馆本身的价值。通过与当地生产者和周边商户合作，旅馆的行动会成为“当地故事”。社区共同参与，才更容易长期持续。</li>
<li><strong>不增加员工负担：</strong>新举措不应增加员工负担。通过建立机制，用一张表或每次15分钟的学习来运转，就能在不增加员工负担的情况下持续改进。</li>
</ul>

<h2>避免失败的3个要点</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>从小处开始——先做好一件事：</strong>一开始就想全部改变，往往难以持续。先从一件事开始。可以从一间客房、一个货架或一句话开始；即使效果不理想，影响也小，更容易调整。</li>
<li><strong>记录——用一张表看清楚：</strong>把行动记录在一张表里，效果就能被看见。你会知道哪些做法有效、哪些需要调整，记录也会成为下一轮改进的依据。</li>
<li><strong>持续——每周一次，每次15分钟：</strong>新行动需要持续。每周留出15分钟，逐渐形成习惯，最后才会成为团队文化。</li>
</ol>
