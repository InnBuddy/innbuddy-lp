16:T20fa,<p>茶畑のある宿の案内は、たいてい「茶摘み体験」に行き着きます。ですが、茶摘みは年間のごく一部の季節にしかできない体験です。茶畑の物語は、摘む前の霜との戦いと、摘んだ後の工程と、その土地の茶の作法の中にあります。この記事は、茶畑のある宿を、暦と数字と工程から設計し直します。</p>

<h2>1. 暦——八十八夜は、霜との賭けの決着日です</h2>
<p>「夏も近づく八十八夜」——この日は、立春から数えて88日目、5月2日頃を指す雑節です。お茶の世界で八十八夜が特別なのは、「八十八夜の別れ霜」という言葉のためです。お茶の新芽は、一度霜に当たると駄目になります。品種改良が進む前の時代、この日を過ぎればようやく遅霜の心配が消え、茶摘みを本格化できる——八十八夜は、<strong>霜との賭けの決着日</strong>でした。1656年の伊勢暦に初めて記され、のちに暦に正式採用されたといいます（季節の雑節の解説による）。八十八夜に摘んだ新茶が縁起物とされるのは、「八」の重なりと、冬の間に蓄えた養分をたっぷり含んだ最初の収穫だからです。</p>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">DID YOU KNOW?</span><strong>茶畑に巨大な扇風機が立っている理由</strong> — 茶産地でよく見かける防霜ファンは、上空の暖かい空気（逆転層）を下ろして茶樹を霜から守る装置である。お茶の産地の冬から春の夜は、ファンの音とともにある——茶畑の近くの宿が、静けさの説明と一緒に伝えておきたい土地の音である。</div>

<h2>2. 数字——お茶の地図は、いま書き換わっています</h2>
<p>日本の荒茶生産は、長らく静岡県が首位を続けてきました。その静岡県が2024年（令和6年）の荒茶生産量で鹿児島県に抜かれ、統計が残る昭和34年以降で初めて2位になりました（農林水産省の発表を報じた産経新聞による。鹿児島約2万7,200トン、静岡約2万5,800トン）。背景には、抹茶需要の拡大に伴う鹿児島の碾茶（抹茶の原料）生産の急増があるのです。一方、静岡の茶の栽培面積は約1万2,800ヘクタールで、依然として全国の36.5%を占めます。</p>
<p>この数字は、茶畑の宿の案内を面白くします。ゲストが泊まっている茶畑は、どこの茶なのでしょうか——静岡か、鹿児島か、そのほかの産地か。産地の順位の話は、お茶の土地の現在進行形の物語です。</p>

<h2>3. 世界——草を敷くだけで、遺産になります</h2>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>静岡の「茶草場農法」——2013年、世界農業遺産</strong> — 静岡県の茶産地には、茶園に敷く草（茶草）を刈る草刈場「茶草場（ちゃぐさば）」が点在する。乾燥させた草を茶園に敷くと土が活き、味と香りの良い茶が育つ——この伝統農法を続けた結果、茶草場には300種以上の草地性植物が観察され、秋の七草やササユリといった希少植物も守られてきた。農業と生物多様性が同じ方向を向いた事例として、2013年、FAOの世界農業遺産（GIAHS）に認定された（掛川市観光協会等による）。</div>
<p>この事例の本質は、<strong>「良いお茶を作ろうとすることが、そのまま草地の生き物を守った」</strong>という構造です。特別な保護活動ではなく、お茶づくりのこだわりが風景を守りました。茶畑の宿の物語も、この文法で書けます——うちの茶畑の草は、どこから来て、敷かれているのでしょうか。その一問が、茶畑に生き物の層を重ねます。</p>

<h2>4. 工程——摘む、蒸す、揉む、乾かす</h2>
<p>煎茶は、摘んだ葉がそのまま茶になるわけではありません。工程はおおむね4段階です。<strong>摘む</strong>（一芯二葉——芽と若葉2枚を指で摘む）→<strong>蒸す</strong>（蒸気で酸化を止めます。緑茶が緑茶である理由）→<strong>揉む</strong>（形を整えながら乾かします。手揉みでは、丸め、押し、ほぐす工程を何度も繰り返します）→<strong>乾燥</strong>。茶揉み体験の醍醐味は、工程の中で<strong>香りが青臭さからお茶の香りに変わっていく瞬間</strong>に出会えることです。体験は、蒸しの後の揉みから始める形（茶園や加工場と組む）が現実的で、指先の温度と鍋の加減は、子どもの手でも存分に感じられます。</p>

<h2>5. 実践——茶畑の宿の時間割</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>茶摘み（季節限定）</strong> — 一番茶は4月末〜5月。八十八夜を目安に、茶畑の年間で最も華やかな2週間。二番茶・三番茶と続きますが、体験は一番茶の新芽が断然良いのです。</li>
<li><strong>手揉み（通年可）</strong> — 蒸した茶葉（冷凍保存したものでも）があれば、茶畑のない季節でも体験ができます。</li>
<li><strong>客室の茶</strong> — 急須と茶碗を客室に。湯の温度（深蒸しは熱め、上級茶はぬるめ）を1行で添えましょう。お湯を注ぐ音と湯気は、部屋の中の茶畑です。</li>
<li><strong>茶畑の散歩</strong> — 冬の剪定、春の萌芽、夏の日除け、秋の茶の実。摘み以外の季節にも、茶畑には見どころの時間割があります。</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. 安全と現実——茶は、生きている作物です</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>農薬の期間</strong> — 摘採の前には、散布から収穫までの安全基準日数を守る運用がされています。体験で葉を摘む場合は、茶園の管理者に「今日摘んでよい畑か」を必ず確認しましょう。</li>
<li><strong>カフェイン</strong> — 濃い茶は子どもや妊婦の方には控えめに、というのが一般的な考え方です。案内に1行添える程度で十分といえます。</li>
<li><strong>私有地</strong> — 茶畑は誰かの畑です。摘み取りは案内された区画だけで（<a href="#idea-village-walk-path">集落の散歩道</a>と同じ作法）。</li>
<li><strong>防霜ファンの音</strong> — 茶畑に近い宿では、春の夜にファンが回ります。静けさが売りの宿ほど、この音の説明（霜から茶を守る音）を先に書いておきたいところです。</li>
</ul>
<div class="note"><span class="tag">InnBuddy VIEW</span>茶畑の宿の一番の体験は、実は摘みでも揉みでもなく、<strong>「その土地の茶を、その土地の湯で飲む」</strong>ことです。水の味が違えば、同じ茶葉でも茶の味が変わります（<a href="#idea-spring-water-taste">里山の水</a>の話と地続きです）。茶畑を見て、茶を淹れて、湯の話をする——この3点がそろったとき、茶畑の宿は完成します。</div>

1b:T2462,<p>The guidance of an inn with a tea field usually ends up at “a tea-picking experience.” But tea picking is an experience available in only a small part of the year. The story of a tea field lies in the fight against frost before picking, in the processes after picking, and in the tea manners of that land. This article redesigns the inn with a tea field from the calendar, the numbers, and the processes.</p>

<h2>1. The calendar — Hachijū-hachiya is the day the wager against frost is settled</h2>
<p>“Summer is drawing near, on the eighty-eighth night” — this day, Hachijū-hachiya, is a zassetsu (a seasonal marker of the old calendar) that falls on the 88th day counted from Risshun, the start of spring, around May 2. Hachijū-hachiya is special in the world of tea because of the phrase “the parting frost of the eighty-eighth night.” Once new tea shoots are struck by frost, they are ruined. In the era before plant breeding advanced, only after this day had passed did the worry of late frost finally disappear, and tea picking could begin in earnest — Hachijū-hachiya was <strong>the day the wager against frost was settled</strong>. It is said to have first appeared in the Ise calendar of 1656 and to have been formally adopted into the calendar later (according to explanations of the seasonal zassetsu). New tea picked on Hachijū-hachiya is considered auspicious because of the doubled “eight,” and because it is the first harvest, full of the nutrients the plant stored through the winter.</p>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">DID YOU KNOW?</span><strong>Why giant fans stand in tea fields</strong> — The frost-protection fans often seen in tea-growing areas are devices that bring warm air down from above (the inversion layer) to protect the tea bushes from frost. In tea-growing regions, the nights from winter into spring come with the sound of the fans — a sound of the land that an inn near the tea fields will want to explain together with its account of the quiet.</div>

<h2>2. The numbers — the map of tea is being rewritten right now</h2>
<p>For a long time, Shizuoka Prefecture held the top position in Japan’s aracha (crude tea) production. In 2024 (Reiwa 6), Shizuoka was overtaken by Kagoshima Prefecture in aracha output and fell to second place for the first time since records began in 1959 (Shōwa 34) (according to the Sankei Shimbun, reporting an announcement by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries: Kagoshima about 27,200 tons, Shizuoka about 25,800 tons). Behind this is a sharp rise in Kagoshima’s production of tencha (the raw material for matcha), driven by the expanding demand for matcha. Meanwhile, Shizuoka’s tea cultivation area is about 12,800 hectares, still 36.5% of the national total.</p>
<p>These numbers make the guidance of a tea-field inn more interesting. Whose tea is the tea field where the guest is staying — Shizuoka’s, Kagoshima’s, or another region’s? The story of the ranking of growing regions is a story of a tea land happening in the present tense.</p>

<h2>3. The world — simply by laying grass, it became heritage</h2>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>Shizuoka’s “chagusaba farming method” — a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System in 2013</strong> — In Shizuoka’s tea-growing areas, there are scattered mowing grounds called “chagusaba,” where the grass (chagusa) laid in tea gardens is cut. When the dried grass is spread in the tea gardens, the soil comes alive and tea with good flavor and aroma grows — as a result of continuing this traditional farming method, more than 300 species of grassland plants have been observed in the chagusaba, and rare plants such as the seven autumn flowers and the Sasayuri lily have been protected. As a case where agriculture and biodiversity faced the same direction, it was designated a Globally Important Agricultural Heritage System (GIAHS) by the FAO in 2013 (according to the Kakegawa City Tourism Association and others).</div>
<p>The essence of this case is the structure that <strong>“trying to make good tea protected the living things of the grassland as it was”</strong>. It was not a special conservation activity; the dedication to making tea protected the landscape. The story of a tea-field inn can be written in this same grammar — where does the grass in our tea field come from, and is it being laid? That one question adds a layer of living things to the tea field.</p>

<h2>4. The processes — pick, steam, roll, dry</h2>
<p>Sencha is not made by picked leaves simply turning into tea. The processes come in roughly four stages. <strong>Picking</strong> (one bud and two leaves — the bud and two young leaves pinched off with the fingers) → <strong>steaming</strong> (stopping oxidation with steam; the reason green tea is green tea) → <strong>rolling</strong> (drying the leaves while shaping them; in hand-rolling, the steps of rounding, pressing, and loosening are repeated again and again) → <strong>drying</strong>. The true joy of a tea-rolling experience is meeting <strong>the moment the aroma changes from grassy to the fragrance of tea</strong> in the course of the process. A realistic format is to start the experience from the rolling after steaming (working with a tea garden or processing plant), and the warmth at the fingertips and the heat of the pan can be fully felt even by a child’s hands.</p>

<h2>5. In practice — the timetable of a tea-field inn</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Tea picking (seasonal)</strong> — The first flush is from late April to May. With Hachijū-hachiya as a guide, these are the most brilliant two weeks of the tea field’s year. The second and third flushes follow, but for an experience, the new shoots of the first flush are by far the best.</li>
<li><strong>Hand-rolling (possible year-round)</strong> — With steamed tea leaves (even frozen ones), the experience can be offered in seasons when there is no picking.</li>
<li><strong>Tea in the guest room</strong> — A teapot and teacups in the guest room. Add one line on water temperature (hotter for deep-steamed tea, cooler for high-grade tea). The sound of pouring hot water and the rising steam are the tea field inside the room.</li>
<li><strong>A walk in the tea field</strong> — Winter pruning, spring budding, summer shading, autumn tea seeds. Even in the seasons other than picking, the tea field has its own timetable of things to see.</li>
</ul>

<h2>6. Safety and reality — tea is a living crop</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pesticide intervals</strong> — Before picking, operations follow the safety standard for the number of days between spraying and harvest. When guests pick leaves as an experience, always confirm with the tea-garden manager, “Is this a field we may pick today?”</li>
<li><strong>Caffeine</strong> — The general view is that strong tea should be kept modest for children and pregnant women. Adding one line to the guidance is enough.</li>
<li><strong>Private land</strong> — A tea field is somebody’s field. Picking only in the plots you are guided to (the same manners as in <a href="#idea-village-walk-path">Village Walking Path - Idea for Walking Map Touring Path, Waterway, Shrine in Village</a>).</li>
<li><strong>The sound of the frost fans</strong> — At inns close to tea fields, the fans turn on spring nights. The more an inn sells its quiet, the more it will want to write the explanation of this sound (the sound of protecting tea from frost) in advance.</li>
</ul>
<div class="note"><span class="tag">InnBuddy VIEW</span>The best experience of a tea-field inn is actually neither picking nor rolling but <strong>“drinking the tea of the land, made with the water of the land”</strong>. If the taste of the water differs, the taste of the tea changes even with the same leaves (this connects directly with <a href="#idea-spring-water-taste">Taste Water of Satoyama - Ideas for Making Spring, Well, Stream Water into Experience</a>). See the tea field, brew the tea, talk about the water — when these three come together, the tea-field inn is complete.</div>

In implementing, design under each inn’s own judgment, including safety, laws and regulations, and consideration for the neighborhood. When handling farmland or fields, do so on the premise of consultation with the farmer or owner.</div>
