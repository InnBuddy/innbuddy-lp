16:T21fe,<p>「うちの食事は赤字でも仕方ない——宿の本体は泊まることだから」。宿の食の話は、しばしばこの一言で終わります。しかし、この一言を分解すると、別のものが現れます。赤字が仕方ないのではない。収益が計算式で見えていないだけです。F&Bの売上は、たった3つの数字の掛け算に分解できます。分解できるものは、管理できる。管理できるものは、口座になる。この記事は、宿の食を「仕方ない赤字」から「管理できる収益の口座」に変える、計算式と3つのレバーの話です。</p><h2>計算式 — 延べ人数 × 付帯率 × 1人単価</h2><p>F&B売上=延べ人数×付帯率×1人単価。延べ人数は宿に泊まった人数、付帯率はそのうち食を利用した人の割合、1人単価は利用した1人あたりの単価です。3つの数字のどれかが動けば、売上は動きます。そして、最初に動かすのは3つのうち「最も弱い1つ」です。付帯率が低い宿で単価を上げても、土台に人数が乗ってこない。朝食だけに絞った計算(人数×単価×追加)は「朝食の収益」の記事に譲り、ここではF&B全体の構造を見ます。</p><h2>3つのレバー</h2><ol><li><strong>延べ人数 — 泊まる人以外を呼ぶ</strong> — プランの朝食込み(延べ人数そのものを増やす)、外部ゲスト(近隣の方や旅先の方のランチとディナー)、催事(宴会・会議・行事)。宿の食が泊まらない人にも開かれると、延べ人数の天井が外れます。</li><li><strong>付帯率 — 知らない人は来られない</strong> — 利用が伸びない最大の理由は、知られていないことです。客室への案内置き、チェックイン時の一言(「朝食は地元の野菜のスープが主菜です」)、メニューの見える化。告知は勧誘ではなく、情報の形で。</li><li><strong>1人単価 — 上を選べるようにする</strong> — 追加メニュー(地ビール、デザート)、セット、ランクの選択肢(通常の朝食と、少し豊かな朝食)。単価を上げるコツは、値段を吊り上げることではなく、選べる上を用意することです。</li></ol><h2>朝食の3つの設計 — 時間枠・追加・待ち時間</h2><ul><li><strong>時間枠</strong> — 7時・8時・9時の枠に分けて、混雑の山を平らにする。山はスタッフの負担になり、食の体験を下げます。</li><li><strong>追加の1品</strong> — 「デザートはいかがですか」の一言。断りやすい空気であることが、勧誘との境界線です(受付のもう一押しの記事で扱いました)。</li><li><strong>待ち時間の演出</strong> — 待ちを体験に変える(煎茶と土地の情報誌、厨房の見える席)。待ちが長いことと、暇なことは別の問題です。</li></ul><h2>コストの側 — 粗利を守るのは、仕込みと食べ残し</h2><p>売上の式の裏側には、原価の式があります。F&Bの管理で見るべきは売上の増えではなく、粗利の増えです。そして粗利を静かに削るのが食品ロスです。日本では、外食産業から年間66万トンの食品ロスが出ていると推計されています(令和5年度推計。農林水産省・環境省の推計を消費者庁が取りまとめた資料による)。レストラン・ホテル等の外食で出るロスの大宗は、食べ残しと仕込みです。</p><div 1b:T2110,<p>"Our food runs at a loss and it cannot be helped — the core of an inn is the stay." Talk of an inn's food often ends with that one line. But take the line apart, and something else appears. It is not that the loss is unavoidable; it is that the revenue is simply not visible as a formula. F&B revenue can be decomposed into the multiplication of just three numbers. What can be decomposed can be managed; what can be managed can become an account. This article is the story of the formula and the three levers that turn an inn's food from "an unavoidable loss" into "a manageable revenue account."</p>

<h2>The formula — headcount × attach rate × spend per guest</h2>

<p>F&B revenue = headcount × attach rate × spend per guest. Headcount is the number of people who stayed at the inn; attach rate is the share of them who used the food; spend per guest is the average spend of each user. If any of the three numbers moves, revenue moves. And the first one to move is "the weakest of the three." Raising the spend at an inn with a low attach rate sets nothing on the foundation. The breakfast-only calculation (guests × price × add-ons) is left to the "Breakfast Revenue" article; here we look at the structure of F&B as a whole.</p>

<h2>The three levers</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Headcount — call in people beyond the guests</strong> — Breakfast-included plans (increasing headcount itself), outside guests (lunch and dinner for neighbors and for travelers), and events (banquets, meetings, occasions). When the inn's food opens to people who are not staying, the ceiling on headcount comes off.</li>
<li><strong>Attach rate — people who do not know cannot come</strong> — The biggest reason use does not grow is that it is not known. A guide placed in the guest rooms, one line at check-in ("breakfast features a local vegetable soup as its main dish"), menus made visible. Announcing is not soliciting: it takes the form of information.</li>
<li><strong>Spend per guest — make an upper choice available</strong> — Add-on menus (local beer, dessert), sets, tiered options (the usual breakfast, and a slightly richer breakfast). The trick to raising spend is not jacking up the price; it is preparing an upper option there to choose.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Three designs for breakfast — time slots, add-ons, the wait</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Time slots</strong> — Divide into 7, 8, and 9 o'clock slots to flatten the crowd's peak. A peak burdens the staff and lowers the food experience.</li>
<li><strong>The one add-on dish</strong> — One line: "Would you like dessert?" An atmosphere easy to decline is the boundary with soliciting (as covered in the upselling article).</li>
<li><strong>Staging the wait</strong> — Turn waiting into experience (sencha tea and a local information sheet, seats with a view of the kitchen). A long wait and an idle one are different problems.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The cost side — what protects gross profit is prep and leftovers</h2>

<p>Behind the revenue formula stands a cost formula. What F&B management should watch is not the growth of revenue but the growth of gross profit. And what quietly shaves gross profit is food loss. In Japan, the restaurant industry is estimated to generate 660,000 tons of food loss a year (fiscal 2023 estimate; from a Consumer Affairs Agency compilation of Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries and Ministry of the Environment estimates). The bulk of the loss arising at restaurants and hotels is plate waste and prep waste.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>In the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries / Ministry of the Environment estimates (fiscal 2023), 660,000 tons — 29% — of the 2.31 million tons of business-sector food loss arises from the restaurant industry (per a Consumer Affairs Agency compilation)</strong> — The prep in an inn's kitchen and the leftovers on its tables are part of this national figure.</div>

<p>Here is one institution that directly concerns the inn's table. In December 2024, the Consumer Affairs Agency and the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare drew up the "Guidelines for Promoting Takeaway of Leftover Food." The scope is stated plainly — "facilities that serve food to customers (restaurants, izakaya, hotels, and the like, including meals at banquets and parties)" — and inn banquets are assumed from the start. Three pillars: (1) the great premise is finishing the food; (2) where food unavoidably remains, takeaway comes with an explanation that responsibility for managing the food afterward rests basically with the guest, and with clean containers provided; (3) an operation that excludes raw and undercooked items. The Ministry of the Environment also offers stickers for the takeaway nickname "mottECO." For banquet leftovers, the classic practices in public materials include discussing portions with the organizer before the start, and the "30-10" call — enjoy the dishes for 30 minutes after the toast, and return to your seat for the last 10.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>An inn in Hokkaido featured in the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries' collection of good practices asked guests' age groups and the like at booking, adjusted the menu to its guest layer, refined the menu from leftover tendencies, and reduced food loss by offering takeaway packs</strong> — The precision of prep is made not only in the kitchen but in the conversation at booking.</div>

<p>What is interesting about the Hokkaido inn is that the reduction of food loss begins not with "kitchen effort" but with "listening at booking." The age groups and food preferences that surface in the booking conversation raise the precision of prep. Ingredients then waste less easily, the food fits the guests, and attach rate, spend, and experience are all protected at once. Reducing loss and the three levers are not separate stories.</p>

<h2>The other side of F&B</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Soliciting to raise the attach rate</strong> — One line, in the form of information, once only. Pushy breakfast soliciting leaves a discontent bigger than the added profit.</li>
<li><strong>A menu that only raises prices</strong> — Preparing an upper choice and jacking up the price are different things. The latter lowers the attach rate of the next stay.</li>
<li><strong>Thin gross profit on events</strong> — Banquets earn headcount at a stroke, and at the same time send ingredient and labor costs soaring. Recalculate in gross profit before accepting.</li>
<li><strong>Hygiene and allergies</strong> — The more covers, the more complex the preparation for allergies becomes. Breakfast hours are also the kitchen's busiest; systems of recording and confirming are the lifeline.</li>
<li><strong>The line on takeaway</strong> — Takeaway stands only when paired with the hygiene explanation premised on guest responsibility and an operation that excludes raw items. Use the guideline's templates.</li>
<li><strong>Forcing "the experience"</strong> — Speaking of regional food is a value, but tastes and portions differ by person. An inn with a small-portion option is richer than any inn.</li>
</ul>

<p>Opening the account means writing out this month's three numbers: headcount, attach rate, spend per guest. Lay the receipts beside the guest register, and they emerge within an hour — no calculator needed. Put a hand, one hand only, on the weakest lever next month. The hour at which F&B turns from "an unavoidable loss" into "a manageable account" is that hour.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. The estimates and the guideline cited are introductions of published materials. Design food hygiene, allergy handling, and takeaway operation in line with the relevant laws and the guidance of your municipality.</div>

<h2>1、餐饮收入计算公式——总人数×附加费×人均价格</h2>
<p><strong>餐饮销售额=总人数×附加费×人均价格</strong>。如果这三件事中的任何一个发生变化，销售额就会发生变化。总人数是指入住旅馆的人数，杂费率是指使用餐饮的人数占入住人数的比例，每人价格是指每人使用餐饮的价格。餐饮销售额可以通过将三个数字相乘来计算。</p>

<h2>2. 三个杠杆——人数、杂费率、单价</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>总人数 — 计划包括早餐、外部客人和活动：</strong>增加总人数有三种方式：计划包含早餐、外来客人、特别活动。通过将早餐纳入您的计划，您可以增加可以吃早餐的总人数。通过邀请外部客人（邻居和游客）吃午餐或晚餐，您可以增加餐饮店的总人数。通过举办特别活动（活动和派对），餐饮业的总人数将会增加。</li>
<li><strong>杂费 — 有人来（客房信息、前台公告、菜单演示）：</strong>提高积分率有三种方式：客房信息、前台公告、菜单呈现。通过在客房内放置早餐和餐厅信息，客人即可知道是否提供餐饮服务。通过在前台宣布“您想吃早餐吗？”，客人会想：“我们吃早餐吧。”通过展示菜单，客人可以看到他们可以吃什么。</li>
<li><strong>每人价格 - 额外菜单/套餐/追加销售：</strong>提高每人价格的方法有三种：额外菜单、套餐和追加销售。通过提供额外的菜单项目（例如当地啤酒、甜点），客人会想：“我们再添加一项吧。”通过准备套餐（例如早餐+当地啤酒），客人会觉得套餐很划算。追加销售（例如从普通早餐到高级早餐）会增加每人的价格。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 早餐要点——时段、添加、等待时间制作</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>时间范围设计（晚间拥堵）：</strong>设计早餐时段有助于缓解拥挤状况。例如，您可以通过将活动分为三个时间段来平衡拥挤：7:00-8:00、8:00-9:00 和 9:00-10:00，并让客人选择他们的时间。当拥堵得到缓解后，员工的工作量就会减少，客人的满意度就会提高。</li>
<li><strong>“附加”设计（邀请添加一件/一件）：</strong>通过为早餐设计“额外”，您可以提高每人的价格。例如：邀请顾客添加额外的项目，例如“您想再喝一杯当地啤酒吗？”或“您想添加一份额外的甜点吗？”，每人的价格将会增加。将额外的邀请作为“推荐”而不是强行推销很重要。</li>
<li><strong>等待时间管理（这样您就不会因等待而不满意）：</strong>您可以通过管理早餐的等待时间来避免沮丧。例如，您可以通过将等待时间变成一种“体验”来减少顾客的不满，例如在等待时提供当地啤酒样品或让客人在等待时阅读当地信息杂志。</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. 成本控制——粮食损失和毛利润</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>管理食物损失（准备量）（参见食物损失文章）：</strong>餐饮成本管理最重要的方面是食物浪费管理。通过适当管理准备的食物量可以减少食物损失。减少食物浪费将降低成本率并增加毛利润。</li>
<li><strong>看“毛利润增加”而不是“销售额增加”：</strong>在管理餐饮利润时，看“毛利的增长”比“销售额的增长”更重要。即使销售额增加，如果成本增加，毛利也不会增加。提高毛利润是餐饮利润管理的目的。</li>
<li><strong>餐饮不是“成本中心”，而是“收入+体验”账户：</strong>餐饮不是“成本中心”，而是“收入+体验”账户。除了通过餐饮增加利润外，为客人提供“品尝当地风味”的体验也可以提高酒店的整体满意度。 F&B是一个既提供“收入”又提供“体验”的账户。</li>
</ul>
