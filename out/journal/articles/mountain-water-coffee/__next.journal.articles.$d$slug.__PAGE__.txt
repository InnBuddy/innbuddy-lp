11:T1cc1,<p>In this series’ brand articles, one property has appeared again and again as an example — <strong>"the property that brews its coffee in mountain water"</strong> (<a href="#branding-desired-name">What Name Do You Want to Be Called: The Entrance to a Branding Dialogue</a>, among others). This article is about its substance. Mountain water, forest air, sunlight through the leaves — a cup brewed in nature becomes an experience different from dining-room coffee. Here, we re-read that cup not as "a drink" but as <strong>"the design of a place and a time."</strong></p>

<h2>The cup that began with goats</h2>

<p>Coffee’s beginning comes down to us as a legend. In the Kaffa region of Ethiopia, the goatherd Kaldi saw his goats, having eaten red berries, grow excited and refuse to sleep. A monk, given the berries, brewed and drank them — and the drowsiness of the long night prayers flew off. Coffee, so the story is handed down, was first needed by people as <strong>"a drink that guards time"</strong> (introduced as a famous legend of Ethiopia).</p>

<p>Legend’s details aside, here lies the essence of a cup. From its beginning, coffee was not a mere indulgence but <strong>a device for properly spending a certain time</strong>. A cup brewed in mountain water stands at the same question of design — which of the guest’s hours do we guard, and deepen?</p>

<h2>The man who built a place for coffee in Japan</h2>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>On April 13, 1888 (Meiji 21), the "Kahi-chakan" coffee house opened in Shitaya, Tokyo</strong> — Opened by Tei Ei-kei, it is regarded as Japan’s first full-scale coffee shop. Coffee was 2 sen a cup. A salon-like place with billiards and newspapers, it came too early and did not last long (per the Heibonsha World Encyclopedia and similar references). The date is also the origin of "Café Day" in Japan.</div>

<p>What makes the Kahi-chakan interesting is that it sold not coffee but <strong>"the time and place for drinking coffee."</strong> What the people of Meiji Tokyo needed was not beans but a place to pass time over a foreign drink. The single seat in your property’s forest is the same thing — what the guest buys is not coffee but <strong>the fifteen minutes spent in that place</strong>.</p>

<h2>Beans and water — a cup is the meeting of "far away" and "here"</h2>

<p>Coffee beans always come from far away. The world’s coffee-growing regions are limited, and in most of Japan the plant will not grow. Precisely so, what can speak of "this land" inside a cup is not the beans but <strong>the water and the place</strong>. Brewing with mountain water means letting the beans of a distant land and the water of this land meet beneath this land’s filtered sunlight — a design in which the world and the land ride in the same cup at once.</p>

<p>The story of making the water itself an experience (drawing spring water, tasting it in vessels, confirming its safety and the regulations) is treated in detail by <a href="#idea-spring-water-taste">Taste Water of Satoyama - Ideas for Making Spring, Well, Stream Water into Experience</a>. This article narrows to the design of the cup brewed with that water.</p>

<h2>Designing a cup — 3 points</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>One line about the water</strong> — Add one line to the bottle or the menu: "brewed with the subsoil water of Mt. ○○." Water-quality testing and thorough boiling-and-cooling are the premise; the explanation needs one line. A long explanation is not read before the taste.</li>
<li><strong>One seat</strong> — In nature within a five-minute walk, make just one seat. Sunlight through leaves, the sound of the stream, a view looking down — a place with one "reason for this seat." Limit it to one couple a day, and the seat becomes a stage, not a dining room.</li>
<li><strong>The gestures</strong> — Let the guests hear the grinding of the beans. Pour the hot water, and the three minutes of blooming — making these three minutes not "waiting time" but <strong>time of fragrance</strong> is the heart of the cup’s design. For vessels, one everyday teacup is enough.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Operation — one couple a day, a special experience</h2>

<p>Offer it as the "forest coffee experience," one couple a day, with a clear experience fee (example: ¥2,000 per couple). Shift the time from breakfast (say, 9:30, after breakfast) and the kitchen stays unwearied. The photograph: mountain, nature, and the cup in one frame — the compositions that get saved are handled by <a href="#instagram-saveable-account">Designing an Instagram Account for "Saves," Not Followers</a>. On rainy days, change — do not cancel — to "a cup at a forest-view seat" by the lobby window. Lowering the place one step in, not calling the experience off, is the manner of the alternative.</p>

<h2>The other side — 5 traps of a cup</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Safety with boiling water</strong> — Outdoors, hot water travels in thermos pots. Design in, from the start, the temperature management of drawn water and the possibility of scalds.</li>
<li><strong>Serving untested water</strong> — Serve it as "mountain water," and testing for potability and heating are the premise. The line of explanation is also a line of responsibility.</li>
<li><strong>Starting with capital investment</strong> — It starts with a hand mill and one drip set. Before assembling brewing machines, fix the design of one seat and three minutes.</li>
<li><strong>Insects and bees</strong> — Outdoor seats that handle sweets attract bees. Answer with the seat’s placement (away from flowers) and lidded vessels.</li>
<li><strong>The gap with everyday coffee</strong> — If the forest cup is special, put the difference from breakfast coffee into words. If both are careless, the special thins too.</li>
</ul>

<p>A little over 1,300 years from the legend of the goats, a little over 130 from the Kahi-chakan — the cup has always been "the design of a place and a time." Tomorrow morning, walk around the property and start by finding one sunlit seat. The beans come from far away, but the design of a cup always starts at your feet.</p>
