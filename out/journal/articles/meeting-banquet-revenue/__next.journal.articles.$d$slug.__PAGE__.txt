11:T1f32,<p>Meetings and banquets are <strong>not "room rental" but "a package of rooms + meals + experience."</strong> Before giving up with "we have no meeting room," look at the weekday hours when the lounge and dining room sit empty. That empty time, and five guest rooms. A small property’s meeting revenue is built from these two. This article is the foundation of the package’s design and its sales channels.</p>

<h2>The kō’s inn — the invention of a mechanism by which groups choose where to stay</h2>

<p>In the Edo period, those who set out for the Ise pilgrimage were chiefly groups called kō — confraternities formed village by village. Members saved travel funds, and representatives chosen by lot made the pilgrimage in turn — popular group travel, in effect. And these kō produced a mechanism: <strong>the kō’s designated inn</strong>. A kō would designate trustworthy hatago inns as its "designated inns," publish them in a register, and a traveler could show the kō’s certificate at a listed inn and stay with peace of mind.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>In the Edo period, kō confraternities designated trustworthy hatago inns as their "designated inns" (sadayado) and issued registers to guide travelers. The inns, for their part, hung signboards bearing the kō’s name at their eaves, and at designated inns a discipline banning gambling and drunken rowdiness was maintained — the "Naniwa-kō" of the early 19th century is known as a confraternity that designated inns where even a solo traveler could stay without anxiety</strong> — Group travel ran on a mechanism that made "where is safe" visible. (Based on period-drama glossaries, shrine history materials, and other publicly available scholarship.)</div>

<p>What deserves attention is the inn’s posture. To secure lodgers, inns strove to become a kō’s designated inn, and welcomed guests <strong>under the kō’s name</strong>. Hanging out the name was also a guarantee of quality. A rowdy inn was dropped from the kō. The kō’s register, in other words, was <strong>a two-way mechanism of trust: groups choosing inns, and inns being chosen by name</strong>.</p>

<h2>Today’s kōchū — 3 customers</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Nearby companies’ training camps and study retreats</strong> — Today’s kōchū (pilgrim parties) are companies and local groups. Properties near mountains or sea can pick up the demand "we want to train in nature, not a city meeting room." Designing corporate regulars who come back week after week is handled by <a href="#corporate-contract-guest">Corporate Contract Guests: Designing Weekly Regulars</a>. Here, we take single training camps and retreats.</li>
<li><strong>Local groups</strong> — Agricultural co-ops, fisheries co-ops, neighborhood associations, chambers of commerce. Local groups find meaning in using local inns. A market that moves with one flyer distributed to the officers.</li>
<li><strong>Remote workers</strong> — Long stays with occasional meetings: demand where working and living blend. The background is treated in detail by <a href="#idea-workation-tourism-stay">Incorporate Workation Tourism into Inn - Stay Ideas Mixing Work, Living, Rest</a>.</li>
</ol>

<h2>What you sell — the 3-piece set</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Rooms</strong> — The participants’ lodging. A training camp of 20 fills 4 rooms. The essence of meeting revenue is filling weekday room occupancy.</li>
<li><strong>The place to meet</strong> — No meeting room needed. Half the lounge, the dining room by day. With a partition and a whiteboard, the place can be sold.</li>
<li><strong>Meals</strong> — Lunch sold not as "a box meal" but as "an experience." The region’s bento, satoyama coffee, the next brewery’s beer. With story, a ¥1,500 box meal becomes the price of an experience (the craft of adding story to a dish is the same as in <a href="#idea-breakfast-local-story">Convey Region with Breakfast - Ideas for Breakfast Putting Regional Story on One Dish</a>).</li>
</ul>

<h2>Designing the "in-between" — what only a property can sell</h2>

<p>A hotel meeting room sells only the meeting hours. A property, though, can sell <strong>the in-between of the meeting</strong>. The morning walk, fifteen minutes of outside air at noon, the bath, the evening meal. What remains in the memory of a training camp is, quite often, not the minutes but the in-between. The design of walking courses can be used as is from <a href="#idea-walk-local-15-30-60">Convey Region with Walk - Ideas for 15, 30, 60 Minute Walks Starting from Inn</a>. Add one line of the in-between — "for breaks, we will guide you to the walking path out back" — and that becomes the difference from a hotel.</p>

<h2>Price — show it as a package</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Quote per person</strong> — Rooms, meeting space, and lunch together: "from ¥8,000 per person." Itemized, it looks expensive; bundled and per person, it also passes internal approval more easily (the foundations of price are in <a href="#pricing-fundamentals">How to Set Room Rates: Pricing Fundamentals</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Weekdays only</strong> — Meetings center on weekdays. Split the pricing — weekends at normal rates, weekdays as the meeting package — and total occupancy rises.</li>
<li><strong>Include an experience</strong> — "Meeting + brewery tour," "meeting + miso-making." The moment an experience is included, the training camp becomes an experience, and satisfaction and price move together.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other side — when meetings exhaust the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Equipment competition</strong> — You can lend a projector, but beating the town’s meeting rooms on equipment is hard. The contest is not equipment but the in-between, the meals, and the staying.</li>
<li><strong>Sound and confidentiality</strong> — With other guests beyond the partition, there are limits to managing topics and noise. A room where you cannot honestly write "reserved exclusively on this day" should not be sold.</li>
<li><strong>Impact on other guests</strong> — On days the lounge is taken, prepare a place for the other lodgers first. Decide the order and the number of groups you accept.</li>
<li><strong>Corporate approval is slow</strong> — The person in charge decides, and the executives do not move. Issue the quote on the spot, with an expiry date. Cancellation terms too, in one line, before accepting.</li>
<li><strong>The stamina of preparation</strong> — Deciding "one group per day" is also strategy. Only the meetings started within what you can turn will come again next year.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is delivering one sheet to one nearby company. "A weekday meeting + a satoyama box lunch + one night." 12:T17db,<p>会议及宴会<strong>“房+餐+体验套餐”而非“租房”</strong>。即使是一家小旅馆，如果有半个休息室和五间客房，也能产生会议收入。在你放弃并说“我做不到，因为我没有会议室”之前，先看看白天开放的休息室和餐厅。休息室工作日白天开放吗？这些空闲时间就变成了会议收入。本文将整理套餐设计和销售渠道，即使在小型酒店也能通过会议和宴会产生收入。</p>

<h2>1. 卖什么——以三件套的方式思考</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>房间——参与者的住宿（以填补工作日的入住率）：</strong>会议和宴会的本质是“平日填满房间”。邻近的公司和当地组织需要过夜进行训练和训练营。对于工作日客房入住率较低的酒店来说，会议提供了填补客房的理由。即使有 5 间客房，也可以容纳 4 间房间，容纳 20 人的会议。</li>
<li><strong>会议地点 — 休息室/餐厅白天可用性：</strong>无需为会议地点创建“会议室”。一半的休息室和餐厅的白天空闲时间就够了。只需用隔断将休息室隔出一半，架起投影仪和白板，就可以把它变成一个聚会场所。靠近山、海和大自然，非常适合在会议间隙放松身心。</li>
<li><strong>膳食 — 午餐、Agri 的便当、Shibui Cafe 的咖啡：</strong>会议餐作为一种“体验”而不是“盒装午餐”出售。 Agri 的便当、当地啤酒厂的啤酒、Shibui Cafe 的咖啡。当你的食物讲述故事时，会议会更令人满意。即使午餐花费1500日元，如果你有一个故事可以讲，那也会被认为是一种“体验”。</li>
</ol>

<h2>2. 卖给谁——3 位邻居和远程工作者</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>邻近企业——培训/营地（依山傍海、亲近自然的优势）：</strong>就近企业的训练、训练营，多选择靠近山或靠海的住宿。人们需要在大自然中而不是在城市的会议室中进行培训。通过向附近的公司分发“培训/营地套餐”的信息，您可以捕捉工作日的会议需求。</li>
<li><strong>地方组织——农业合作社、渔业合作社、居委会的聚会：</strong>农业合作社、渔民合作社、居委会、商会等当地组织的聚会往往在当地的客栈举行。通过向当地组织分发“会议套餐”信息，您可以挖掘当地对会议的需求。当地组织发现使用当地住宿很有意义。</li>
<li><strong>远程工作人员——长期住宿和会议的混合体：</strong>远程工作人员在长时间停留期间偶尔会开会。通过告诉长期客人他们也可以使用会议空间，您可以增加会议收入。长期住宿和会议的混合是对会议的新需求。</li>
</ul>

<h2>3. 如何创建价格——在包装上显示价格</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>以“每人○○日元（含客房、会议、午餐）”套餐所示：</strong>如果你把房间、会议地点和午餐分开，你的客人会觉得“贵”。通过在套餐上注明“每人8000日元（包括住宿、会议和午餐）”，可以让人感觉很划算。将套餐价格显示为每人的单价非常重要。</li>
<li><strong>“仅限工作日”更低价格：</strong>会议主要在工作日举行。通过仅在工作日降低价格，您可以填写工作日的客房入住率。周末常规房价和工作日会议套餐的分离提高了整体入住率。</li>
<li><strong>包括“经验”：</strong>通过将体验纳入您的会议套餐中，您可以提高单价。示例：“会议+啤酒厂参观”“会议+味噌制作体验”。包括体验将会议从“培训”转变为“体验”并提高满意度。</li>
</ul>
