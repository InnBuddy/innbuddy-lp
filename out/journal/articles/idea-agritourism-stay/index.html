「農家に泊まる」体験は、世界的には「農家を守る政策」として育てられてきました。観光客のための仕組みではなく、農村のための仕組みだったのです。この記事は、イタリアとフランスという2つの制度の設計から、宿の体験に使える骨格を取り出す話です。

イタリア1985年 — 「農業が主業」という条件の法律

1970年代のイタリアでは、小規模農家の収益が悪化し、若者が都市へ流出していました。農家が宿泊や食事で客を取り始めると、当初は「無許可の宿泊業」として罰金を科されることもあったといいます。転機は1985年。農家の観光受け入れ（アグリツーリズモ）を定めた最初の法律が成立しました。この制度の巧妙な点は、優遇を与える代わりに条件を付けたことです。農業による収入が観光による収入を上回ること。つまり「宿をやりたい農家」ではなく「農業を続けるための客」という位置づけです。この法律は他の欧州諸国のモデルとなり、2022年時点の研究では、イタリアのアグリツーリズモは2万5千軒を超え、年間15億ユーロを超える経済規模になっています。観光が農業を食い殺さないよう、法律が食い止めたのです。

CASEイタリアは1985年、アグリツーリズモを定めた最初の法律を成立させ、「農業収入が観光収入を上回ること」を条件に農家の観光受け入れを支えた — 2万5千軒超・年間15億ユーロ超（2022年時点の研究）。「観光のために畑をやめない」という逆転の設計が制度的な成功の核である。

フランス1951年 — 空き家を守るために、客を呼びます

フランスの話はもう一つの方向から同じ問題を解きました。戦後の農村では、農業の不況で家々が荒れ、人口が流出していました。1951年、上院議員エミール・オベールがアルプスの山村に最初の「ジット・リュラル」（田舎の貸家）を開きます。荒れかけた農家の建物を休暇施設として再生し、都市の人々に貸します。1955年には全国組織「Gîtes de France」が発足し、最初のガイドブックには146軒が掲載されました。現在では6万件を超える物件を持つ、欧州最大の田舎の滞在ネットワークに成長しています。等級は麦の穂の数で示されます。フランスの発明は、「田舎の家を守るために、都会の人に貸す」という発想の転換でした。客は利益のためだけでなく、建物と村を存続させるための装置だったのです。

CASEフランスは1951年、上院議員エミール・オベールが最初の「ジット・リュラル」を開き、荒れかけた農家を休暇貸家として再生するネットワークを始めた — 1955年の全国組織発足時は146軒、現在は6万件超。「空き家を守るために客を呼ぶ」発見が、今日の田舎滞在の原型である。

日本の歩み — 余暇法から「農泊」へ

日本では1994年、農山漁村余暇法が最初の一歩となり、欧州の農村滞在を見込んだ「グリーン・ツーリズム」の呼び名で交流が進められました。その後、農林水産省は「農泊」（農山漁村滞在型旅行）の育成を掲げ、2023年度までに656の農泊地域を創出して目標を達成し、施策は成長期へ移行したとされます。日本型の特徴は、宿泊そのものより「農業体験と人との交流」が中心であること。畑仕事を一緒にし、食卓を囲みます。イタリアの「農業が主業」の条件や、フランスの「家を守る貸し方」の設計と並べると、日本の農泊が何を重んじてきたかが見えてきます。

宿で使える、農の時間割

  • 「今日の畑」を一行で — 農家の時間割は季節と天気で毎日変わります。「今朝、トマトの摘心をしました」「今日はじゃがいもの収穫日です」。玄関に一行出すだけで、農の営みは見学になります。
  • 初物を朝食へ — 畑の最初の収穫を朝食に回しましょう。物語ではなく事実が朝食を輝かせます（朝食で地域を伝えるの記事の方法です）。
  • 農具と種に履歴を一行で — 「この鍬は〇〇で使われていたものです」「この豆の種は、うちで20年続いている種です」。道具と種は、畑の最も長い記憶です。
  • 「手伝える日」と「見るだけの日」を正直に — 農繁期は手伝いが迷惑になることもあります。今日は見るだけ、の日も案内に書きましょう。正直さは農家の信用です。
  • 子どもの畑当番 — 水やりや収穫の一作業を「当番」として任せましょう。作業は一日で終わりますが、任せられた記憶は残ります。

農業を観光にする責任

  • 農機械と薬剤 — トラクタの動く畑、農薬や肥料の散布区域への立入は、事故と健康のリスクです。境界を示し、散布日は案内から外しましょう。
  • 動物との接触 — 家畜に触れた後の手洗いは必須。免疫力の低い子どもや高齢者には特に。動物の餌やりは飼い主の指示で。
  • 暑さと体力 — 夏の畑仕事は本物の重労働です。体験の時間・水分・日陰の確保を最初から設計します。
  • 衛生と保険 — 収穫物の持ち帰りや食事の提供には衛生管理が伴い、体験の受入には保険の整備が要ります。
  • 農家の本音 — 体験を「安い労働力」として設計しないようにしましょう。体験は教える行為であり、農家の手間を上回る対価（金銭ではなく感謝と学びでも）で成立します。

The experience of “staying at a farm” has, around the world, been nurtured as “a policy for protecting farms.” It was not a system for tourists but a system for rural communities. This article draws out a framework usable for inn experiences from the designs of two systems, in Italy and France.

Italy, 1985 — a law with the condition that “farming is the main business”

In 1970s Italy, the earnings of small farms were deteriorating, and young people were leaving for the cities. When farmers began taking in guests for lodging and meals, they were at first sometimes fined for “unlicensed lodging business.” The turning point came in 1985, when the first law setting out farms’ reception of tourists (agriturismo) was passed. The clever point of this system is that it attached conditions in exchange for granting preferential treatment: income from farming must exceed income from tourism. In other words, its positioning is not “farmers who want to run an inn” but “guests for the sake of continuing to farm.” This law became a model for other European countries, and according to research as of 2022, Italy’s agriturismi number more than 25,000, with an economic scale of more than 1.5 billion euros a year. The law stopped tourism from devouring farming.

CASEIn 1985, Italy passed its first law setting out agriturismo, supporting farms’ reception of tourists on the condition that “farming income exceeds tourism income” — More than 25,000 farms and more than 1.5 billion euros a year (research as of 2022). The reversed design of “not giving up the fields for the sake of tourism” is the core of its institutional success.

France, 1951 — inviting guests to protect empty houses

The French story solved the same problem from another direction. In postwar rural areas, houses were falling into disrepair due to the agricultural slump, and the population was leaving. In 1951, Senator Émile Aubert opened the first “gîte rural” (country rental house) in an Alpine mountain village. Restoring a decaying farm building as a holiday facility and renting it to city people. In 1955, the national organization “Gîtes de France” was launched, and its first guidebook listed 146 properties. Today it has grown into Europe’s largest network of countryside stays, with more than 60,000 properties. Grades are shown by the number of ears of wheat. France’s invention was the shift in thinking of “renting to city people in order to protect country houses.” Guests were not only for profit but a device for keeping buildings and villages alive.

CASEIn 1951, French Senator Émile Aubert opened the first “gîte rural” and began a network that restores decaying farmhouses as holiday rentals — 146 properties when the national organization was launched in 1955, more than 60,000 today. The discovery of “inviting guests to protect empty houses” is the prototype of today’s countryside stays.

Japan’s path — from the Rural Vacation Act to “nōhaku”

In Japan, the 1994 Act on the Promotion of Rural Vacations in Agricultural, Mountain, and Fishing Villages was the first step, and exchange was promoted under the name “green tourism,” with European rural stays in view. Since then, the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries has made fostering “nōhaku” (stay-based travel in agricultural, mountain, and fishing villages) a goal, and by fiscal 2023 it had created 656 nōhaku areas, achieving its target, and the policy is said to have moved into a growth phase. The distinctive feature of the Japanese model is that the center is not lodging itself but “farm experiences and exchange with people.” Doing fieldwork together, gathering around the table. Set alongside Italy’s condition that “farming is the main business” and France’s design of “renting to protect the house,” what Japan’s nōhaku has valued comes into view.

A farming timetable an inn can use

  • “Today in the field” in one line — A farm’s timetable changes every day with the season and the weather. “This morning we pinched the tomato tips.” “Today is potato harvest day.” Just putting one line at the entrance turns the work of farming into something to watch.
  • The first harvest to breakfast — Send the field’s first harvest to breakfast. Not a story but a fact makes breakfast shine (the method of the article “Convey Region with Breakfast - Ideas for Breakfast Putting Regional Story on One Dish”).
  • A line of history for farm tools and seeds — “This hoe was used in such-and-such place.” “The seeds for these beans have been kept going at our farm for 20 years.” Tools and seeds are the field’s longest memory.
  • Be honest about “days you can help” and “days to just watch” — In the busy farming season, help can sometimes be a nuisance. Write “today is a watching-only day” in the guidance too. Honesty is a farmer’s credit.
  • A children’s field duty — Entrust one task, such as watering or harvesting, as a “duty.” The work ends in a day, but the memory of being trusted remains.

The responsibility of turning farming into tourism

  • Farm machinery and chemicals — Entering fields where tractors are moving, or areas where pesticides and fertilizers are being applied, carries risks of accidents and to health. Mark the boundaries, and leave spraying days out of the guidance.
  • Contact with animals — Hand-washing after touching livestock is essential, especially for children and elderly people with weaker immunity. Feed animals only on the owner’s instructions.
  • Heat and stamina — Summer fieldwork is genuinely hard labor. Design the length of the experience, hydration, and shade from the start.
  • Hygiene and insurance — Taking produce home or serving meals involves hygiene management, and receiving guests for experiences requires insurance to be in place.
  • The farmer’s true feelings — Do not design the experience as “cheap labor.” An experience is an act of teaching, and it works only with a return that exceeds the farmer’s effort (which can be gratitude and learning rather than money).

InnBuddy VIEW

Italy protected farming from tourism with the condition that “farming is the main business,” and France protected the countryside with the shift of “renting to protect the house.” What the two systems have in common is not experience but “the design of continuity.” Start small, by putting one line about today in the field at the entrance. The work of farming becomes an inn experience the moment it is guided.

This article is intended for sharing ideas. Receive guests for farm experiences after putting safety, hygiene, and insurance in place and confirming the division of roles with farmers and the community.
