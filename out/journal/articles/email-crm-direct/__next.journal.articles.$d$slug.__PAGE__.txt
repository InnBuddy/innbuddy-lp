11:T1c33,<p>On the morning of checkout, the tie between inn and guest is severed once. The booking site finishes its role the moment the reservation is made, and prepares no path back for the person who thinks "I would like to return." Social media can only wait to appear by chance on a timeline. Within all that, there is exactly one channel that reaches the people who have stayed — with no advertising cost, no commission, and certainty: email. But this tool has manners defined by law, and sending without knowing them can become a problem not only of trust but of penalties. We begin with the law.</p>

<h2>Before you send — the Specified Electronic Mail Act</h2>

<p>The law regulating email that contains advertising and publicity — the Act on the Regulation of Transmission of Specified Electronic Mail, known in short as the Specified Electronic Mail Act — was enacted in 2002, when spam had become a social problem. It began as a "do not send to those who ask you not to" system, but the December 2008 revision reversed the policy: email may be sent only to those who have given prior consent — the shift to an opt-in regime. Since then, advertising email has been not "stop when refused" but "send only to those who have agreed."</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>The Specified Electronic Mail Act (enacted 2002, revised December 2008) makes prior consent (opt-in) the principle for advertising email, requires keeping records of that consent, and mandates display of the sender's name and address and of the notice that reception can be refused, together with the address for such requests</strong> — The line "to stop these emails, click here" is not a courtesy but a statutory display duty.</div>

<p>The penalties for violation are also worth knowing. Sending with falsified sender information, or defying an administrative order, carries up to one year of imprisonment or a fine of up to ¥1 million; corporations can be fined up to ¥30 million. An inn's newsletter and SMS to mobile phones alike fall under this law. That said, how an inn takes consent can be plain: on the form at booking or check-in, a field for the email address, one sentence — "We will use it to send news from the inn; you can stop it at any time" — and a checkbox. Then keep the record. That, more or less, is all the law asks.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Violations of the Specified Electronic Mail Act (falsified sender information, defiance of administrative orders, and the like) carry up to one year of imprisonment or a fine of up to ¥1 million, and corporations can be fined up to ¥30 million (the Act's penalty provisions; also applied to SMS)</strong> — The one email an inn sends is not outside the law's scope.</div>

<h2>The three emails to send — before arrival, after checkout, and the next season</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>The pre-arrival email — reducing anxiety</strong> — Access, what to bring, one line on the day's weather. The night before a trip, people close their eyes on anxiety. This email's role is not to sell but to reduce anxiety. Check-in goes smoothly, and even the first impression on arrival improves as a result.</li>
<li><strong>The post-checkout email — thanks, and a request for a review</strong> — On the night they get home, or the next morning. One line asking their impressions, and one line: "if you are willing, a review." Why reviews become an inn's asset is left to the review-management article.</li>
<li><strong>The next-season email — a seasonal letter</strong> — "The mountain snow has begun to melt." "The season of fireflies is coming." Written not as a selling sentence but as news of the land in that season. An inn is one of the places remembered as "I would like to go back"; the seasonal letter is a single call placed to that memory.</li>
</ol>

<h2>CRM — records turn into "the one line that fits the guest"</h2>

<p>The foundation supporting the three emails is the record of customer information — CRM. Three things are enough to record: stay history (when, on which plan), requests (pillow, allergies, room preferences), and anniversaries. The use of these records is not a mailing list; it is material for making "the one line that fits the guest." "You were here with your little one last time." "You mentioned the pillow did not suit you, so we have prepared a replacement this time." When that one line rides on a broadcast email, the email becomes a letter. The law of handling data — stating the purpose of use, consent, designing deletion — was covered in the guest-data basics article; email is a tool on that same line.</p>

<h2>The other side of email</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Designing not to send</strong> — The meaning of "at most once a month" is not the number you may send but the decision of what not to send. In the months you did not send, the next seasonal letter ripens.</li>
<li><strong>The form of consent</strong> — Consent buried in the fine print of a form cannot be called obtained. It must be a form in which the purpose and the stopping method can be read, and checked.</li>
<li><strong>Execute stops immediately</strong> — If a stop request arrives, remove the address that day. Nothing cuts trust in an inn faster than email that will not stop.</li>
<li><strong>A human reads the replies</strong> — Do not make the sender address send-only. Someone will always reply with a question. Email becomes a corridor only when there is a window that reads what comes back.</li>
<li><strong>Do not remember too much</strong> — Use records only for topics the guest enjoyed discussing. The discomfort of being over-remembered is the same craft of the inn covered in the data article.</li>
<li><strong>How to read the numbers</strong> — Open rates and click rates can inform improvement, but they are not a measure of a letter's quality. Whether it arrived is read by whether a reply comes, and by whether a feeling is written in the notes of the next booking.</li>
</ul>

<p>The letter you did not write will never be delivered. Start small: draft next season's letter in just three lines — mountain news, sea news, festival news. The mailing list is already asleep inside the guest register and the booking records.</p>

<h2>1. 为什么要发电子邮件？以零成本提供的“退货”联系点</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>以零成本交付给“过夜的人”：</strong>电子邮件将以零成本发送给曾经入住过该酒店的人。没有广告费用，也没有OTA费用。住过那里的人更有可能打开您的电子邮件，因为他们了解这家旅馆。零成本发送的电子邮件是最强大的直接联系方式。</li>
<li><strong>通过 OTA 无法到达的“再次”联系点：</strong>OTA 不允许在退房后与客人联系。 OTA是一个“预订”平台，不提供“退货”联系点。电子邮件允许您创建无法通过 OTA 联系的“返回”联系点。通过在结帐后通过电子邮件创建“退货”联系人，您可以增加直接预订。</li>
<li><strong>您可以积累材料来创造“适合客人的词”：</strong>通过发送电子邮件，您可以看到客人的反应（打开、点击、回复）。通过记录客人的反应，你可以积累素材，创造出“适合客人的词”。例如，你可以创造出适合客人的短语，比如“上次是和孩子在一起，这次做情侣怎么样？”</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. 发送三件事——抵达前、结帐后和下个季节。</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>抵达前——访问、信息、期望（减少焦虑）：</strong>到达前的电子邮件传达访问权限、信息和期望。发送诸如“如何访问...”、“要带什么...”和“我期待明天”等电子邮件，以减少客人的焦虑并提高他们的期望。抵达前电子邮件可减少客人的焦虑，使办理入住手续更加顺利。</li>
<li><strong>结账后——谢谢+评论请求（见小经验文章）：</strong>结帐后电子邮件将表示感谢并请求审核。发送一封电子邮件表达您的谢意并请求评论，例如“感谢您抽出时间”或“如果您能写评论，我会很高兴。”您可以在结帐后通过电子邮件请求评论来增加评论数量。</li>
<li><strong>下一季——季节性活动（参见季节性文章）：</strong>下一封季节性电子邮件将传达季节性活动。我们发送传达下一季吸引力的电子邮件，例如“在春天，您可以享受......”或“在夏天，我们推荐......”。您可以通过发送有关下一季的电子邮件让您的客人想要再次回来，从而增加直接预订。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. CRM基础——记录什么和“适合客人的话”</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>要记录的事情 - 住宿历史、请求、周年纪念日（生日、周年纪念日）：</strong>CRM记录的是您的入住历史（入住时间）、请求（枕头请求、过敏等）和周年纪念日（生日、周年纪念日）。通过记录住宿历史、请求和周年纪念日，您可以创建适合客人的消息。</li>
<li><strong>创建“适合客人的词”的材料：</strong>利用 CRM 中记录的入住历史记录、请求和周年纪念日，我们创建“适合客人的词语”。例如：“上次你和孩子在一起，这次作为情侣怎么样？”“上次你说你的枕头不合适，但这次我们为你准备了一个新枕头。”通过创造适合客人的短语，他们会感觉“你还记得我。”</li>
<li><strong>“适合客人的一句话”就像一封信：</strong>CRM的目的不是一次性发送所有消息，而是向每位客人发送信件。大量电子邮件会让客人觉得他们不适合您。通过向每位客人发送看起来像信件的电子邮件，客人会觉得这封电子邮件是写给他们的。</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. 规则——每月一次/个性化/取消方式</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>每月最多一次（不足以发送）：</strong>电子邮件的频率将限制为每月一次。如果您每月发送一次以上，您的客人会觉得很麻烦。通过将其限制为每月一次，客人将收到一封包含“必要信息”的电子邮件。</li>
<li><strong>个性化（姓名/之前的入住意见）：</strong>电子邮件将进行个性化处理（姓名、之前入住的评论）。通过包含他们的姓名和上次入住的反馈，例如“谢谢○○先生上次的光临”，客人会觉得这封电子邮件是写给他们的。</li>
<li><strong>请务必显示取消方式：</strong>我们将始终在电子邮件中提供有关如何取消的说明。通过向他们展示如何取消订阅，例如“单击此处取消订阅”，客人可以放心，他们可以随时取消订阅。出示取消方式也是一项法律义务。</li>
</ul>

<h2>行动清单</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>第 1 周：创建 3 个模板</strong> — 为三封电子邮件创建模板：到货前、结账后和下一季。包括访问、信息、期望、感谢+审核请求和季节性活动。</li>
<li><strong>第二周：定义记录项目（历史、请求、周年纪念日）</strong> — 定义要在 CRM 中记录的项目（住宿历史记录、请求、周年纪念日）。通过决定要记录的项目，您可以积累材料来创造“适合客人的单词”。</li>
<li><strong>第 3 周：结账后开始发送电子邮件</strong> — 从结帐后电子邮件开始（谢谢 + 审核请求）。通过结账后电子邮件增加评论数量。</li>
<li><strong>第 4 周：开始发送抵达前电子邮件和下一个季节性电子邮件</strong> — 开始发送抵达前电子邮件（访问、信息、期望）和下一季的电子邮件（季节性活动）。通过三封电子邮件，直接“再次”机制被激活。</li>
<li><strong>延续：每月最多一次/个性化/取消方法</strong> — 通过将电子邮件的发送频率限制为每月一次、对其进行个性化设置并提供取消订阅的方式，电子邮件变得更像是一封信，而不是令人讨厌的东西。每月记录一次直接预订和重复率，并持续改进。</li>
