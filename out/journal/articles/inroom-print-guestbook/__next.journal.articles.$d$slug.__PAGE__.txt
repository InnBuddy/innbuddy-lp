11:T1bec,<p>In-room print and the guest book are <strong>not "paper" but "media design."</strong> Every sheet of paper a property hands its guests has a role. Guidance reduces anxiety; a map makes tomorrow; a menu turns tonight into anticipation. And the guest book —. This article is the foundation for designing the room’s paper as media (its aspect as the property’s record ledger is handled by <a href="#guest-data-basics">Guest Data Basics: What to Record, and How to Use It</a>. Here: the paper guests "read" and "write")</p>

<h2>Ema — write, leave, and the next person reads</h2>

<p>At shrines hang small wooden tablets bearing written wishes: the <strong>ema</strong>. The custom of offering live horses is said to have gradually given way to tablets painted with horses’ pictures. People write a wish, hang it up, and someone reads it. Temple and shrine grounds were <strong>one of Japan’s oldest media — a place where private words were left in public</strong>.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>The ema is a small tablet dedicated at shrines and temples, bearing wishes or gratitude. It is said to derive from the offering of live horses, later replaced by boards painted with horses (traced to Nara-period records). Dedicated ema are hung in a corner of the shrine hall, where the next visitor’s eyes fall on them</strong> — The three beats — "write, leave, be read" — had been turning in the grounds for hundreds of years. (The origin of ema, from live horses to painted boards, is introduced as lore of shrine and temple culture, with "is said to" wording.)</div>

<p>What makes ema interesting is that <strong>the wish is private, yet the moment it is hung it becomes the place’s</strong>. Beside someone’s "safety of the household" hangs a stranger’s "success in the exams." The reader is, for some reason, moved by wishes unrelated to their own. The role the guest book plays inside a property is exactly this.</p>

<h2>The guest book — letters from guest to next guest</h2>

<p>Reviews are words aimed outward. Posted on OTA sites, read by people who do not know the property (the territory of <a href="#review-management-3-numbers">Review Management: The Basics of Score, Count, and Recency</a>). The guest book, by contrast, is <strong>words that stay in the property</strong>. A guest who stayed writes; the guest who stays next reads. Three points of design:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Placement</strong> — The desk in the room, or the corner of the dining room. A spot that catches the eye but where the writer feels no shyness. Do not forget the pen (a guest book without a pen is just a notebook).</li>
<li><strong>The first page</strong> — A notebook no one has written in is hard to write in. On the first page, write one line from the host: "Please leave the moments you loved at this property." The same as hanging the first wish on the ema rack.</li>
<li><strong>The property’s reply</strong> — Add a small reply beside the words written. The guest book is not a storehouse of guest voices but <strong>strata of dialogue between property and guest</strong>. Readers, from a notebook with replies, read this property’s mood correctly.</li>
</ul>

<h2>In-room print — the roles of 4 kinds</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Guidance (reduces anxiety)</strong> — Check-out time, the place of breakfast, Wi-Fi connection, emergency contacts. Paper that spares the asking is, by itself, thoughtfulness.</li>
<li><strong>Map (makes tomorrow)</strong> — Walking courses, shops’ closing days. How to make 15/30/60-minute picture maps based at the property is handled by <a href="#idea-walk-local-15-30-60">Convey Region with Walk - Ideas for 15, 30, 60 Minute Walks Starting from Inn</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Menu (turns tonight and tomorrow morning into anticipation)</strong> — One sheet for dinner and breakfast. With the items, one line for the star of the meal (the craft of adding story to a dish is in <a href="#idea-breakfast-local-story">Convey Region with Breakfast - Ideas for Breakfast Putting Regional Story on One Dish</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Memo pad and letter paper (leaves room to write)</strong> — Paper for the guest to write something on. What one wants to write mid-journey is more than expected. Leaving room to write is the in-room version of the ema spirit — <strong>the readiness to receive words</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<p>Three manners of design: <strong>consolidate to one sheet, keep margin, include one photograph.</strong> Past four kinds of print, it gets discarded before being read. Before adding information, first ask whether it can be merged into one sheet.</p>

<h2>The other side — when paper exhausts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Runaway paper</strong> — Seasonal events, discount vouchers, notices, warnings… When the desk is buried in paper, even the important sheet goes unread. One sheet on the desk is the ceiling.</li>
<li><strong>Freshness of information</strong> — No paper cuts trust like "last year’s guide." Once a month, review even just the numbers and closing days.</li>
<li><strong>The temptation of QR-only</strong> — Abolish paper for QR codes, and a new inconvenience is born: smartphone fumbling at midnight. Paper is the medium whose battery never dies. Use both: that is the rule.</li>
<li><strong>Compelling writing</strong> — Repeat "please do write" at check-out, and the ema becomes a survey. A medium that works only because writing and reading both belong to freedom.</li>
<li><strong>Personal information written in</strong> — Avoid designs that have guests write addresses or phone numbers in the guest book. Keep it a place where only words remain.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is clearing the desk in the room. Merge four kinds into one sheet, keep the margins, add one photograph. And on the guest book’s first page, write your one line. Paper is silent — but only when read does it become the property’s voice: <strong>delivering someone’s words to the next someone</strong>. What the ema in the shrine grounds have been doing all along, redone on the small paper of a guest room. That is the design of paper media.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. The origin of ema (from the offering of live horses to boards painted with horses) is introduced with the wording "is said to," as lore of shrine and temple culture. Please design guest book operation on the premise of not asking for personal information.</div>

12:T1795,<p>客房内的印刷品和留言簿<strong>“媒体设计”代替“纸张”</strong>。许多旅馆将印刷品视为“纸张”，并认为暂时将其放在那里即可。然而，从不同的角度来看，印刷品也是一种“媒介”。媒介有一个“角色”。留言簿是讲述客栈故事的媒介，店内导游是减少客人焦虑的媒介，Wi-Fi导游是增加客人便利性的媒介。本文将解释如何将客房内的印刷材料和留言簿设计为媒体设计而不是纸张。</p>

<h2>1.印刷品的作用——三种媒体</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>留言簿——讲述旅馆故事的媒介：</strong>留言簿是讲述客栈故事的媒介。留言簿讲述了旅馆的故事，包括其历史、当地景点和员工推荐。留言簿是客人了解旅馆的一个媒介。有故事，才能让客人爱上客栈。</li>
<li><strong>设施信息——减少客人焦虑的媒体：</strong>内部信息是减少客人焦虑的媒介。内部信息将告知客人他们可能关心的任何问题，例如入住和退房时间、早餐时间和地点、Wi-Fi 连接方式以及紧急联系信息。设施内有指导，客人会觉得可以放心地度过时光。</li>
<li><strong>Wi-Fi 指南/周边信息 — 增加便利性的媒体：</strong>Wi-Fi 指南和周边信息（附近的咖啡馆、餐馆、旅游景点等）是增加客人便利性的媒体。我们提供Wi-Fi连接方法、附近咖啡馆和餐馆的信息以及旅游景点的信息等让客人更加方便的信息。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. 设计三要点——一页、空白、照片</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>合并为一张纸：</strong>将印刷材料放在一页上非常重要。如果印刷页数太多，客人会觉得阅读起来很乏味。将设施指南、Wi-Fi指南以及周边信息集中在一页上，让客人感觉只需阅读一页即可了解所有内容。我们将准备一本单独的留言簿。</li>
<li><strong>提供边距：</strong>印刷材料中留有边距非常重要。客人发现印刷材料中充斥着难以阅读的文字。添加空白可以使印刷品更具可读性。页边距是“设计”的一部分，有页边距可以使印刷材料看起来“时尚”。</li>
<li><strong>插入照片：</strong>通过在印刷材料中包含照片，客人会发现更容易在视觉上理解。旅馆指南包括旅馆内部的照片，周边信息包括附近咖啡馆和旅游景点的照片，留言簿包括旅馆的历史和当地景点的照片。有了照片，印刷品就变成了可供观看的东西，而不是可供阅读的东西。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 运营——每月一次回顾和客人反馈</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>每月审查一次印刷材料：</strong>印刷材料每月审核一次。通过检查信息是否过时或者是否有客人经常询问的项目来改进印刷材料。在审查印刷材料时，反映客人的声音很重要。</li>
<li><strong>反映嘉宾心声：</strong>如果您的客人经常问“我不知道如何连接 Wi-Fi”或“附近的咖啡馆在哪里？”等问题，请将该信息添加到您的印刷材料中。通过反映客人的声音，印刷材料成为“减少客人焦虑”的媒介。</li>
<li><strong>印刷品不是“纸”而是“媒体设计”：</strong>印刷材料不是“纸”，而是“媒体设计”。每种媒介都有自己的角色，通过根据该角色进行设计，印刷材料可以成为“提高客人满意度”的工具。通过将它们合并到一页中，添加空白区域并包含照片，印刷材料变成了可以看到的东西，而不是可以阅读的东西。</li>
</ul>

<h2>行动清单</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>第一周：收集所有当前的印刷材料并组织角色。</strong> — 收集客房内现有的所有印刷材料，并将其整理成三种用途：留言簿（故事）、内部信息（减少焦虑）和 Wi-Fi 信息/环境信息（增加便利性）。</li>
<li><strong>第 2 周：合并为一页，留出一些空间，并添加照片</strong> — 将设施指南、Wi-Fi 指南和周边信息合并在一页上，留出一些空间，并附上照片。创建了一本单独的留言簿作为讲述这个故事的媒介。</li>
<li><strong>第三周：安装在客房并观察客人的反应</strong> — 将印刷材料放置在客房中并查看客人的反应。检查客人是否说“容易理解”和“美丽”之类的话。</li>
<li><strong>第四周：创建每月一次的回顾系统</strong> — 创建了每月审查一次印刷材料的系统。在印刷材料中添加了客人经常询问的项目并更新了过时的信息。</li>
<li><strong>继续：改进印刷材料作为媒体设计</strong> — 将印刷材料视为“媒体设计”而不是“纸张”，不断改进印刷材料。通过反映客人的声音并根据他们的角色进行设计来提高客人满意度。</li>
