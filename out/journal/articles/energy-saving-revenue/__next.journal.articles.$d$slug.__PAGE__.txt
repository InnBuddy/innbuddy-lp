11:T1de0,<p>Try rereading the electricity bill that arrives every month as one more profit-and-loss statement. Cutting the bill by ¥10,000 is the same profit as raising sales by ¥10,000 — and unlike sales, no OTA commission comes off it. No cleaning required, no inventory, no extra guest service. ¥10,000 in sales would mean two bookings at ¥5,000 per guest; a ¥10,000 cut in the electricity bill arrives through mechanism alone. That is the idea of a "revenue account." But an account does not grow merely by being opened. First, two numbers that set this account's interest rate.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>According to a survey by the Energy Conservation Center, Japan, easing the air-conditioner's set temperature by 1°C can cut power consumption by about 13% in cooling and about 10% in heating</strong> — A temperature setting is not a tool of endurance but a component of a mechanism whose savings can be read in advance.</div>

<p>The other number is the origin of "28 degrees." The Ministry of the Environment's Cool Biz guideline of 28°C room temperature in cooling (20°C in heating under Warm Biz) comes from the ceiling of the room-temperature range (17–28°C) set by the Order for Enforcement of the Act on Maintenance of Sanitation in Buildings and the office health standards regulations under the Industrial Safety and Health Act. In other words, 28 degrees is not a number of aesthetic endurance but the ceiling line of a statutory sanitation standard. When setting the temperature of staff rooms and offices, whether you can explain it as "the upper limit within the law" makes a difference in persuasiveness.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>The 28°C the Ministry of the Environment gives as a cooling-season guideline rests on the ceiling of the room-temperature range (17–28°C) defined by the enforcement order of the Building Sanitation Act and the office health standards regulations (the heating-season guideline is 20°C)</strong> — "28 degrees" is not a number of endurance but the ceiling line of a sanitation standard.</div>

<h2>The basics of measuring — record, convert per room, watch the seasonal peaks</h2>

<p>To grow the account, first know the balance. (1) Record the monthly electricity charge from the last three bills. (2) Divide by the number of rooms to get "utilities per room" (¥100,000 a month over 10 rooms is ¥10,000 per room) — this lets you manage the number free of occupancy effects. (3) See how high the summer and winter peaks rise. If the peaks are climate control, the hands of reduction come down to three places.</p>

<h2>The three places to cut — lighting, climate, hot water</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Lighting — motion sensors plus a timetable</strong> — Corridor, toilet, and lounge lighting at night. A two-layer setup: switching to motion sensors, and a timetable that ends "always on after 10 p.m." Lighting is the easiest place to start, and the place where the effect is visible every night.</li>
<li><strong>Climate — set-temperature rules and a card for the guest rooms</strong> — Decide set-temperature rules for shared spaces and offices (reference: cooling 26–28°C, heating 20°C). In each guest room, one card stating the vacant-room setting and a guideline for while it is occupied. Because the numbers — 13% per degree in cooling, 10% in heating — can be read, the card can be written not as "a request to endure" but as "the inn's way of thinking."</li>
<li><strong>Hot water — making usage visible</strong> — One card in the bathroom: a shower uses roughly 10 liters of hot water per minute. Hot water feeds straight into the gas bill; precisely because it is less visible than climate control, a display alone changes awareness.</li>
</ul>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">SIMULATION</span><strong>Suppose a 10-room inn with a total monthly electricity bill of ¥200,000 cuts 10% through the lighting timetable and set-temperature management: that is equivalent to ¥20,000 a month, ¥240,000 a year, in added profit (a reference value; results vary greatly with equipment, occupancy, and contracts)</strong> — Converted to sales, that is 24 stays at ¥10,000 per guest. To measure the effect, record the three months before and after the change the same way, and compare.</div>

<h2>Running it with the staff — visibility and "versus last month"</h2>

<p>"Please save electricity" does not last. What lasts is visibility and sharing the result. (1) Post the per-room electricity cost every month where the staff can see it. (2) Share one line at the morning meeting: how much it fell versus last month. (3) Decide in advance what the savings will fund (an upgrade to supplies, a partial return to local activities). Endurance does not last; achievement does. At an inn where the amount saved can be given a name, energy saving becomes one of the staff's jobs.</p>

<h2>The other side of saving — cutting comfort defeats the purpose</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The temperature of an occupied room belongs to the guest</strong> — The mechanism's targets are vacant rooms and shared spaces. Leaving the setting to the guest during the stay is the great premise. The temperature card, too, is written not as "an enforced setting" but as "an explanation of the inn's thinking."</li>
<li><strong>"28 degrees" is not the one right answer</strong> — The Ministry of the Environment itself notes that 28 is a guideline to be read against outdoor temperature, humidity, and physical condition. For stays with small children or the elderly, stay flexible. 28 is a ceiling line, not a certificate of comfort.</li>
<li><strong>An inn made too dark</strong> — Mix up cutting lighting with degrading the quality of light, and you lose security, photographs, and atmosphere at once. What you reduce is "light in places where no one is." The design of light itself is a separate story.</li>
<li><strong>Equipment investment starts from your own measurements</strong> — For LED replacement or high-efficiency water heaters, calculate the payback period from your inn's own records, not from the catalog's savings figures. Measure first: that is the order of investment.</li>
<li><strong>Don't advertise saving you aren't doing</strong> — Tell the story of "an environmentally considerate inn" with the real names of the reductions you implement (the lighting timetable, the set-temperature rules). A blank signboard eventually stops working.</li>
</ul>

<p>The electricity bill is one of the few pieces of paper that arrives every month at an inn with room for improvement written on it. Start small: print this month's bill, and pin up three months side by side. The account's interest rate — 13% and 10% per degree — is already written in this article.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. The reduction rates cited are from a survey by the Energy Conservation Center, Japan, and the calculations are reference values for understanding; actual results vary with equipment, occupancy, and contract terms.</div>

<h2>1. 为什么是“利润账户”——减少就是利润</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>减少=利润。可视化的减少带来可管理的利润：</strong>销售额增加一万日元很难，但电费减少一万日元是可能的。更重要的是，由于没有 OTA 费用（15-20%），电费的降低比 10,000 日元的销售更有利可图。节能是一个应该被视为“利润”而不是“销售额”的账户。</li>
<li><strong>“省”是精神，“利”是机制：</strong>即使你热情地说“请节约用电”，也不会长久。它需要结构化。管理运动传感器、时间表和温度设置。通过创建一个系统，可以在不增加员工负担的情况下减轻其负担。节能是通过“机制”而不是“精神”来实现的。</li>
<li><strong>它也可以是一个“体现对该地区的考虑”的故事：</strong>节约能源不仅仅是减少能源消耗，还包括对地区的考虑（参见可持续管理文章）。通过告诉客人“为了保护当地的自然，我们的照明配备了运动传感器”，客人就会感受到这是一家环保旅馆。这是一个具有成本效益的帐户，既能创造收入，又能创造故事。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. 测量基础——没有记录就无法管理</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>记录您每月的电费单：</strong>首先，记录您最近三个月的电费账单。收集电费并记录每月的电费。通过记录，您可以看到堆积如山的电费（使用供暖和制冷的季节）。</li>
<li><strong>按房间换算（除以房间数量）：</strong>将电费除以房间数即可计算每个房间的电费。例：每月电费为10万日元，如果旅馆有10间客房，则每间客房为1万日元。通过按房间换算，您可以在不受入住率影响的情况下管理电费。</li>
<li><strong>查看季节性山脉（空调）：</strong>看看季节性的电费账单。如果您的电费在夏季和冬季增加，可能是由于供暖和制冷所致。您可以通过检查空调的温度设置和使用时间来减少电费。通过观察季节性山脉，您可以看到减少的点。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 三大减量——照明、空调、热水</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>照明 — 运动传感器 + 时间表（公共区域夜间）：</strong>通过在公共区域使用运动传感器进行夜间照明，您可以在周围无人时降低电费。将走廊、厕所和休息室的夜灯变成运动传感器。通过设置时间表（例如，晚上 10 点后使用运动传感器）可以进一步减少。减少照明是节省能源最简单的方法。</li>
<li><strong>空调-温度设定/控制（客房温度控制卡）：</strong>只要改变空调或暖气的设定温度1度，电费就会改变约10%（参考值）。通过准备客房温度控制卡（例如空调26度、暖气20度）并告知客人“环保温度设置”，可以在降低成本的同时获得客人的理解。将供暖和制冷管理到不影响客人舒适度的水平非常重要。</li>
<li><strong>热水 — 了解用量（浴室的估计显示量）：</strong>热水用量可根据浴室内的大致显示来确定（例如淋浴每分钟10升）。您可以通过在浴室中展示“环保淋浴时间”指南来减少客人使用的热水量。减少热水也可以降低燃气成本。</li>
</ul>

<h2>4. 在员工中运行——可视化并分享结果</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>将每月记录张贴在显眼的地方：</strong>将每月电费单和每个房间的电费单张贴在工作人员可以看到的地方。通过可视化记录，工作人员将“了解电力成本”。可视化是节能的第一步。</li>
<li><strong>分享“日元较上月下降”（分享结果）：</strong>如果您能比上个月减少电费，请与您的员工分享，并说：“与上个月相比，我节省了 5,000 日元。”通过分享结果，员工可以体验到“节能的成果”，并获得继续节能的动力。分享成果让节能成为一种成就感，而不仅仅是忍受。</li>
<li><strong>将节省下来的钱返还给当地社区：</strong>通过将减少的电费的一部分返还给当地活动（例如当地清洁活动、当地活动），员工感到他们通过节约能源为当地社区做出了贡献。节能不仅是“收入账户”，也是“为当地社区着想”。</li>
</ul>
