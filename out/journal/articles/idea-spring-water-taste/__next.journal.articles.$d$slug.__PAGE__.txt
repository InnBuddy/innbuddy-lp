<p>この記事では、水を「声のある体験」に変える方法を、欧州と日本の文化から引き出し、避けて通れない安全と法規の話とセットで考えます。水の話は、順番を守れば強いコンテンツになるのです。</p>

<h2>1. WORLD — 水を飲みに、街へ行く文化</h2>
<p>2021年、UNESCOは「欧州の大温泉都市群」を世界遺産に登録しました。イギリスのバース、ドイツのバーデン・バーデン、フランスのヴィシー、チェコのカルロヴィ・ヴァリ、ベルギーのスパ——7カ国11都市です。</p>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>7カ国・11都市</strong> — 「欧州の大温泉都市群」は2021年にUNESCO世界遺産に登録された。1700年代から1930年代にかけて栄えた温泉保養文化の都市群で、「飲泉殿（Trinkhalle）」という水を飲むための建物や、保養のための散歩道・緑地が、都市の構造として今も残っている。</div>
<p>この文化の面白さは、人々が<strong>水を飲みに</strong>、何週間も滞在しに出かけた点にあります。医師が処方した泉質の水を、飲泉殿で決まった量ずつ飲み、緑の丘を散歩します。欧州では、水は飲料ではなく、<strong>目的地そのもの</strong>でした。日本の温泉文化が「浸かる」水を中心に発達したのと対照的に、欧州は「飲む」水を中心に都市を発達させました。</p>
<p>ここから翻訳できるのは、宿は「水を出す」のではなく「<strong>水の飲み方を演出できる</strong>」ということです。器と、場所と、時間。それが設計の対象になります。</p>

<h2>2. JAPAN — 「名水」をお祝いする国</h2>
<div class="note"><span class="tag">DID YOU KNOW?</span>日本には「水を顕彰する」制度があります。1985年、環境庁（現・環境省）が「全国名水百選」を選定しました。湧き水や河川、用水などを「名水」として認定する試みで、各地に水を汲みに訪れる人々の風景が生まれました。名水地に並ぶポリタンクの列は、ある意味で欧州の飲泉文化の親戚です。</div>
<p>日本の水には、もうひとつ個性があります。軟水が多いことです。欧州の多くの地域は硬水で、日本の水は抽出や出汁に適した軟水が中心——和食や茶の湯が、日本の水とともに育ってきたという文脈は、コーヒーと茶の世界では広く知られているのです。面白い対比になります。欧州が「鉱物を味わう」文化なら、日本は「軟水で淹れる」文化です。同じ「場所の水」でも、売り方がまったく違うのです。</p>

<h2>3. 宿で水を体験にする——4つの型</h2>
<p>実装は「湧く・汲む・淹れる・温もる」の4型に整理できます。</p>
<p><strong>①湧く（見せる）</strong>——宿の水源を、見られる場所にします。井戸、湧き口、沢。案内板1枚「この宿の水のはなし」。土地の水系地図——水がどこから来て、どこへ行くか——をロビーに貼るだけでも、「水の宿」の輪郭が生まれます。水源は宿が語れる数少ない「土地の内部」だからです。</p>
<p><strong>②汲む（出す）</strong>——水質の確認が取れた水源なら、ガラスのピッチャーに汲んで食卓に出しましょう。「今朝、裏の湧き口で汲みました」——この一言が言える宿は、そう多くありません。ただし汲み置きはしません。当日汲み、冷やし、器は毎回洗います。これが衛生の基本です。</p>
<p><strong>③淹れる</strong>——里山の水で淹れるコーヒーと茶。軟水の話を1行の説明カードに添えるだけで、朝の一杯が「この土地の水の味」になります。詳しくは<a href="#mountain-water-coffee">山の水で淹れるコーヒー</a>で扱いますが、要は「水を主語にする」だけで、飲み物の説明が変わります。</p>
<p><strong>④温もる（触れる・聴く）</strong>——沢の音を聴く席、水辺の朝の散歩、夏の沢で足を浸す数分（安全の確認は前提）。水の温度は、言葉より先に記憶に残ります。真夏の沢の冷たさを知っている身体は、その宿を忘れません。</p>

<h2>4. 避けて通れない話——飲用の水の安全と法規</h2>
<p>ここは、この記事でいちばん大切な部分かもしれません。水の体験は、<strong>安全の上にしか成立しません</strong>。</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>飲んでもらう水の前提</strong> — 井戸水や湧き水を飲用に供する場合は、水質検査と、自治体・保健所への確認が前提になります。宿の給水設備が「簡易専用水道」に該当する場合は、管理の基準が定められています。まず管轄の窓口に問い合わせることから始めるのが正しい順序です。</li>
<li><strong>容器詰めは別世界</strong> — 水をボトルに詰めて提供・販売する場合、食品衛生法上の製造業としての許可等が必要になるのです。「汲んだ水をかわいいボトルで」は、規制の上では製造業です。</li>
<li><strong>水質は変動する</strong> — 大雨後の濁り、周辺の農地や人家の影響。検査は一度でなく定期的に。雪解け期の水の表情の変化は、体感として楽しい反面、管理の観点では要注意の季節でもあります。</li>
</ul>
<p>判断が難しい場合の、安全な代替策も用意しておきましょう。市販の地元の名水（名水百選の地などの瓶水）をガラスの器に移して出し、「この地域の水です」と案内します。<strong>安全をクリアした上で「場所の水」の物語を守る</strong>——この折り合いの付け方を知っておくと、どの宿でも水の体験を始められます。</p>

<h2>5. どんな宿から始められるか</h2>
<p><strong>水源を持つ宿</strong>（井戸・湧き口・沢）は、まず水質検査の問い合わせから。安全が確認できれば「飲める水の宿」は希少な訴求になります。それまでは「見せる」「聴く」水で待ちましょう。<strong>水源のない宿</strong>は、産地の水を置く、水で淹れる体験（コーヒー・茶）に寄せる、沢や川の風景を案内する、の3方向から選べます。<strong>都市型</strong>でも、硬水と軟水の話を朝食の説明に使うことはできます——「欧州の朝食と日本の朝食、水の物語」という案内1枚で、朝食は文化の話に変わります。</p>

11:T2437,<p>The bottle of water placed in the room can be bought at any convenience store. But the stream running behind the inn? The water of the well? — That water can be tasted only in that place. What lodging sells is "time that can be had only in that place," and water should be its most familiar material; and yet at Japanese inns, water is usually served in silence.</p>

<p>In this article we draw out, from the cultures of Europe and Japan, ways to turn water into "an experience with a voice," and think about them together with the safety and legal matters that cannot be skirted. The story of water becomes strong content if you keep the order.</p>

<h2>1. WORLD — a culture of going to town to drink the water</h2>

<p>In 2021, UNESCO inscribed "the Great Spa Towns of Europe" as a World Heritage site. Bath in England, Baden-Baden in Germany, Vichy in France, Karlovy Vary in Czechia, Spa in Belgium — eleven towns in seven countries.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>11 towns, 7 countries</strong> — "The Great Spa Towns of Europe" was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2021. They are towns of the spa-cure culture that flourished from the 1700s to the 1930s, and structures for drinking the water — the Trinkhalle, or drinking hall — together with promenades and greenery for the cure, remain today as part of the urban fabric.</div>

<p>What is interesting in this culture is that people traveled <strong>to drink the water</strong>, staying for weeks. The water of the spring the doctor prescribed, drunk in fixed amounts at the drinking hall; walks on the green hills. In Europe, water was not a beverage — <strong>it was the destination itself</strong>. While Japan’s hot-spring culture grew around water you soak in, Europe grew towns around water you drink.</p>

<p>What can be translated from here is that the inn should not "serve water" but <strong>"stage how the water is drunk."</strong> The vessel, the place, the time. Those become the objects of design.</p>

<h2>2. JAPAN — a country that celebrates its "famous waters"</h2>

<div class="note"><span class="tag">DID YOU KNOW?</span>Japan has a system for honoring water. In 1985, the Environment Agency (today’s Ministry of the Environment) selected the "One Hundred Famous Waters of the Nation." Springs, rivers, and channels are recognized as "famous waters," and the sight of people coming to draw water spread across the regions. The rows of plastic tanks at famous-water sites are, in a sense, relatives of Europe’s drinking-hall culture.</div>

<p>Japanese water has one more character: it is mostly soft. Much of Europe is hard water, while Japan’s water centers on soft water suited to extraction and to dashi stock — the context in which washoku and the way of tea grew up together with Japanese water is widely known in the worlds of coffee and tea. It makes an interesting contrast. If Europe is a culture of "tasting the minerals," Japan is a culture of "brewing in soft water." The same "water of the place" is sold in utterly different ways.</p>

<h2>3. Turning water into experience at the inn — four patterns</h2>

<p>Implementation sorts into four patterns: well, draw, brew, warm.</p>

<p><strong>(1) Well (show it)</strong> — Make the inn’s water source a place that can be seen. The well, the spring mouth, the stream. One sign: "The story of this inn’s water." Just posting a map of the local water system in the lobby — where the water comes from, and where it goes — gives the outline of "an inn of water." The water source is one of the few "interiors of the land" an inn can speak of.</p>

<p><strong>(2) Draw (serve it)</strong> — Where the water quality has been confirmed, draw it into a glass pitcher and set it on the table. "This morning we drew it at the spring behind the inn" — the inns that can say this one phrase are not many. But do not keep drawn water standing. Draw it that day, chill it, and wash the vessels every time. That is the basis of hygiene.</p>

<p><strong>(3) Brew</strong> — Coffee and tea brewed in satoyama water. Add one line on a card about the soft water, and the morning cup becomes "the taste of this place’s water." The details are treated in <a href="#mountain-water-coffee">Coffee Brewed with Mountain Water: Designing a Cup in Nature</a>, but the point is that making water the subject changes the explanation of the drink.</p>

<p><strong>(4) Warm (touch it, hear it)</strong> — A seat for listening to the stream, a morning walk by the water, a few minutes soaking the feet in a summer stream (with safety confirmed first). The temperature of water reaches memory before words do. A body that knows the cold of a midsummer stream does not forget that inn.</p>

<h2>4. What cannot be skirted — the safety and the law of drinking water</h2>

<p>This may be the most important part of this article. The experience of water <strong>stands on nothing but safety</strong>.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The premise of water you have guests drink</strong> — To serve well water or spring water for drinking, water-quality testing and confirmation with the municipality and health office are premises. Where an inn’s water supply falls under the "private water supply" category, standards for its management are set. The correct order is to begin by contacting the office with jurisdiction.</li>
<li><strong>Bottling is another world</strong> — To put water into bottles and serve or sell it, licenses as manufacturing under the Food Sanitation Act and the like become necessary. "The water we drew, in a pretty bottle" is, in regulatory terms, manufacturing.</li>
<li><strong>Water quality moves</strong> — Turbidity after heavy rain, the influence of nearby fields and houses. Testing is not once but periodic. The changing face of the water in the thaw is delightful as experience and, from the standpoint of management, a season to watch.</li>
</ul>

<p>For the hard cases, prepare a safe substitute. Serve a local famous water sold in bottles (from a One Hundred Famous Waters site, for instance), decanted into glass, and guide: "This is the water of this region." <strong>Keep the story of "the water of the place" after clearing safety</strong> — know how to strike this balance, and any inn can begin the experience of water.</p>

<h2>5. Which inns can begin</h2>

<p><strong>An inn with a water source</strong> (a well, a spring, a stream) starts with inquiring about water-quality testing. Once safety is confirmed, "an inn with drinkable water" is a rare appeal. Until then, wait with water you "show" and "hear." <strong>An inn without a source</strong> can choose among three directions: set out water from a famous local source, lean into experiences of brewing (coffee, tea), or guide the scenery of streams and rivers. Even the <strong>urban inn</strong> can use the story of hard and soft water in the breakfast explanation — with one card, "A European breakfast and a Japanese breakfast: the story of water," breakfast turns into a story of culture.</p>

<h2>6. The reality of running it</h2>

<p>Hygiene of drawn water (drawn same-day, chilled, vessels washed), maintenance of pipes and storage tanks, the cost and frequency of testing (these vary by region and by the items tested, so no estimate is given here — please confirm), and responding to the water’s seasonal changes. The experience of water is "a luxury built on safety," and only the inns that keep this order earn the right to tell the story of water. It may sound grand, but the trust of an inn that has made a guest ill from its water cannot be won back by any lovely story (InnBuddy VIEW).</p>

<h2>Closing</h2>

<p>The people of Europe went off on cures of weeks to drink the water. Travel in Japan has kept company with water through the way of tea and the hot springs. Is the water at your inn silent today? The first step can be a call to the window for water-quality testing, or quitting the morning habit of drawn-in-advance water and switching to same-day drawing. When the water gains a voice, the inn’s "placeness" thickens from a single glass.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. Cases and figures are estimates as of writing, based on public materials. In implementing, design under each inn’s own judgment, including safety, laws and regulations, and consideration for the neighborhood. When serving spring water or wild plants and foods to guests, always confirm the safety of food and drink.</div>
