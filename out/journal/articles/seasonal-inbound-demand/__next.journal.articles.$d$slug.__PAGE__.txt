11:T1c72,<p>Talk of inbound demand tends to run on feel: "We've been seeing more foreign guests lately." But the true shape of demand is not a feeling; it is a "wave" that can be read in numbers. Line up 2024 by month in the Japan National Tourism Organization (JNTO) statistics, and four clean peaks stand in the arrival figures: March (3,081,781), July (3,292,500), October (3,312,000 — a record for a single month at the time), and December (3,489,800 — the first month since records began to exceed 3.4 million). Spring, summer, autumn, year-end — preparing for these four waves is itself the year's design of inbound acquisition.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Visitor arrivals to Japan in 2024 totaled 36,869,900 for the year (an all-time high). By month: December 3,489,800 (the first month above 3.4 million), October 3,312,000 (a record single month at the time), July 3,292,500, and March 3,081,781 — four peaks across spring, summer, autumn, and year-end (Japan National Tourism Organization estimates)</strong> — Inbound demand arrives every year as four waves aligned with the calendar.</div>

<h2>The Japanese calendar, and the world traveler’s calendar</h2>

<p>Read the four peaks, one by one. <strong>The March peak is the cherry blossoms.</strong> Trip planning starts the year before, the moment the bloom forecasts are published, and bookings concentrate four to eight weeks out. But because the bloom shifts year to year, an inn on this wave is safer selling "how to spend the cherry-blossom season" than "guaranteed full bloom." <strong>The July peak is the Northern Hemisphere's summer holidays.</strong> A long wave on which school breaks in the West, Southeast Asia, and China overlap — families and long stays increase. <strong>The October peak is the autumn foliage and Asia's autumn holidays.</strong> The wave is short and tall — the foliage's best weeks last only about two to three weeks per region — and this wave alone tends to book even further in advance. <strong>The December peak is the year-end city stay.</strong> Countdowns, illuminations, and travelers who want to experience a Japanese New Year. That the tallest peak of 2024 was December is a number that betrays many inns' intuition (that year-end means fewer guests as Japanese head home to their families). The traveler's calendar is not the Japanese calendar. One more caution: demand from China and Southeast Asia creates another small peak at Lunar New Year — falling anywhere from late January into February depending on the year.</p>

<h2>The preparation timeline — from eight weeks out</h2>

<p>Work that must catch a wave needs to be finished before the wave arrives. The guide is eight weeks ahead.</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Eight weeks out — update the listing</strong> — Photos (to the current season), title, plans. The story of ranking on the booking sites is left to the listing-optimization article.</li>
<li><strong>Six weeks out — design the pricing</strong> — Three tiers: early-bird, standard, last-minute. How wide to set the gap against the off-season is decided here too.</li>
<li><strong>Four weeks out — social announcements and the response setup</strong> — Announcements in the wave's language (English seasonal greetings — the 24 sekki article helps) and a system that answers inquiries the same day. Overseas inquiries arrive at midnight, with the time difference.</li>
<li><strong>Two weeks out — final checks</strong> — Cancellation policy, payments (overseas cards, digital payment), guidance through to arrival. Leak here, and the wave's benefit turns into complaints.</li>
</ol>

<h2>The off-season — use it as "the window for experiments"</h2>

<p>The valleys between the four waves — typically June, mid-to-late September, late November, and February — are wasted as merely "the slow months." When demand is small, failure is small. In other words, the off-season is <strong>a window for testing new plans and experimenting with prices at the lowest cost of failure</strong>. The breakfast-included plan you want to try, the weekday discount you want to try, a trial day-use offer. Test it small in the valley, then raise it to production before the wave. The year's design is these two rhythms: earn with the waves, test with the valleys.</p>

<h2>The other side of seasonal strategy</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The fragility of the wave-only inn</strong> — An inn full in the four wave months thins out on the valleys' fixed costs. A valley strategy (experiments, maintenance, leave, local guests) matters as much as the wave strategy.</li>
<li><strong>The cherry-blossom and foliage gamble</strong> — Bloom and peak color move every year. An inn that declares "full bloom scheduled" is hurt in the year it misses, unless something else is prepared. On weather-dependent peaks, add weather-independent content (the onsen, the food, the experiences).</li>
<li><strong>Multilingual preparation</strong> — The wave's language is not always English. Which market's wave you take decides the guidance language and the payments you support. There is no need to assemble every language overnight — choose the waves you take.</li>
<li><strong>The limits of capacity</strong> — Of course a wave month cannot take more bookings than the rooms — but cleaning, breakfast, and transfers cap out at the same time. Over-accept on occupancy alone and the quality of the reviews drops, breaking the next year's wave.</li>
<li><strong>Fairness in peak-season pricing</strong> — A run of "expensive because it's popular" falls under the "unpermitted increases" named by the fairness research in this series. If you raise, raise together with the reasons of content (seasonal dishes, the visible view).</li>
<li><strong>Statistics are the whole picture</strong> — Total arrivals do not match your region or your guest layer. What to read in JNTO's numbers is "the shape of the wave"; what to read in your own booking records is "your own wave."</li>
</ul>

<p>Start small: print next year's calendar, color the four peaks (March, July, October, December), and give the valleys a different color. On the peaks, write the "eight weeks out" reminder; in the valleys, write "the one experiment." One sheet of calendar becomes the blueprint for a year.</p>

<h2>1. 4波</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>春天（樱花）</strong> ——最大的山。预订需提前 4 至 8 周开始。</li>
<li><strong>夏季（假期+活动）</strong> ——长长的波浪。 4-8 周前。</li>
<li><strong>秋天（秋叶+活动）</strong> — 短而高的波浪。 2-4 周前。</li>
<li><strong>新年假期</strong> — 庆祝活动如山。 2-4 周前。</li>
</ul>
<p>之间<strong>淡季（5月下旬、8月底、11月）</strong>是定价策略窗口。</p>
<h2>2. 准备时间表（8周清单）</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>8 周前</strong> — 更新列表（照片、标题、计划）。</li>
<li><strong>6 周前</strong> — 价格设计（早期折扣、标准折扣和最后一刻折扣）。</li>
<li><strong>4 周前</strong> — SNS公告+询问回复系统。</li>
<li><strong>2 周前</strong> — 最终确认（取消/付款/抵达）。</li>
</ol>
<h2>3、淡季策略</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>淡季是“涨价窗口”</strong> — 在低竞争和稳定需求时期尝试价格。</li>
<li>它还可以用于测试新计划（失败成本很低）。</li>
</ul>

<h2>为什么现在值得关注？</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>客人期待的变化：</strong>“季节性入境需求 - 准备什么和何时准备的基本知识” 与五年前相比，客人的期望发生了变化。过去“有了更好”的服务，如今已变成“没有就难以被选择”。适应这些变化，是住宿设施持续经营的前提。</li>
<li><strong>与地区关系：</strong>在网上吸引顾客的努力增加了整个地区的价值，而不仅仅是旅馆本身的价值。通过与当地生产者和周边商户合作，旅馆的行动会成为“当地故事”。社区共同参与，才更容易长期持续。</li>
<li><strong>不增加员工负担：</strong>新举措不应增加员工负担。通过建立机制，用一张表或每次15分钟的学习来运转，就能在不增加员工负担的情况下持续改进。</li>
</ul>

<h2>避免失败的3个要点</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>从小处开始——先做好一件事：</strong>一开始就想全部改变，往往难以持续。先从一件事开始。可以从一间客房、一个货架或一句话开始；即使效果不理想，影响也小，更容易调整。</li>
<li><strong>记录——用一张表看清楚：</strong>把行动记录在一张表里，效果就能被看见。你会知道哪些做法有效、哪些需要调整，记录也会成为下一轮改进的依据。</li>
<li><strong>持续——每周一次，每次15分钟：</strong>新行动需要持续。每周留出15分钟，逐渐形成习惯，最后才会成为团队文化。</li>
</ol>
