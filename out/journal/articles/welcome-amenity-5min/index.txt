In the first five minutes after a guest arrives, the greater part of the property’s impression is decided. This article is the foundation for designing those five minutes (the words and gestures of the welcome — how to stand, how to bow, the first sentence — are handled by <a href="#omotenashi-arrival-departure">Omotenashi Arrival and Departure: Two Points of Japanese Hospitality</a>. <h2>The hatago knew the traveler’s first five minutes</h2>

<p>When an Edo traveler reached a hatago inn, what did they do first? In the Totsuka post station scene of the "Tōkaidōchū Hizakurige" (1802), the serving maid first brings <strong>a basin of hot water for rinsing the feet</strong> and carries the luggage to the room; Yaji-san and Kita-san rinse their feet and gaiters in the basin, and only then go up into the room and have tea served. Hiroshige’s Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō also depicts travelers rinsing their feet in a basin.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>At Edo hatago inns, what a traveler did first on arriving was "wash the feet" — the maid drew hot water into a rinsing basin, and the traveler washed feet and gaiters before going up to the room and receiving tea. The scene appears in the lodging passages of the "Tōkaidōchū Hizakurige" and in Hiroshige’s ukiyo-e</strong> — The first minutes of the hatago began not with things but with "care of the body."</div>

<p>Here, almost intact, is the design of the modern welcome amenity. A trip <strong>arrives in the body</strong> — the feet have walked, the throat is dry, the skin has sweated. The hatago cared for the traveler’s body before their heart. Hot water for the feet (the fatigue of travel), a hand towel (sweat and dust), tea (thirst). The first five minutes were not a stage for showing hospitality’s sentiment, but <strong>five minutes for putting the body back</strong>.</p>

<h2>3 seconds, and 5 minutes</h2>

<p>A property’s first impression is said to be roughly decided within three seconds of arrival (the entrance’s scent, the brightness, a person’s face). And what decides whether that impression becomes an experience or vanishes is the five minutes after it. Stretching the three-second impression into a five-minute experience — on that line lies the welcome amenity. The design that dissolves anxiety before arrival is handled by <a href="#guest-journey-map">The Whole Guest Experience: Mapping the Touchpoints</a>, and the design of scent at the moment of entering the room by <a href="#scent-music-five-senses">Scent & Sound — A Stay the Five Senses Remember</a>. Here: the five minutes in between.</p>

<h2>3 points of design — cold, one bite, one line of story</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>One cold drink</strong> — The arrived body is usually thirsty. Choose by season and by the manner of arrival — cold tea or herbal tea on a hot day, a warm cup on a cold one. As with the hatago’s basin, matching "the body right now" is what looks most like kizuki.</li>
<li><strong>One bite of sweets</strong> — Something eaten in a single bite. A cookie, a Japanese sweet, dried fruit. A large sweet only adds "the work of eating" to the first five minutes.</li>
<li><strong>One line of story</strong> — "This tea is from the farm next door." One line is enough. The first five minutes are not the time to pack in explanations.</li>
</ol>

<h2>3 points of operation — per person, within 5 minutes, by situation</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>One serving per person</strong> — Not a large shared plate, but a small dish per guest. Seeing the dishes lined up by headcount, a person feels "this was prepared for me."</li>
<li><strong>Within 5 minutes</strong> — At properties where arrivals concentrate at 3 p.m., "within five minutes" is the promise most easily broken. For busy hours, guard it with mechanism: set it on a tray, place it in front of the room beforehand.</li>
<li><strong>Match the situation</strong> — A child’s bite for the family with children. On a rainy day, a towel for taking the wet luggage. Read the manner of arrival — the person who came walking, by car, by bicycle or motorcycle: the place of thirst and fatigue differs. As the hatago looked at straw-sandaled feet, <strong>kizuki begins with watching how the guest arrives</strong>.</li>
</ul>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">DID YOU KNOW?</span><strong>The custom of welcoming with an oshibori (a damp rolled towel) is counted as a scene of Japanese service descending from the hand-towel culture the hatago offered travelers</strong> — Care of the body — wiping sweat — became, as it was, the greeting of welcome.</div>

<h2>The other side — when the five minutes exhaust the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Becoming a gift set</strong> — An arrangement of luxury sweets ends as a present received. What the first five minutes should speak of is not the property’s formality but notice of the body.</li>
<li><strong>Hygiene and display</strong> — For food, one line of expiry and ingredients. Especially for fresh items and in summer. Even a one-bite sweet needs its explanation for guests with allergies.</li>
<li><strong>Packing in the story</strong> — Tell the producer, the method, the particularity in the first five minutes, and the tea goes cold and the guest tires. The story, one line. The rest, at mealtime.</li>
<li><strong>The weight of handmade</strong> — Serve what cannot be prepared daily, every day, and on the third day the floor breaks. Choose a sustainable form from the start.</li>
<li><strong>The kindness of leaving alone</strong> — For a late-night arrival, a couple worn out from the journey home, quietly placing it and withdrawing can be the right answer. Kizuki is not a matter of the volume of words.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is the three items to have ready by 3 p.m. tomorrow. One cold drink, one bite of sweets, one line of story. As the hatago washed the traveler’s feet with a basin of hot water, the first five minutes <strong>enter through the body</strong> — that is this country’s unchanged way of welcoming, unchanged for hundreds of years.</p>

1c:T16cf,<p>欢迎礼品<strong>“前 5 分钟进行设计”</strong>。许多旅馆将迎宾小品视为“茶和糖果”，并认为提供这些就可以了。然而，如果你从不同的角度来看，迎宾设施是为“前五分钟”设计的。客人到达您的旅馆后的前五分钟将决定他们对您的旅馆的印象。迎宾礼品是一种让客人在入住五分钟内感受到“这家酒店在为我着想”的工具。本文整理了如何设计迎宾礼品，不仅仅是茶和糖果，而是前 5 分钟。</p>

<h2>1. 为什么前5分钟——3秒和5分钟的第一印象</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>第一印象在 3 秒内形成：</strong>客人对酒店的第一印象是在抵达后 3 秒内形成的。无论是入口处的香味、工作人员的笑容，还是休息室的明亮度，只需3秒就能决定这家酒店好看与否。迎宾礼品是一种将 3 秒的第一印象延长为 5 分钟的体验的工具。</li>
<li><strong>在前 5 分钟内让他们感觉“这家酒店正在考虑我”：</strong>重要的是要让客人在到达旅馆后的五分钟内感觉到“这家旅馆正在为他们着想”。迎宾礼品是一种让人们在前五分钟内感觉“人们在想着我”的工具。例如：“谢谢你的辛勤工作。请喝点冷茶。” “一定很热。请喝点凉凉茶。”通过提供符合客人情况的信息，客人会感觉到您在为他们着想。</li>
<li><strong>前5分钟讲述“客栈的故事”：</strong>迎宾礼品也是一个在前五分钟内讲述客栈故事的工具。例如：“这茶是隔壁农民做的”，或者“这甜品是用当地啤酒厂的啤酒做的。”通过添加故事，客人可以了解“旅馆的故事”。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2.设计三点——冷、咬、故事</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>寒冷——抵达的客人口渴：</strong>客人抵达客栈，长途跋涉都口渴了。提供冷饮，如冷茶、花草茶或果汁，会让您的客人感到“焕发活力”。冷饮是在前五分钟内安抚客人的好方法。</li>
<li><strong>咬一口——一口就能吃完的甜食：</strong>迎宾甜食应该是可以一口吃完的甜食。客人可能会发现很难吃大蛋糕或需要咬几口的糖果。客人觉得可以一口气吃完的零食（例如饼干、日式点心、干果等）很容易吃。</li>
<li><strong>故事——关于当地材料和生产者的一句话：</strong>用迎宾礼品讲述一个故事。客人可以通过添加当地食材和有关生产者的台词来“品味故事”，例如“这茶是隔壁农民制作的”或“这种糖果是用当地啤酒厂的啤酒制成的”。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3、操作——一次1人，5分钟内，根据客人情况量身定制</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>准备一份：</strong>将为每位客人提供迎宾礼品。通过将单独的部分盛在小盘子或杯子上而不是盛在大盘子上，您的客人会觉得食物是专门为他们准备的。为每个人准备食物是待客之道的基础。</li>
<li><strong>5 分钟内上菜：</strong>客人抵达后 5 分钟内将提供迎宾礼品。如果超过5分钟，客人就会觉得还没有结束。 5分钟内提供服务，让客人感觉立即得到服务。</li>
<li><strong>适应客人情况：</strong>根据客人的情况提供迎宾礼品。例如：热天的冷茶、冷天的热茶、给带孩子的客人准备儿童糖果等等。通过适应客人的情况，让客人感觉你在为他们着想。</li>
</ul>
