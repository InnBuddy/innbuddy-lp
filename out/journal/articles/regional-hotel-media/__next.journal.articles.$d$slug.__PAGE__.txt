11:T191f,<p>Regional hotels hold a "structural advantage" in being featured by the media — the angle of "the region." Not buying advertising, but being covered. This article organizes the craft of it, starting from the theory of "why things get reported."</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">RESEARCH</span><strong>In 1965, the peace researchers Johan Galtung and Mari Holmboe Ruge analyzed the news of various countries and organized the conditions under which an event is selected as "news" as "news values" (selection criteria), in the paper "The Structure of Foreign News." The more criteria met — frequency, clarity, proximity, meaningfulness, personalization, unexpectedness, and others — the more likely a story is to be reported: a finding that became the foundation of later journalism research, showing that the selection of news runs not on whim but on predictable criteria</strong> — Whether something is reported has a structure.</div>

<h2>Regional hotels stand on the ground from the start</h2>

<p>Translate this theory for properties, and something interesting emerges. The four elements of a story that gets reported — <strong>the regional angle, the human element, social resonance, and timeliness</strong> — are, in fact, a paraphrase of the news values.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>The regional angle = proximity</strong> — What exists in this region and nowhere else. The story "nearest" to the regional media’s readers.</li>
<li><strong>The human element = personalization</strong> — The owner, the staff, the producers. People are interested in people. More than a story of systems, the story of one life is chosen.</li>
<li><strong>Social resonance = meaningfulness</strong> — Employment, regional vitality, the environment. The more a story overlaps the readers’ concerns (social issues), the more "meaning" it is judged to carry.</li>
<li><strong>Timeliness = the season</strong> — Season, events, anniversaries. The reason to report it now.</li>
</ul>

<p>When three of the four are in place, there is "value in covering it" — this is not a rule of thumb but a match with the theory’s prediction (the more criteria met, the more likely the selection). <strong>While big-city hotels struggle to build "the regional angle," regional properties hold this criterion from the start.</strong> What is missing is only the deliberate assembling of the remaining three.</p>

<h2>The press kit — a tool for making coverage "easy"</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The 3 versions of the story</strong> — 30-second, 3-minute, 10-minute. The same one-source three translations as in the story article.</li>
<li><strong>A fact box</strong> — Numbers, history, track record. Show the grounds (the discipline of numbers is shared with the zero-waste article — attach the measurement method).</li>
<li><strong>A photo set</strong> — Five to ten high-resolution images + credit notation. Articles are often born from photographs.</li>
<li><strong>A contact, and a promise of response</strong> — One person in charge, and "within how many hours we reply." A reporter’s deadline does not wait for the property’s convenience.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The essence of "coverage, not advertising" — third-party words</h2>

<p>Why coverage rather than advertising? A self-funded advertisement is "what the property wants to say"; an article is "what a third party found worth reporting" — the difference in credit here is the same structure as the relation between reviews and your own site (see the review-management article). And the regional reporter is always searching for "stories of the region." The demand for stories exists every day; what is short is supply — stories shaped into a form that easily becomes an article. <strong>First build a track record in the local media</strong>, then deliver, with a seasonal hook (season, events, anniversaries), once a quarter. The national media come from the far end of that accumulation.</p>

<h2>The other side — when PR exhausts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Forwarding the same pitch everywhere</strong> — What the local paper wants is not a national story but a local one. Rewrite the substance of "proximity" for each outlet.</li>
<li><strong>Exaggerating the numbers</strong> — Articles are written on confirmed facts. If the numbers in the press kit have no grounds, the trust does not come back to the property.</li>
<li><strong>What comes after coverage is the real contest</strong> — If the experience of the guests who arrive because of the article falls below what the article described, the article widens the drop instead. Keep the order: fix the substance before it is reported.</li>
<li><strong>The burden on the one person in charge</strong> — Set the SLA at a realistic level, such as "reply within 24 hours." Pile the PR role onto a concurrent post, and slow replies become lost opportunities.</li>
<li><strong>Do not make publication the goal</strong> — The article’s URL is an asset reusable as evidence in booking-site descriptions and on social (see the article on where to exhibit the story). Do not discard it after one use.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is to write the regional angle in one line — "the only △△ in 〇〇." To that line, add a person (who) and a season (why now). The first page of the press kit begins from those three lines. What the media report is not "a hotel" but "a story." And inside a regional property, the story is already sleeping, ready to be written.</p>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. The news-values research is a classic of journalism studies (1965), a theory that has since been extended and criticized as the media environment changed. Whether coverage happens depends on each outlet’s editorial policy.</div>

<h2>为什么现在值得关注？</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>客人期待的变化：</strong>当谈到“如何让当地酒店出现在媒体上”时，客人的期望与五年前相比发生了变化。过去“有了更好”的服务，如今已变成“没有就难以被选择”。适应这些变化，是住宿设施持续经营的前提。</li>
<li><strong>与地区关系：</strong>公关和媒体的努力增加了整个地区的价值，而不仅仅是旅馆本身。通过与当地生产者和周边商户合作，旅馆的行动会成为“当地故事”。社区共同参与，才更容易长期持续。</li>
<li><strong>不增加员工负担：</strong>新举措不应增加员工负担。通过建立机制，用一张表或每次15分钟的学习来运转，就能在不增加员工负担的情况下持续改进。</li>
</ul>

<h2>避免失败的3个要点</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>从小处开始——先做好一件事：</strong>一开始就想全部改变，往往难以持续。先从一件事开始。可以从一间客房、一个货架或一句话开始；即使效果不理想，影响也小，更容易调整。</li>
<li><strong>记录——用一张表看清楚：</strong>把行动记录在一张表里，效果就能被看见。你会知道哪些做法有效、哪些需要调整，记录也会成为下一轮改进的依据。</li>
<li><strong>持续——每周一次，每次15分钟：</strong>新行动需要持续。每周留出15分钟，逐渐形成习惯，最后才会成为团队文化。</li>
</ol>
