牧場の風景を「美しい」と語る文章はたくさんあります。この記事は少しだけ違う場所から始めます。牛の都合です。毎朝食卓に牛乳があるのは自然の摂理ではなく、牛の出産と搾乳の時間割が、休みなく回り続けている結果です。その時間割を知ると、同じ風景が違って見えます。

牛乳は「いつでもある」のではありません

牛は、子牛を出産してはじめて乳が出る動物です。妊娠期間は約280日。出産後、次の子を身ごもる準備が進み、次の出産の約2か月前には搾乳を止めて体を休ませます。これを乾乳といい、出産・泌乳・妊娠・乾乳のサイクルはおよそ12〜15か月で回ります。牛舎の牛たちは、それぞれ別の周期にいるのです。あの牛は泌乳の真ん中、この牛は来月出産予定、もう一頭はいま休んでいます。牛舎の前に「今日の牛のカレンダー」を一枚貼るだけで、牧場の案内は「眺め」から「暮らしの記録」に変わります。

FACT牛乳は出産のあとにはじめて出る。妊娠期間は約280日、出産・泌乳・妊娠・乾乳のサイクルは約12〜15か月 — 獣医師・酪農関連の専門資料による。牛舎の牛がそれぞれ別の周期にいること自体が、案内の第一歩になる。

朝夕2回、365日——酪農の時間割

搾乳は1日2回、朝と夕。正月も台風の日もありません。酪農家の生活は、牛の時間割に合わせて組まれています。牧場と連携した体験を設計するとき、この時間割が最初の制約条件です。朝の搾乳は早く、たいてい宿の朝食より前。だから「早朝の搾乳に立ち会い、そのあと朝食に戻る」という流れは物理的に成立します。反対に「10時からのんびり乳搾り」は、牛の都合で成立しません。体験の時間帯を牛側に合わせられるかどうかが、企画の現実的な分かれ目です。

DID YOU KNOW?1頭の乳牛から出る乳は、1日20〜40kg。1リットルパックにして毎日20〜40本分 — 朝夕2回の搾乳で365日続く、酪農の時間割の基礎体力。

もうひとつ、数字を持っておくと案内が深くなります。乳用牛を飼う農家の数は、この10年ほどでおよそ3割減りました（年率3〜5％の減少。農林水産省畜産局の資料による）。牧場は「当たり前にある風景」ではなく、全国的に数が減っていく側の風景です。「いま、目の前にある」という事実を伝えるだけでも、ゲストの見え方は変わります。

スイスでは、夏の牧場が村の「チーズ貯金」になります

アルプス地方では、夏の約4か月、牛を高地の牧場（アルプ）に上げます。牛飼いが牛を預かって毎日搾乳し、山小屋でチーズを作ります。秋になると、牛は花輪とカウベルで飾られて村へ下ろされるのです。この「牧下り（アルプアブツーク）」は村の祭りで、山小屋で作ったチーズは、乳量に応じて牛の持ち主である農家へ分配されます。この分配祭りが続くスイスのユスティス谷では、祭りの歴史は1739年まで遡れます。一番ミルクを出した牛が、一番大きな花輪を着るのです。

スイスのメディアがあるアルプを取材した数字が示唆的です。3か月のシーズンで約11万5,000リットルの牛乳から、チーズ約11トンが作られました。単純に計算すると、牛乳およそ10リットルでチーズ1kg。あの大きなチーズの塊は、濃縮された夏の牧草です。なぜこの話が面白いのでしょうか。牧場の風景は、個人の努力だけではなく「預け合いの仕組み」で保たれているからです。牛を預け、草地を分かち、チーズを分配します。日本の里山も同じで、牧場の堆肥が畑や果樹園の土を作る循環は、牧場・草地・畑が互いを支える関係の上にあります。牧場の風景を案内する宿は、結局、このつながりを案内しているのです。

チーズ作り体験の、いちばん伝えたいこと

搾ったばかりの乳は日持ちしません。だから人類は乳を、チーズという保存可能なかたちに変えてきました。チーズ作り体験の核心は、レシピの習得ではなく、この「濃縮と保存」の発想に触れることです。宿で体験できるのは、モッツァレラやカッテージチーズのようなフレッシュタイプが中心。熟成チーズは衛生と設備のハードルが高く、近くのチーズ工房や牧場と組めるかどうかが現実的な条件です。

子ども向けには、乳搾りやチーズよりも先に「牛との距離の作法」を伝える順番が安全です。牛の後ろには立ちません。触れたあとは手を洗いましょう。子牛に触れすぎないようにします。この3つを最初に伝えるだけで、体験の安全の質が変わります。

はじめに、向き不向き

  • 向く宿 — 牧場・酪農家と日常的に連携できる距離にある宿。朝の時間帯にゲストがいる宿。子ども連れ・三世代の旅行を受け入れる余裕のある宿。
  • 向かない宿 — 朝の時間帯を体験に使えない宿。動物との接触のリスクを説明・管理できる体制がない宿。においや鳴き声への苦情を織り込めない立地の宿。
  • 費用と負担 — 乳搾り体験は、体験者が増えるほど牧場側の仕事が増える体験。人数制限と報酬の設計を、引き合わせの段階で決めておきましょう。
  • 法規制 — 搾りたての生乳をそのまま提供することはできません。提供する牛乳は、定められた加熱殺菌を経たものに限ります。

There are plenty of texts that call a pasture landscape “beautiful.” This article starts from a slightly different place: the cow’s schedule. Milk on the breakfast table every morning is not a law of nature; it is the result of a timetable of calving and milking that keeps turning without a break. Once you know that timetable, the same landscape looks different.

Milk is not “always there”

A cow is an animal that gives milk only after giving birth to a calf. Gestation lasts about 280 days. After calving, preparation for the next pregnancy proceeds, and about two months before the next calving, milking is stopped to let the body rest. This is called the dry period, and the cycle of calving, lactation, pregnancy, and dry period turns over roughly 12 to 15 months. The cows in a barn are each at a different point in their cycle. That cow is in the middle of lactation, this one is due to calve next month, and another is resting right now. Simply posting a single “today’s cow calendar” in front of the barn turns the pasture’s guidance from “a view” into “a record of a way of life.”

Gestation is about 280 days, and the cycle of calving, lactation, pregnancy, and dry period is about 12 to 15 months — Based on specialist materials from veterinarians and the dairy sector. The fact that each cow in the barn is in a different cycle is itself the first step of the guidance.

Twice a day, morning and evening, 365 days — the dairy timetable

Milking happens twice a day, morning and evening. There is no New Year’s holiday and no day off for typhoons. A dairy farmer’s life is organized around the cows’ timetable. When designing an experience in partnership with a pasture, this timetable is the first constraint. Morning milking is early, usually before the inn’s breakfast. So a flow of “attending the early-morning milking, then returning for breakfast” physically works. On the other hand, “a relaxed milking session from 10 a.m.” does not work, because of the cows’ schedule. Whether the time slot of the experience can be fitted to the cows’ side is the realistic dividing line of the plan.

DID YOU KNOW?One dairy cow gives 20 to 40 kg of milk a day — the equivalent of 20 to 40 one-liter cartons every day — The basic stamina of the dairy timetable, continuing 365 days with milking twice a day, morning and evening.

One more number deepens the guidance. The number of farms keeping dairy cattle fell by roughly 30% over about the last ten years (a decline of 3 to 5% a year, according to materials from the Livestock Industry Bureau of the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries). A pasture is not “a landscape that is simply there”; it is a landscape on the side whose numbers are falling nationwide. Just conveying the fact that “it is here, in front of you, now” changes how guests see it.

In Switzerland, the summer pasture becomes a village’s “cheese savings”

In the Alpine regions, cows are taken up to high-altitude pastures (Alps) for about four months in summer. Herders look after the cows, milk them every day, and make cheese in the mountain huts. In autumn, the cows are decorated with garlands of flowers and cowbells and brought back down to the village. This “descent from the alp” (Alpabzug) is a village festival, and the cheese made in the mountain huts is distributed to the farmers who own the cows, according to their milk yield. In Switzerland’s Justistal valley, where this distribution festival continues, the history of the festival can be traced back to 1739. The cow that gave the most milk wears the largest garland.

The figures from a Swiss media report on one alp are suggestive. Over a three-month season, about 11 tons of cheese were made from about 115,000 liters of milk. A simple calculation gives roughly 10 liters of milk for 1 kg of cheese. Those big wheels of cheese are concentrated summer grass. Why is this story interesting? Because a pasture landscape is maintained not only by individual effort but by “a system of entrusting to one another.” Entrusting the cows, sharing the grassland, distributing the cheese. The same holds for Japan’s satoyama: the cycle in which a pasture’s manure builds the soil of fields and orchards rests on a relationship in which pasture, grassland, and fields support one another. An inn that guides guests through a pasture landscape is, in the end, guiding them through this connection.

What a cheese-making experience most wants to convey

Freshly drawn milk does not keep. That is why humankind has turned milk into a storable form called cheese. The core of a cheese-making experience is not mastering a recipe but touching this idea of “concentration and preservation.” What can be experienced at an inn is mainly fresh types such as mozzarella and cottage cheese. Aged cheeses have high hurdles of hygiene and equipment, and the realistic condition is whether the inn can partner with a nearby cheese workshop or pasture.

For children, the safe order is to convey “the manners of distance from cows” before milking or cheese. Do not stand behind a cow. Wash your hands after touching. Do not touch calves too much. Just conveying these three at the very start changes the quality of the experience’s safety.

First, who it suits and who it doesn’t

  • Inns it suits — Inns within a distance that allows everyday partnership with a pasture or dairy farmer. Inns where guests are present in the morning hours. Inns with room to welcome families with children and three-generation trips.
  • Inns it doesn’t suit — Inns that cannot use the morning hours for an experience. Inns without a system to explain and manage the risks of contact with animals. Inns in locations that cannot factor in complaints about smells and animal noises.
  • Costs and burden — A milking experience is one in which the pasture’s work increases as the number of participants grows. Settle the design of group-size limits and payment at the stage of being introduced.
  • Regulations — Freshly drawn raw milk cannot be served as it is. Milk that is served is limited to milk that has undergone the prescribed heat sterilization.

InnBuddy VIEW

What a landscape with a pasture really is: the timetable of the cows’ lives and the system of entrusting to one another that supports it. An inn that can guide guests through both becomes not “an inn with a nice view” but “an inn that can explain how the land works.” The first step can be small. Place a single “today’s cow calendar” in front of the barn. That alone is enough for guests to begin reading the landscape.

This article is intended for sharing ideas. Experiences with animals presuppose the management of safety and hygiene. Decide whether to carry them out in line with the pasture’s guidance and the inn’s own capacity.

农场景观创意集合

  • 欣赏牧场风景的地方 — 在可以看到农场美丽风景的地方放置长凳和椅子，并介绍为可以欣赏农场风景的地方。通过奶牛吃草的场景以及享受牧场四个季节的场所来传达牧场的魅力。在一家旅馆，将果田旁边的农场风景介绍为“农场风景”。
  • 挤奶体验 — 让在农场挤奶成为一种小经历。挤牛奶和挤奶后品尝牛奶的经历将留在孩子们的记忆中。一家旅馆将在农场挤奶的体验介绍为“在农场挤奶”，并建议您花时间享用用牛奶制成的奶酪。
  • 奶酪制作体验 — 用挤奶制作奶酪是一次小小的体验。通过在小板上介绍奶酪的制作过程和奶酪陈化的故事，您可以讲述从农场到餐桌的故事。其中一家旅馆提供名为“农场奶酪制作”的奶酪制作体验，您可以将自己制作的奶酪作为纪念品。
  • 农场和果园的想法 ——有果田的客栈，农场也是果田旁边的风景。其中一家旅馆推出了“牧场和果园”，可以同时享受牧场和果园的体验，并计划将牧场散步和果园采摘体验结合起来。它还介绍了利用农场牛的堆肥为果田制造土壤的循环。
  • 带着孩子照顾奶牛 — 将照顾奶牛变成孩子们的一次小经历。孩子们会记得喂养和刷牙的经历。一家旅馆为孩子们组织了“奶牛护理体验”，让他们学习如何护理奶牛。
