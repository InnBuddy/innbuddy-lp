11:T1de1,<p>Group incident response is <strong>not "20 times one person" but "one unhappiness of 20 people."</strong> The school-trip bus runs late. The group’s baths are not drawn. Dinner service is an hour behind — when 20 people are uncomfortable at once, what is needed is not 20 apologies but one response that reaches all 20 at once. This article is the foundation of that simultaneous response (the basic pattern for responding to one person’s complaint is handled by <a href="#service-recovery-basics">Service Recovery: The "One Move" That Turns a Complaint Into Trust</a>. Here: what changes when the count grows).</p>

<h2>The greatest poison of confusion is "not knowing"</h2>

<p>Edo’s town fire brigades, arriving at a fire, first raised the <strong>matoi</strong> standard. They hoisted the brigade’s emblem high, making visible where, and whose, brigade was working. The firemen’s work was not only pouring water — it was also <strong>creating "visible order" inside the chaos</strong>. The standard-bearer waving the flag becomes the center of the scene. Confusion is not something only fire makes.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Edo’s town fire brigades, on reaching a fire, first hoisted their brigade’s emblem, the "matoi" standard. It was a device of visibility within chaos — showing the scene at a glance which brigade was fighting fire where</strong> — The first step in calming a crowd’s confusion is neither water nor apology, but "one visible sign."</div>

<p>When 20 people are uncomfortable at once, what happens first in the property is, like a fire, <strong>a blaze of information</strong>. "What are you going to do about this," "not yet?", "who do I even tell" — without facts, each of these grows wild in each head. What makes a group complaint different from an individual one is that <strong>dissatisfaction amplifies in the air</strong>. The order of response follows from that.</p>

<h2>The first 3 minutes — raise the flag</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>One line of fact</strong> — "There is a fault in the hot-water supply; restoration is expected in 30 minutes." State <strong>what is known</strong>, first. Do not state guesses.</li>
<li><strong>One line of response</strong> — "Until then, we will serve tea in the lobby." In a fog of unknowns, one line of response alone changes the tension of the scene.</li>
<li><strong>The time of the next update</strong> — "At ○ o’clock, 30 minutes from now, we will announce again." Cutting in a time for the next word — this is the most effective line of all (the psychology of waiting is handled in the turnover article).</li>
</ol>

<h2>3 steps — accept, correct, one move (group version)</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Accept (to everyone)</strong> — "We are sorry for the inconvenience to you all." First, one word to the whole. Begin apologizing person by person, and the remaining nineteen ripen meanwhile.</li>
<li><strong>Correct (concretely, to the representative)</strong> — The organizer, the tour conductor, the leading teacher. To the person standing near the flag, convey the concrete response and its time. But <strong>do not end with the representative alone</strong> — leave the other nineteen behind, and the blaze swallows the representative too.</li>
<li><strong>One move (to everyone)</strong> — One thing beyond expectations, to all: tea in the lounge, an extended rest, an adjusted next schedule. Translate the individual’s "one move" into a form delivered to everyone at once.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Partitioning the scene — cutting the chain of discontent</h2>

<p>Group discontent has a physical spread. Voices in the corridor infect other guests passing by. Where possible, <strong>prepare one place for the response</strong> (a corner of the lobby, the room beside the banquet hall). Gathering the 20 in one place stops the spread of discontent and, at the same time, means <strong>everyone sees the moment of resolution simultaneously</strong>. Apology to a group is, as a principle, <strong>simultaneous distribution</strong>, not a stacking of individuals. The order is group first, individuals after (reversed, the early-heard and late-heard receive different information, and new discontent is born).</p>

<h2>Recording and sharing — assets for the next group</h2>

<ul>
<li>What happened, why, how it was fixed — on one sheet, the same day.</li>
<li>Share it with the staff and add it as a line of the manual. <strong>Group-response cases are team assets</strong> — the mechanism for not meeting the same failure "for the first time" a second time.</li>
<li>If you accept groups, prepare <strong>the flag before acceptance</strong>. The organizer’s name and contact, arrival time, room assignments, meal times — gathered on one sheet beforehand, this becomes the response sheet when an incident happens (the receiving side of group design is handled by <a href="#meeting-banquet-revenue">Meeting and Banquet Revenue: Rooms Plus Meeting Basics</a>).</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other side — when group response exhausts the floor</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Not everyone can gather</strong> — For incidents before arrival, or after the group has gone into town, the group never assembles. Respond with a call to the representative and one word to everyone at their return.</li>
<li><strong>The organizer’s face</strong> — The organizer stands to be blamed by the members as well. Do not blame the organizer further at the apology. Treat them as an equal, an information-sharing partner.</li>
<li><strong>Excess of the one move</strong> — Make the amends goods or the refund too big, and a strange economy of "expecting incidents" is born. The one move is a gesture; amounts follow the rules.</li>
<li><strong>The speed of social media</strong> — One of the 20 films it and spreads it in 3 minutes. The speed of raising the first line (the flag) makes the reputation that follows (a principle shared with <a href="#review-management-3-numbers">Review Management: The Basics of Score, Count, and Recency</a>).</li>
<li><strong>The confusion of "everyone apologizing"</strong> — Narrow the responders to one. With everyone going around apologizing, the facts waver and the confusion accelerates. One flag only.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step, before accepting a group, is writing <strong>the "one line of the first 3 minutes"</strong> in advance. "Fact + response + time of next update" — even with the blanks empty, whether one sheet with these three frames exists changes the first three minutes entirely. As the firemen kept their matoi ready, the property keeps a flag ready. That is the smallest beginning of group response.</p>

Please design incident response to your property’s scale, structure, and insurance coverage; for incidents requiring statutory response, consult the appropriate authorities.</div>
