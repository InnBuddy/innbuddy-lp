9:T1a65,<p>山の旅の宿が預かるのは、山そのものではありません。山の「前」と「後」です。登山前の晩の過ごし方と、下山した後の時間——山の旅の記憶の大半は、実は山の中ではなく、その前後の宿で作られます。この記事は、山の入り口としての宿の設計を、数字と制度と実務から考えます。</p>

<h2>1. 数字——山は、人が多いものです</h2>
<p>環境省は2005年から、富士山の4つの登山道（吉田・須走・御殿場・富士宮）のそれぞれ八合目に赤外線カウンターを設置し、登山者数を調べています。2023年の夏期（開山日から9月10日まで）の全ルート合計は約22万1,000人、2024年は20万4,316人で、コロナ前の2019年（約23万5,000人）の87%でした。環境省が同時に伝えているのは、低体温症・高山病・ケガの救助要請が毎年相次いでいることです。山の入り口の宿は、この「20万人と遭難」の両方に接する場所にあるのです。宿の案内の質は、山の安全に直結しています。</p>

<h2>2. 登山口は、聖地の門でした</h2>
<p>日本の山の入り口には、しばしば鳥居や小さな堂が立っています。日本の山は、古くは遊びの場ではなく信仰の対象だったからです。山岳信仰と修験道の世界では、山に入ることは修行であり、登山口は聖なる領域への門でした。娯楽としての登山——山岳会やクラブ活動としての山歩き——が日本に入ってきたのは明治期以降です。この歴史は、山の入り口の宿の案内にそのまま使えます。登山口の鳥居の写真と「山に入る前の作法」の1枚カードは、ハイキングの案内を、土地の祈りの物語につなげるのです。</p>

<h2>3. 道を憲法で守る国——スイス</h2>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>6万5千キロの道網を、国が守る</strong> — スイスの標識つきハイキングコースは延べ約6万5,000キロメートル（地球一周の1.6倍に相当）。道標は1930年代に全国で整備・統一された。さらにスイスは、歩道とハイキングコースを良好で安全な状態に保つことを連邦憲法に定めた、世界でもまれな国である。整備は州と自治体の管轄で、約1,500人のボランティアが枝の伐採や道標の調整を支えている（スイス政府放送SWIの解説による）。</div>
<p>スイスの事例が教えるのは、山を資源と考える国は、山への「入口」——道と案内——にまで制度を作るということです。宿も同じといえます。山の入り口としての宿の質は、ベッドの数ではなく、<strong>道・案内・人という入口のセット</strong>で決まります。道の整備は宿の仕事ではありませんが、道を知り、案内し、話せる人は、宿の仕事なのです。</p>

<h2>4. 実務——前・朝・後の3つの時間</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>前夜</strong> — 早朝出発に合わせた朝食の相談（前夜のうちに用意するか、3時の軽食か）。弁当の注文（山の上で食べる食事は、山の記憶の主役になります）。天気とルートの情報。登山計画書の文化——行程を家族と宿に残していく登山者を、宿が褒めるだけで文化は根づきます。ヘッドライトやレインウェアの貸出。</li>
<li><strong>朝</strong> — 見送りの一言。体調の確認を挨拶に混ぜましょう（頭痛は高山病の初期症状——「顔色が優れないですね」の一言が、山での事故を止めることがあります）。</li>
<li><strong>下山後</strong> — いちばん先に用意するのは風呂です。次に欲しいのは温かい汁物。そして濡れた靴とレインウェアを一晩で乾かす乾燥室——登山客の心を掴む定番の設備です。山の道具の展示（ザイル、ランプ、古い地図）と、山の本のコーナーは、下山後の夜の話の種になります。</li>
</ul>
<p>山の話を聞ける人がいること——これが山の宿の最大の設備です。下山した夜、宿の主人がその日のルートの話（どこが混んでいたか、どこの展望が開いていたか）をできる宿は、翌日の登山者の計画まで支えます。</p>

<h2>5. 責任——入山の判断を、担う</h2>
<p>山の入り口の宿には、宿ならではの責任があります。悪天候の日には「今日は山をやめます」と言える宿であること——登山の安全管理のかなめは、入山の判断にあるのです。売上を気にして登らせる宿は、山の入口としての信頼を失います。地域の登山ガイドや山小屋との連携（天候情報、ルート情報、緊急時の連絡先）を作っておきましょう。長期天気予報と登山者の体力レベルの両方を聞いた上での助言は、宿の稼ぎ時である以上に、宿の使命です。</p>
<div class="note"><span class="tag">InnBuddy VIEW</span>山の入り口としての宿の価値を決めるのは、設備の充実ではなく、<strong>「山から帰ってくる人を見る目」</strong>です。行ってらっしゃいを言い、無事に戻った顔を見て、風呂と食事と話を用意します。山の旅の成功は、頂上ではなく下山後の宿で確定します。</div>

11:T1b55,<p>What a mountain-trip inn takes charge of is not the mountain itself. It is the "before" and the "after." The evening before the climb and the hours after the descent — most of the memory of a mountain trip is, in fact, made not on the mountain but at the inn on either side of it. This article considers the design of an inn as the entrance to the mountain, through figures, institutions, and practical work.</p>

<h2>1. The figures — mountains are full of people</h2>
<p>Since 2005, the Ministry of the Environment has counted climbers with infrared counters installed at the eighth station of each of Mt. Fuji’s four trails (Yoshida, Subashiri, Gotemba, Fujinomiya). The total over all routes in the 2023 summer season (from the mountain’s opening day to September 10) was about 221,000; in 2024 it was 204,316 — 87% of the pre-pandemic 2019 figure of about 235,000. What the ministry reports at the same time is that rescue calls for hypothermia, altitude sickness, and injuries follow one another every year. An inn at the mountain’s entrance stands at the point that touches both — the two hundred thousand, and the accidents. The quality of the inn’s guidance connects directly to safety on the mountain.</p>

<h2>2. The trailhead was once the gate of a sacred ground</h2>
<p>At the entrances to mountains in Japan, torii gates and small halls often stand. Japan’s mountains were, in the old days, not places of play but objects of faith. In the world of mountain worship and shugendō, entering the mountain was itself a discipline, and the trailhead was the gate into sacred territory. Climbing as recreation — mountaineering clubs and hill walking as a pastime — came to Japan in and after the Meiji era. This history goes straight into the guide of an inn at a mountain’s entrance. A photograph of the torii at the trailhead and a single card on "the manner of entering the mountain" connect a hiking guide to the story of the land’s prayers.</p>

<h2>3. The country that protects its trails by constitution — Switzerland</h2>
<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>A network of 65,000 kilometers of trails, protected by the nation</strong> — Switzerland’s signposted hiking routes total some 65,000 kilometers (equivalent to 1.6 times around the earth). The trail markers were put in order and standardized nationwide in the 1930s. Switzerland is, further, a country rare in the world for having written into its federal constitution the keeping of footpaths and hiking trails in good and safe condition. Maintenance lies with the cantons and municipalities, and about 1,500 volunteers support the cutting of branches and the adjusting of signposts (per the Swiss broadcaster SWI’s explanation).</div>
<p>What the Swiss case teaches is that a country that thinks of mountains as a resource builds institutions even for the "entrances" to them — the trails and the guidance. The same holds for an inn. The quality of an inn as a mountain’s entrance is decided not by the number of beds but by <strong>the set of entrances: trail, guidance, and people.</strong> Maintaining the trails is not the inn’s work; but a person who knows the trails, guides them, and can talk about them is.</p>

<h2>4. Practical work — the three times of before, morning, after</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>The night before</strong> — Consulting on breakfast for an early start (prepared the night before, or a light meal at three). Taking orders for the packed lunch (a meal eaten on the mountain becomes the leading player of the mountain’s memory). Weather and route information. The culture of the climb plan — the inn has only to praise the climbers who leave their itinerary with family and inn, and the culture takes root. Lending out headlamps and rainwear.</li>
<li><strong>The morning</strong> — A word of send-off. Fold a check of condition into the greeting (a headache is an early symptom of altitude sickness — the single remark "You don’t look well" can sometimes stop an accident on the mountain).</li>
<li><strong>After the descent</strong> — What to prepare first is the bath. What they want next is something warm to drink. And a drying room that dries wet boots and rainwear overnight — the classic piece of equipment that captures a climber’s heart. A display of mountain tools (rope, lamps, old maps) and a corner of mountain books give the night after the descent its seeds of talk.</li>
</ul>
<p>That there is someone to talk mountains with — this is the mountain inn’s greatest facility. On the night after a descent, an inn whose host can talk over that day’s route (where it was crowded, where the view opened) supports even the next day’s climbers’ plans.</p>

<h2>5. Responsibility — shouldering the judgment of entering</h2>
<p>An inn at a mountain’s entrance has a responsibility all its own. On days of bad weather, to be an inn that can say "today we call off the mountain" — the linchpin of climbing safety management lies in the judgment of entering. An inn that sends climbers up out of worry for revenue loses its credibility as the mountain’s entrance. Build working ties with the local mountain guides and mountain huts (weather information, route information, emergency contacts). Advice given only after hearing both the long-range forecast and the climber’s fitness is, more than the inn’s busy season, the inn’s mission.</p>
<div class="note"><span class="tag">InnBuddy VIEW</span>What decides the value of an inn as a mountain’s entrance is not the fullness of its facilities but <strong>the eye that watches people come back from the mountain.</strong> Say go, watch the faces return safe, and prepare the bath, the meal, and the talk. The success of a mountain trip is fixed not at the summit but at the inn after the descent.</div>

<div class="note">This article is intended for sharing ideas. Cases and figures are estimates as of writing, based on public materials. In implementing, design under each inn’s own judgment, including safety, laws and regulations, and consideration for the neighborhood. For activities in the mountains and in the snow season, include checks of weather and equipment in the design.</div>
