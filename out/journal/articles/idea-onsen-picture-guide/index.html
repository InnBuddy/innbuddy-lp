温泉は、日本の文化の中でも「言葉で説明しにくいもの」の筆頭です。洗い場で体を洗うこと、湯船がみんなのものであること、タオルを湯に入れないこと——これらはどれも、日本で育った人には説明するまでもない空気ですが、初めての人には想像すら難しいのです。だからこそ、温泉の案内は「絵」の実力がいちばん試される場所です。

温泉地2,839か所、「ある」だけでは選ばれません

環境省の「温泉利用状況」によると、2025年3月末時点の温泉地数は全国で2,839か所、源泉の総数は2万7,899です。温泉地の数は2019年度から6年連続で減っており、この6年間で132か所減りました。源泉が最も多いのは大分県の5,094か所で、湧出量も全国1位です。数がゆっくり減っているという事実は、宿にとってこう読み替えられます。温泉は「ある」だけでは選ばれません。温泉地ごとの説明と体験の質が、これからの差になります。絵の案内は、その最も手堅い投資です。

手順が建築に刻まれた浴場 — ローマのテルマエ

2,000年前のローマの公衆浴場（テルマエ）の入浴は、手順の連続でした。アポディテリウム（脱衣所）で衣服を脱ぎ、パライストラ（運動場）で体を動かし、テピダリウム（温浴室）で熱に慣らし、カルダリウム（高温浴室）で本格的に温まり、フリギダリウム（冷浴室）で締めます。この順番は、浴場の建物の部屋の配置として、そのまま刻まれています。入浴者は廊下を進むだけで、正しい順序を通過するように建てられていたのです。

なぜこの話が面白いのでしょうか。巨大な浴場に案内板が一枚もなくても、建物そのものが案内板だったからです。本質は「説明とは動線の設計である」ということ。日本の温泉にも同じことが言えます。靴を脱ぐ所→脱衣所→洗い場→湯船。この動線のそれぞれに絵を置けば、説明は建物が運んでくれます。壁に一枚「絵の案内板」を飾るだけでは、順番は伝わりません。順に、進みながら出会う形に置きましょう。ここが設計の核心です。

絵で伝える技術は、東京生まれ

「言葉に頼らず絵で伝える」ことの国際的な系譜は、意外なところにあります。1964年の東京オリンピックです。この大会で、絵文字による標識「ピクトグラム」がオリンピックで初めて本格導入されました。勝見勝らの指揮の下、若手のクリエーターたちが施設案内用の絵文字を39種類作り、その著作権を放棄してまで普及を優先したといいます。以後、ピクトグラムはメキシコ大会以降の五輪や万博、世界中の空港に広がり、トイレの男女マークの世界標準もこの流れの中で生まれました。

つまり「絵で伝える」ことは、日本が国際社会に持ち込んだ発明です。温泉の絵の案内は、その発明の延長線上にあります。絵は翻訳ではなく、すでに世界標準になった言語なのです。

作法の核 — 湯船は「みんなの湯」です

絵にするべき順番を具体的に並べると、こうなります。①靴を脱ぐ ②脱衣所で服を脱ぐ（タオルを一枚持っていく）③洗い場で体を洗う ④かけ湯で体を湯に慣らす ⑤湯船に入る（タオルは頭に載せるか、湯船の外へ）⑥上がったら体を拭いてから脱衣所へ。この6枚のうち、いちばん伝えたい本質は③と⑤の関係です。日本の温泉の湯船は、洗った体で入る「みんなの湯」です。洗い場と湯船は別の部屋ではなく「順番」でつながっている——これが絵で伝われば、作法はルールではなく理屈として理解されます。

数字で伝えるものも決めておきます。源泉の温度（例：42℃）、泉質の名前、pH、そして「源泉かけ流し」か「循環」か。硫黄の匂いは「湯の説明書」の一部です。飲用できる湯は限られます——飲める場合は「飲用可」の表示がある場所だけ、という一行も添えます。

タトゥーの説明は、一枚のカードで

海外ゲストの入浴で避けて通れないのがタトゥーの話です。観光庁は2016年3月、「入れ墨（タトゥー）がある外国人旅行者の入浴に際し留意すべきポイントと対応事例」を示し、シール等で覆う方法や衛生的な入浴着での対応などを例示しました。対応は施設ごとに異なり、ルールを明示する宿、カバーシールで入浴できる宿、貸切風呂を案内する宿があります。大事なのは、どの選択であれ「説明すること」です。ルールが分からないゲストは、トラブルを起こす前に黙って去ります。当宿の考えを絵と英語で一枚にして、予約の時点で見える場所（サイトの写真、予約確認メール）に置きましょう。それが「説明のない温泉」から抜け出す最短のコースです。

熱い湯との付き合い方

  • のぼせ — 長く浸かるより、数回に分けて入ります。湯上がりに水を飲める場所を、絵と一緒に案内しましょう。
  • 温度 — 「熱い」は感覚なので、温度は数字で表示します。熱い源泉には、少しずつ慣らす旨の一行を添えましょう。
  • 滑りと子ども — 洗い場は濡れて滑ります。子どもから目を離さない旨は、家族にこそ伝わる絵で。
  • 体調 — 飲酒後の入浴は避けましょう。持病のある方は無理をしないという一般の注意を添えます（医学的な効能の主張はしない）。
  • 向く宿・向かない宿 — 大浴場・露天風呂を持つすべての宿で絵の案内は機能します。作れない宿はほぼありません。あるのは「まだ並べていない」だけです。

Among the elements of Japanese culture, the onsen (hot spring) is at the top of the list of “things hard to explain in words.” Washing your body at the washing area, the bath being for everyone, not putting your towel in the water — to people who grew up in Japan these are all part of the air, needing no explanation, but for first-timers they are hard even to imagine. That is exactly why onsen guidance is the place where the true power of “pictures” is tested most.

2,839 hot-spring areas: simply “being there” is not enough to be chosen

According to the Ministry of the Environment’s “Hot Spring Use Status,” as of the end of March 2025, there were 2,839 hot-spring areas nationwide and 27,899 hot-spring sources in total. The number of hot-spring areas has fallen for six consecutive years since fiscal 2019, a decrease of 132 over those six years. Oita Prefecture has the most sources, at 5,094, and is also first in the nation in flow volume. The fact that the numbers are slowly falling can be reread this way for an inn. An onsen is not chosen simply because it is “there.” The quality of each hot-spring area’s explanation and experience will make the difference from now on. Picture guidance is the most reliable investment in that.

Baths with the sequence carved into the architecture — the Roman thermae

Bathing in the public baths (thermae) of Rome 2,000 years ago was a series of steps. Undress in the apodyterium (changing room), exercise in the palaestra (exercise ground), get used to the heat in the tepidarium (warm room), warm up properly in the caldarium (hot room), and finish in the frigidarium (cold room). This order is carved, as it is, into the layout of the rooms in the bath buildings. They were built so that bathers passed through the correct sequence simply by walking along the corridors.

Why is this story interesting? Because even without a single sign in a huge bathhouse, the building itself was the sign. The essence is that “explanation is the design of circulation.” The same can be said of Japanese onsen. Where you take off your shoes → the changing room → the washing area → the bath. Place a picture at each point along this route, and the building will carry the explanation for you. Merely hanging one “picture guide board” on a wall does not convey the order. Place them so that guests encounter them in sequence as they move forward. This is the heart of the design.

The craft of conveying with pictures was born in Tokyo

The international lineage of “conveying with pictures without relying on words” lies in an unexpected place: the 1964 Tokyo Olympics. At these Games, signs made of picture symbols, “pictograms,” were introduced in earnest for the first time at an Olympics. Under the direction of Katsumi Masaru and others, young creators made 39 types of picture symbols for facility guidance, and it is said they even gave up their copyright to prioritize their spread. From then on, pictograms spread to the Olympics from the Mexico City Games onward, to world expos, and to airports around the world, and the world standard for the men’s and women’s restroom symbols was also born from this current.

In other words, “conveying with pictures” is an invention Japan brought to the international community. Onsen picture guides lie on the extension of that invention. Pictures are not translation but a language that has already become a world standard.

The core of the manners — the bath is “everyone’s water”

Laid out concretely, the sequence to put into pictures is this. ① Take off your shoes ② Undress in the changing room (take one towel with you) ③ Wash your body at the washing area ④ Pour hot water over yourself (kakeyu) to get your body used to the water ⑤ Get into the bath (put the towel on your head or outside the bath) ⑥ After getting out, dry your body before going into the changing room. Of these six pictures, the essence most worth conveying is the relationship between ③ and ⑤. The bath of a Japanese onsen is “everyone’s water,” entered with a washed body. The washing area and the bath are not separate rooms but are connected by “order” — if this gets across through pictures, the manners are understood not as rules but as logic.

Also decide what to convey with numbers. The temperature of the source (e.g., 42°C), the name of the spring type, the pH, and whether it is “gensen kakenagashi” (free-flowing from the source) or “recirculated.” The smell of sulfur is part of “the water’s instruction manual.” Water that can be drunk is limited — add a line saying that if drinking is possible, it is only at places displaying “safe to drink.”

Explain tattoos with a single card

The topic of tattoos is unavoidable in bathing by overseas guests. In March 2016, the Japan Tourism Agency issued “Points to Note and Examples of Responses Regarding Bathing by Foreign Travelers with Tattoos,” giving examples such as covering them with stickers and responding with hygienic bathing wear. Responses differ by facility: there are inns that state their rules clearly, inns where guests can bathe with cover stickers, and inns that guide guests to private baths. What matters, whatever the choice, is “explaining.” Guests who do not understand the rules leave silently before causing trouble. Put the inn’s position on a single sheet with pictures and English, and place it where it can be seen at the time of booking (photos on the website, booking confirmation emails). That is the shortest route out of “an onsen without explanation.”

How to get along with hot water

  • Overheating — Rather than soaking for a long time, bathe in several rounds. Show, with a picture, the place where guests can drink water after bathing.
  • Temperature — “Hot” is a sensation, so display the temperature as a number. For hot sources, add a line advising guests to get used to the water gradually.
  • Slipping and children — The washing area is wet and slippery. Convey “Do not take your eyes off children” with a picture that gets through to families in particular.
  • Physical condition — Avoid bathing after drinking alcohol. Add, as a general caution, that people with chronic illnesses should not push themselves (make no claims of medical efficacy).
  • Inns it suits, inns it doesn’t — Picture guidance works at every inn with a large communal bath or open-air bath. There are almost no inns that cannot make them. There are only inns that “have not lined them up yet.”

What onsen picture guidance really is: the design of circulation and the power of pictures that have become a world standard. In the Roman baths, the building itself was the guidance. The Tokyo Olympics made pictures the world’s common language. Start small: line up six pictures from the entrance to the bath, in the order guests move through. That alone turns the onsen into “a place without a language barrier.”

  • 图片引导板 — 创建一个引导牌，用图文并茂的方式解释如何进入温泉。如果按顺序介绍图画，比如在盥洗区洗澡的图片、进入浴缸的图片、从浴缸出来的图片，即使字数很少，也会更容易传达信息。一家旅馆用图片来说明如何在果田享受露天浴池，并以“如何在果田享受露天浴池”的方式进行介绍。海滨度假胜地的游泳池指南等通俗易懂的图解指南深受海外客人的喜爱。
