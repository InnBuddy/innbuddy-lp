11:T1e5c,<p>Corporate contract guests are <strong>not "a discount" but "the design of weekly regulars."</strong> One company signed, and one to two rooms a week stay filled for three months. Single training camps and retreats were handled in the meetings and banquets article; the corporate contract is another business again. <strong>The same faces come back, week after week.</strong> This article is the foundation for designing these weekly regulars.</p>

<h2>The kyakuchō — remembering as management, Edo style</h2>

<p>Edo merchants and wholesalers kept ledgers in which they wrote down their trading partners: the <strong>okyakuchō</strong> — a customer register (at wholesale houses also called the kyakusenchō). The name of each counterpart, the goods begun, the flow of payments. To a merchant house, a customer not written in the ledger was not yet "our customer."</p>

<p>In the world of high-end restaurants, this became human memory. The head waiter memorized the regulars’ preferences and had the tea at the right temperature before they even stepped up to the room — the luxury catering tea houses of the Bunka–Bunsei era (1804–1830) flourished as places where the domains’ Edo-residence stewards and powerful merchants came as <strong>regulars</strong>. Being remembered was trust; remembering was the heart of the business. The true identity of the kizuki (noticing) that Edo service prides to the world is, in fact, this <strong>culture of the customer ledger</strong>.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Edo-period merchant houses and wholesalers kept "okyakuchō" ledgers recording their trading partners (also "kyakusenchō" at wholesale houses; examples survive in Edo-period merchant documents). The luxury catering tea houses of the Bunka–Bunsei era flourished on shogunal officials, domains’ rusui stewards, and powerful merchants as their regulars</strong> — Writing "who, what, when" was a basic manner of Edo business.</div>

<h2>3 corporate demands</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Nearby construction and projects</strong> — Dams, roads, wind-power maintenance: people on site for months at a time. Monthly stays belong to the long-stay article, but "spending half the week here" as the project’s base property is demand just as common.</li>
<li><strong>Sales and maintenance rounds</strong> — People covering the same area on the same weekday every week. The person whose territory is "this area, every Tuesday" stays every Tuesday. The purest form of the weekly regular.</li>
<li><strong>Corporate contracts for remote workers</strong> — Twice a week, working in the lounge and staying the night. The background of demand that makes a property "a place to work" is handled in the workation article.</li>
</ol>

<h2>3 points of design</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Price — quote weekly and monthly</strong> — Not per night: from ¥15,000 for two nights a week, from ¥50,000 for eight nights a month (reference values). What the company’s person in charge can set up is a budget, not a night count. The weekly price is chosen for one reason alone: it is easy to budget (the foundations of price are in <a href="#pricing-fundamentals">How to Set Room Rates: Pricing Fundamentals</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Billing — month-end lump sum, by bank transfer</strong> — Not personal settlement out of pocket. Bill all at once at month’s end, and receive by transfer. This is the modern, safe form of the business by which Edo merchants stacked trust on kake credit. Kake, though, always carries the risk of bad debt. That belongs to the other side.</li>
<li><strong>Room — keep the same room ready</strong> — The joy of the weekly regular is having "my own room." If the same room is not free, then the same type: "we have prepared your usual type of room." This is the heart of the customer ledger.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The modern kyakuchō — what to write</h2>

<p>What belongs in the contract file is not the company’s name but <strong>the person</strong>. The contact’s name. The preferred room and the room tea (coffee, or green tea). The time of day they arrive. Breakfast or not. The car’s plate number. With just this one line, from the second stay onward, the service becomes something different from the first. The manners of what to record and how to use it are handled by <a href="#guest-data-basics">Guest Data Basics: What to Record, and How to Use It</a>. The corporate contract file is, among these, the warmest ledger of all — one specialized to "the same person coming back."</p>

<h2>Telling — one PDF</h2>

<ul>
<li>Put the "weekly plan for corporate guests" on a single sheet: prices (weekly and monthly), billing method, the same-room promise, and nearby attractions (a understated café, coworking, a walking path).</li>
<li>Where to circulate it: the general affairs and accounting windows of nearby companies, and the project site offices. A one-sheet PDF is a form easy to pass around inside a company.</li>
<li>One company signed, and one to two rooms a week stay filled for three months. That this is a business with a different number of sales calls also goes in one line of the flyer.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other side — when contracts exhaust the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Selling the discount cheap</strong> — Once a contract price goes down, it does not come back up. A relationship begun as "a discount" loses its reason to exist the night the night-count drops. Write in the flyer that the price is for the convenience of the week, not a markdown.</li>
<li><strong>Bad debt</strong> — The month-end lump sum is convenient, but the property carries the risk of non-collection. Start the first contract short — one to two months. Trust is something to extend after payment records have stacked up.</li>
<li><strong>Regulars’ presumptions</strong> — Make "the usual room" an absolute designation, and a full-house night scorches the relationship. Write into the first flyer the room to receive with "the same type."</li>
<li><strong>Fixing the weekday tilt</strong> — A mechanism that fills weekdays while shaving high-priced weekends defeats itself. The peak-season acceptance cap also goes in one line of the contract.</li>
<li><strong>Verbal promises</strong> — Start with just "bill me at month’s end for whatever I stay," and months pass unsettled. At least one sheet — even three lines: price, billing, termination.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is delivering one sheet to one nearby company. And on the second stay, can you call the contact by name? "Welcome back, Mr./Ms. ○○." When you can say that one line, the first line of your property’s kyakuchō is written. A corporate contract is not the setting of a discount — it is <strong>designing a relationship that can be remembered, week by week</strong>.</p>

12:T1a29,<p>企业合同客户<strong>“每周常客设计”而不是“折扣”</strong>。许多旅馆将公司合同视为“公司折扣”，但从不同的角度来看，公司合同是一种创造填补工作日的“每周常客”的机制。靠近时尚建筑或有很酷咖啡馆的住宿成为每周住一到两次的企业客户的“第二个家”。一旦选定一家公司，三个月内每周将填满一到两个房间。本文将整理如何将企业合同设计为“每周定期”而不是折扣。</p>

<h2>1. 谁入住公司酒店？企业需求的三类</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>附近建设/项目——来山/海/自然景点的人：</strong>当您靠近山脉、海洋或大自然时，就会有人来到您的社区进行建筑或项目。这些人在该地区停留数月，从事水坝建设、道路建设、风力发电维护、渔业研究等工作。他们正在寻找一个可以在大自然中放松的住宿场所，而不是廉价的商务酒店。</li>
<li><strong>销售/维护 — 每周同一天来一次的人员：</strong>附近一家公司的人每周都会在同一天来一次进行销售或维护。这些人定期来，例如该地区的每周二和该地区的每周四。他们每周都住在同一个住处，把它当作第二个家。</li>
<li><strong>远程员工的公司合同——联合办公的替代方案：</strong>由于公司合同，一些远程员工使用旅馆作为联合办公的替代品。我每周两次在酒店的休息室工作并过夜。旅馆不仅仅是一个住宿的地方，也是一个工作的地方。远程员工的企业合同是企业的新需求。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. 设计三点——每周思考</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>价格 - 将价格显示为“每周 XX 日元/每月 XX 日元起”，而不是每晚的价格（参考值/示例）：</strong>公司合同的价格按周或按月显示，而不是按晚显示。例如：每周 2 晚 15,000 日元起，每月 8 晚 50,000 日元起，等等。公司发现通过每周或每月显示预算更容易制定预算。如果你看每晚的价格，会觉得很贵，但如果你看每周的价格，就会感觉很划算。</li>
<li><strong>计费——月底一次性计费/转账（非个人付款）：</strong>公司合同的账单将在每月月底进行计费和转账。如果客人单独付费（客人提前向公司收费），客人的负担会增加。通过在月底一次性开具发票和付款，对双方都有好处：客人不必提前付款，记账可以一次处理完毕。</li>
<li><strong>房间——尽可能保持同一个房间（感觉就像是你自己的房间）：</strong>我们尽可能为公司合同的常客提供相同的房间。入住同一间客房后，客人会感觉这是自己的房间。拥有“自己的房间”的感觉会增加重复率。如果没有相同的房间，我们会准备一个相同类型的房间，并说：“我们已经准备了一个普通类型的房间。”</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 传达——一位导游和街区的魅力</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>在一份名为“企业每周计划”的 PDF 中：</strong>有关公司合同的信息在一份 PDF 中总结为“公司每周计划”。包括价格（周/月）、计费方式（月底一次性计费）、房间（同房）、周边景点（酷咖啡馆、时尚建筑、联合办公、农业体验）。通过将信息合并到一个 PDF 中，公司人员可以更轻松地在公司内部传递信息。</li>
<li><strong>附近很酷的咖啡馆、时尚建筑（联合办公）和农业体验的套装指南：</strong>对于企业合同信息，我们不仅会提供住宿信息，还会提供附近的咖啡馆、时尚建筑（联合办公）和农业体验信息。企业客户不仅看重住宿，更看重周边环境。通过提供附近景点的全面指南，企业客户会觉得他们想在该地区工作。</li>
<li><strong>一旦选定一家公司，三个月内每周将填满一到两个房间：</strong>对于企业合同，一旦确定一家公司，三个月内每周都会住满一到两个房间。通过与一家公司签订企业合同，工作日入住率可以显着提高。企业合同不是“折扣”，而是“填补工作日工作时间的常规设计”。与第一家公司获得企业合同是迈向企业合同的第一步。</li>
</ul>
