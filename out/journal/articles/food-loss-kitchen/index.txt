1b:T1e3e,<p>Food loss is <strong>not only "mottainai" (what a waste) but "a leak of gross profit."</strong> Satoyama vegetables, fish from the sea, the mountain’s gifts — prepping too much and throwing it away loses the respect for producers and the gross profit at once. This article is the foundation of the mechanism for making the kitchen’s prep volume visible and reducing it (the design of how to communicate it, and turning mottainai into a world word, are handled by <a href="#zero-waste-communication">Is Zero Waste Really Possible? Communicating Sustainable Operations</a>).</p>

<h2>Prep is a bet on tomorrow</h2>

<p>What happens in the evening kitchen is cooking preparation — and at the same time, <strong>a bet on "how many come tomorrow."</strong> This bet has asymmetric rules. A shortage can be recovered with extra prep the next morning. But an excess becomes garbage, losing gross profit, disposal costs, and the producers’ faces, all at once. Prep, in other words, is not "more is safer"; <strong>betting in the direction of "slightly less, and correct" is the rational play</strong>.</p>

<p>Japan has the wisdom of "hara hachibu" — eight parts full. Staying healthy at 80% of what you could eat: much of life’s wisdom warns against excess rather than shortage. The same principle works for prep. <strong>Prep at eight parts; make up the rest in the morning.</strong> That is the foundation of this article.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">FACT</span><strong>Japan’s food loss is estimated at about 4.72 million tons in fiscal 2022 (2.36 million tons from households, 2.36 million from businesses), in the publication (2024) by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry and Fisheries, the Ministry of the Environment, and the Consumer Affairs Agency. The Food Loss Reduction Promotion Act, enforced in 2019, makes reduction a national movement for businesses and consumers together</strong> — A property’s kitchen sits inside that 2.36 million tons of business-side loss.</div>

<h2>The Garot law for one inn — the story of visibility changing law</h2>

<p>France has a history of a country changing food waste by law. The trigger was one "look" by Arash Derambarsh, a municipal councillor of Courbevoie, near Paris. The movement he spread — sharing what supermarkets looked like when waste was kept invisible, the locked dumpsters, the food being discarded — grew nationwide, and in 2016 the world’s first law banning food waste, the Garot law, was enacted.</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>France’s Garot law (2016): supermarkets over 400 square meters in total floor area are banned from discarding unsold food, obligated to donate it to food banks and the like, with fines defined for violations. Doggy bags at restaurants also expanded from an initial recommendation to an obligation on all restaurants</strong> — The starting point was one councillor "seeing" the waste. What is invisible is not measured, and does not change.</div>

<p>What this law teaches is not the content of the regulation but that <strong>"waste exists only once it is written down."</strong> A property kitchen’s whiteboard is, so to speak, a one-inn Garot law. Writing one line each day of what was thrown out — that small institution is the first step that changes behavior.</p>

<h2>Measuring — 3 manners</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Write what and how much you threw out, every day</strong> — On the kitchen whiteboard, what was discarded that day. The rule is "right beside where you discard." A distant board does not get written.</li>
<li><strong>Write it as "what share of prep," not weight</strong> — "20% of the vegetables," "10% of the fish." The heaviness of prep that weight does not show becomes visible as a share.</li>
<li><strong>In one week, identify the single most-discarded dish</strong> — Improvement starts from one dish. Not the whole at once.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Reducing — 3 mechanisms</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Split the prep</strong> — Prep in two rounds, morning and noon, with the morning light. Add after seeing the lunch reservations. The first move in dividing the bet small.</li>
<li><strong>Design for reuse</strong> — Carry leftover vegetables into the next day’s soup or breakfast’s small bowl. Write it on the menu not as "leftovers" but as "prep that uses the whole field," and it becomes story (the craft of adding story to a dish is in <a href="#idea-breakfast-local-story">Convey Region with Breakfast - Ideas for Breakfast Putting Regional Story on One Dish</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Link prep to bookings</strong> — Ten bookings, 2 kg of vegetables; twenty, 4 kg: build a correspondence table of bookings and prep volume. A mechanism that puts information into the bet.</li>
</ul>

<p>Waste is a negative line in the world of gross profit. The breakfast numbers’ calculation is handled by <a href="#breakfast-revenue-math">Breakfast Revenue: The Math of Guests x Price x Add-on</a>, and the display discipline that cuts over-laying by <a href="#breakfast-buffet-media">Turning Breakfast Into Media: Buffet Design</a>. The three articles together guard gross profit from three directions.</p>

<h2>The other side — when reducing hurts the floor</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The boundary of hygiene</strong> — Reuse presupposes temperature and time control. Follow food-hygiene guidance, and write the judgment criteria in advance.</li>
<li><strong>The experience of sold-out</strong> — Prep at eight parts increases "sold out." Can you explain it — "Today’s portion ends with what we prepared; please understand it is the field’s ways"? Still, continuous stock-outs eventually lower ratings. Eight parts, carefully.</li>
<li><strong>Records becoming a shell</strong> — Fix the writer on one person, and on that person’s day off the record disappears. Keep it in a form anyone can write one line of.</li>
<li><strong>A hole in the bookings link</strong> — Same-day cancellations and walk-ins always move. Do not over-trust the table; keep the morning’s extra-prep move alive.</li>
<li><strong>The floor that hides numbers</strong> — Writing "discarded" takes courage. The moment records are used for scolding, the floor starts hiding them. Say at the start: the whiteboard is an information tool, not a scolding tool.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is five minutes after close. Today’s prepped volume and today’s discarded volume, as two lines on the whiteboard. Keep it up a week, and the dish your property bets most heavily on will surely appear. Reducing food loss is not a matter of willpower — it advances with <strong>records and small institutions</strong>.</p>
