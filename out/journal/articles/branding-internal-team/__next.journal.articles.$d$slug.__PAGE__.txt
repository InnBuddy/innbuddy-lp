11:T1a83,<p>What a property aims for shows, before any logo or photograph, in <strong>one sentence on the morning floor</strong>. A brand reaches guests only after it reaches the staff — physically, words travel only through people. This article designs the <strong>brand for the staff (the internal brand)</strong>, not for guests, on a single sheet of paper.</p>

<h2>The family precept: not on the wall, but in the breast</h2>

<p>The merchants of Ōmi left, as family precepts, the questions for moments of wavering judgment. Sanpō yoshi — good for the seller, good for the buyer, good for the world. Before "does it pay," the question was whether a course of action stood on all three. And another: "duty first, profit after" (sen-i kō-ri). A family precept was not the proprietor’s declaration of resolve but <strong>a map opened at the forks of judgment</strong>. When no one was watching, the merchant decided the trade by the one line in his breast.</p>

<h2>The sheet at the chest — the credo card</h2>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>The Ritz-Carlton condenses its management philosophy into the "Gold Standards," and every employee carries a "credo card" at the chest</strong> — To be looked at again in moments of wavering action. The motto: "We are ladies and gentlemen serving ladies and gentlemen." Read-alouds open every shift, and forums exist where employees debate and improve the philosophy. (Per the company’s public materials and commentary.)</div>

<p>Why it is interesting: the philosophy is not "posted" but <strong>"issued to carry."</strong> A philosophy on the wall fades each time you pass it; a philosophy in the breast can be opened at the moment of judgment. Then the read-alouds — one minute a day, in voice. Then the debates — making it one’s own, not something handed down. The Ōmi merchant who slipped the family precept into his breast and the modern hotelier who slips the card into a breast pocket are doing the same thing. <strong>The internal brand is a standard that lets people judge when no one is watching</strong> — the one line by which the staff of a front desk can decide on their own, on a night the owner is away.</p>

<h2>Translating it to a property — 1 sentence and 3 questions</h2>

<p>A small property’s credo needs no more than one sheet. On the front, <strong>one sentence of "what we aim for."</strong> On the back, <strong>3 questions of judgment</strong>. The sentence, in your property’s own words ("a property where you taste coffee brewed in mountain water, amid nature"). The three questions are the modern translation of sanpō yoshi.</p>

<ol>
<li>Is it good for the guest?</li>
<li>As your own work, can you be proud of it?</li>
<li>Ten years from now, could you still say this judgment aloud?</li>
</ol>

<p>Any response that cannot stand on the three questions, decline. Any response that stands, proceed without waiting for the manager’s confirmation. A small property’s strength is that <strong>speed of judgment can be guaranteed by one sheet</strong>.</p>

<h2>3 faces of telling — words, experience, the everyday</h2>

<ol>
<li><strong>Tell with words</strong> — The "aim" sayable in one sentence. Make it a sentence the staff can say as is. Difficult words, rewritten into words the newest member can say most easily.</li>
<li><strong>Tell with experience</strong> — The experience that works best is <strong>having the staff stay at the property themselves</strong>. The room’s sounds, the bath’s temperature, the morning light — a property one has not experienced cannot be spoken of.</li>
<li><strong>Tell in the everyday</strong> — One minute of the daily meeting. Today’s one line, aloud ("today, let’s add a word about mountain vegetables at dinner"). Past one minute, the message stops arriving.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Operation — at entry, and for the long run</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Hand it over at hiring</strong> — Tell the one sentence in the interview and hire the people it resonates with. Hand the card on the first day. Slipping it into the breast on day one is the most natural moment.</li>
<li><strong>Put one line into evaluation</strong> — "Did they make judgments that stood on the three questions?" A philosophy not in the evaluation disappears in a busy month.</li>
<li><strong>Every shift, one minute</strong> — The same manner as the Ritz-Carlton. The read-aloud continues because it is one minute.</li>
</ul>

<h2>The other side — 5 traps of the internal brand</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Using the owner’s words as they are</strong> — The more splendid the sentence, the worse it sits in the floor’s mouth. A sentence hard to say, rewrite in the floor’s words.</li>
<li><strong>Becoming a shell</strong> — A credo no one remembers: take it down from the wall once. A forgotten philosophy becomes a lie the moment it is framed.</li>
<li><strong>Multilingual staff</strong> — A sentence that cannot be translated into the mother tongue is not the real thing. Search together for the sentence that can be.</li>
<li><strong>Confusing it with the manual</strong> — The credo is the reason for judgment; the manual is procedure (the territory of <a href="#service-standard-basics">Service Basics: The "Standard" That Does Not Vary by Person</a>). Mix them, and both break.</li>
<li><strong>Over-telling</strong> — Past three minutes of the morning meeting, the value of the one minute vanishes.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is writing one sentence tonight. You need show it to no one. <strong>Read it aloud at tomorrow’s morning meeting, and see whether it fits your mouth</strong> — confirm only that. A sentence that fits the mouth enters the staff’s breast. A sentence entered in the breast eventually reaches the guest’s ear.</p>

<li><strong>如果员工不知道自己在做什么，他们就会对自己提供的服务感到困惑：</strong>如果旅馆的目标（例如，“在大自然中可以享用山水冲泡的咖啡的旅馆”）没有传达给员工，他们将不知道自己在做什么，并对他们提供的服务感到困惑。示例：不同的员工以不同的方式冲泡咖啡、客户服务人员使用不同的语言等。</li>
<li><strong>员工能否为自己的工作感到自豪将会导致留任：</strong>当员工了解旅馆的目标时，他们就会为自己的工作感到自豪。例如：“我致力于提供用山水冲泡的咖啡”或“我致力于制作一杯可以在大自然中享用的咖啡。”当您对自己的工作感到自豪时，您的员工就会留下来。</li>
<li><strong>品牌取决于员工是否可以与客人交谈：</strong>如果旅馆的目标传达给员工，那么员工就能够向客人传达旅馆的目标。例如：“这种咖啡是用山水冲泡的”，“这种咖啡在大自然中品尝时味道更好。”品牌通过员工的言语传达给客人。</li>
</ul>

<h2>2. 如何沟通——3种沟通方式</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>用一句话表达“我们的目标”：</strong>用一句话描述旅馆的目标是什么。例如：“可以在大自然中享用山水冲泡的咖啡的旅馆”、“通过食物讲述海盐的故事和山的恩赐的旅馆”等。如果能用一句话说出来，工作人员就更容易记住。</li>
<li><strong>通过经验进行沟通——我们员工的经验：</strong>工作人员会通过他们的经验告诉您旅馆的目标。例如：体验员工用山水冲泡咖啡、体验员工制作海盐等。通过体验，员工可以身体力行地了解自己的目标。</li>
<li><strong>日常生活中的沟通——日常会议中的 1 分钟：</strong>我们会在每日例会上用一分钟的时间传达旅馆的目标。例如，在晨会上，可以这样说：“今天，让我们集中精力在大自然中享受山水冲泡的咖啡。”通过每天沟通一分钟，员工就会意识到自己每天的“目标”。</li>
</ol>

<h2>3. 运营——把你的内部品牌变成一种“文化”</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>招聘时沟通：</strong>我们会告诉您旅馆在招聘时的目标是什么。在采访中说“我们的旅馆的目标是成为一家可以在大自然中享用山水冲泡的咖啡的旅馆”，您就可以雇用与您的目标有共鸣的人。</li>
<li><strong>纳入评价：</strong>将旅馆的目标纳入您的评估中。例如：“我们是否能够提供用山水冲泡的咖啡？”“我们是否能够创造出一杯可以在大自然中享用的咖啡？”以目标作为评估的基础。</li>
<li><strong>内部品牌成为“文化”：</strong>通过将旅馆的目标传达给员工并让他们每天意识到这一点，内部品牌就成为一种“文化”。当谈到文化时，员工自然会按照他们的目标去做。文化不是以手册的形式传播，而是以氛围的形式传播。</li>
</ul>
