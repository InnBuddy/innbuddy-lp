— ミニバーは信任から始まった</h2><p>ミニバーの歴史は、思ったより新しいもので、その始まり方も予想と違います。1974年、香港ヒルトンの飲食部門の責任者ロバート・アーノルドは、スイス行きの飛行機で出された小さなウイスキーの瓶に着想を得て、全客室に小型冷蔵庫を設置します。水、ソフトドリンク、チョコレート、ナッツ、小瓶の酒——客室内の飲料売上は約5倍、宿全体の売上も約5%増えたと伝わります(当時の報告値)。</p><div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>1974年、香港ヒルトンが全客室にミニバーを導入。客室内飲料売上は約5倍、宿全体の売上も約5%増と伝わる(当時の報告値)。注目すべきは開始当初の精算方法——フロントでの自己申告だった</strong> — ミニバーは「監視」でなく「信任」から始まった仕組み</div><p>売れたのは何だったのでしょう。水ではありません。<strong>「深夜に、誰にも会わずに一杯」という便益</strong>です。その後の歩みも含めて読むと、本質が見えてきます。ミニバーの品はやがて市価の数倍に値付けされ(3〜4倍という試算もあります)、点検と品出しの人件費がかさみ、ゲストの不信を買って——世界のホテルチェーンは、ミニバーを空の冷蔵庫に置き換えていきました。つまり、<strong>ミニバーは商品の値段で成り立っていたのではなく、便益と信頼で成り立っていた。そのどちらかを崩したとき、死んだ</strong>のです。</p><h2>翻訳 — 1974年の原点から始める</h2><p>この歴史が、小さな宿への処方箋になります。室内コマースを作るなら、衰退期の常識(品を置いて高く売る)ではなく、出発点の常識(その土地の品を、良心的な値段で、信任で)から始める。</p><ol><li><strong>その地域でしか買えないものを置く</strong> — 1b:T1e80,<p>The minibar and in-room sales are <strong>not "a vending machine" but "in-room commerce."</strong> A vending machine is business that works only between the person inserting coins and the machine dispensing goods. But the marketplace called the guest room has no machine in it. <strong>The person who placed the goods is not there.</strong> This article is the foundation for designing business that stands on this special condition.</p>

<h2>1974, Hong Kong — the minibar began with trust</h2>

<p>The minibar’s history is newer than most imagine, and it began differently too. In 1974, Robert Arnold, food and beverage director of the Hong Kong Hilton, took his inspiration from the small whiskey bottles served on his flight to Switzerland, and installed compact refrigerators in every room. Water, soft drinks, chocolate, nuts, small bottles of alcohol — in-room beverage sales are said to have risen about fivefold, and the whole hotel’s revenue by about 5% (figures reported at the time).</p>

<div class="stats"><span class="tag">CASE</span><strong>In 1974, the Hong Kong Hilton introduced minibars in all rooms. In-room beverage sales reportedly rose about fivefold, and the hotel’s total revenue by about 5% (figures reported at the time). What deserves attention is how payment worked at the start — self-declaration at the front desk</strong> — The minibar began not from "surveillance" but from trust.</div>

<p>What sold? Not the water. <strong>The benefit of "a late-night drink, meeting no one."</strong> Read together with what followed, the essence comes into view. Minibar goods came to be priced at multiples of street prices (estimates of 3–4× exist), restocking and checking labor piled up, guests’ distrust was bought — and the world’s hotel chains replaced the minibar with an empty refrigerator. Which is to say: <strong>the minibar stood not on the price of goods but on benefit and trust. The moment either broke, it died.</strong></p>

<h2>Translation — start from the 1974 origin</h2>

<p>This history becomes a prescription for small properties. If you build in-room commerce, start not from the declining-era convention (place goods, price them high) but from the starting-point convention (local goods, honest prices, on trust).</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>Place what can only be bought in that region</strong> — Local beer, regional juices, regional sweets. The moment you place what a convenience store sells, comparison begins, and trust is whittled away.</li>
<li><strong>Place what connects to the property’s story</strong> — The juice served at breakfast, the local sake drunk at dinner. "That experience, once more, in my room" appeals to the body’s memory.</li>
<li><strong>Price within easy reach</strong> — Multiple pricing is the very habit that killed the minibar. An honest price returns, even on a thin margin, in the form of trust.</li>
</ol>

<h2>Display — what is invisible does not sell</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Place it where it can be seen</strong> — Not hidden inside the refrigerator. One item on top of it, one beside the table. On the shelf, one item at a time, with margin to breathe.</li>
<li><strong>Put the prices on a single card</strong> — This prevents check-out trouble from items overlooked. Add the price tag and one line about the producer ("the brewery in the next village").</li>
<li><strong>An assortment for the night</strong> — The customer of in-room sales is your guest’s own night self. Choose what the night welcomes (the makings of a warm drink, something a little sweet, one small bottle).</li>
</ul>

<h2>Operation — hold no stock, read it in gross profit</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Little stock, restock on sale</strong> — If you can agree with producers on "restock when it sells," you cut inventory risk and food loss at once (the discipline of food loss is in <a href="#food-loss-kitchen">Food Loss: Reducing Prep in the Kitchen</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Read it in gross profit</strong> — Small as the revenue looks, it is a monthly line that shows its worth in gross profit. High-margin goods are not rare here.</li>
<li><strong>Swap one item once a month</strong> — Keep what sells; move on from what does not. Keep updating the repeater’s "what will they have this time?"</li>
</ul>

<h2>Designing the settlement — asking without breaking trust</h2>

<p>The original form of a self-declaration culture is the unmanned farm stand in the countryside. Vegetables at a farmhouse eave and a box for payment — the trust design of that "honesty box" is treated in detail by <a href="#idea-morning-market-stay">An inn with Morning Market - Ideas for Making Morning Market, Light Truck Market, Eaves Market into Morning at Inn</a>. The guest-room version is simpler. A declaration card hung on the door, or one question at check-out — "Did you use anything from your room?" Whether that one question can be asked <strong>in the tone of thanks, not of interrogation</strong>: trust breaks, or holds, on the asking alone.</p>

<h2>The other side — when in-room commerce hurts the property</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>The weight of self-declaration</strong> — Some guests feel "under suspicion" in declaring. Writing it, or saying it in their own words — prepare one form that makes the hurdle lowest.</li>
<li><strong>Responsibility for alcohol</strong> — Minors, health, night noise. If you place it, keep the volume small (the wisdom of the 1974 miniature), and note that selling alcohol is subject to local regulation.</li>
<li><strong>Expiry and seasons</strong> — Chocolate at summer room temperature, cans freezing in winter. Placing food means expiry and storage management come with it.</li>
<li><strong>Over-expecting revenue</strong> — In-room commerce is trim on the experience, not the lead. The night guest room’s media can be made of paper as well as goods (<a href="#inroom-print-guestbook">In-Room Print and Guest Book: Designing Paper Media</a>).</li>
<li><strong>The revenge of free</strong> — There is also an operation that boldly makes one item free and turns it into reputation. An account that discards revenue to buy memory. Which to choose depends on the property’s story — but the option "not to place at all" is always worth keeping dear.</li>
</ul>

<p>The first step is one item on top of the refrigerator. Something of the region, something you can add one line of story to. With a card carrying the price and the producer’s line, and settlement as the one question at check-out. Watch a month of sales, then decide whether to add a second — in-room commerce is not built at once, like a vending machine, but <strong>is trust, stacked item by item</strong>.</p>

1c:T16de,<p>迷你吧和客房销售<strong>“室内商业”代替“自动售货机”</strong>。很多酒店把迷你吧当作“自动售货机”，认为只装饮料就可以了。然而，如果换个角度看，迷你吧就是“室内商业”。室内商务是一种在客房内为客人提供“购买”体验的系统。迷你吧提供的不仅仅是“销售”，更是“体验”。本文概述了如何将迷你吧和室内商务设计为室内商务而不是自动售货机。</p>

<h2>1. 卖什么——三个条件和一个故事</h2>
<ol>
<li><strong>只能在该地区购买：</strong>最好在迷你吧里出售只能在该地区购买的物品。当地啤酒、当地果汁、当地糖果以及其他只能在当地购买的物品也值得带回家。通过直接与当地制片人合作，故事就诞生了。</li>
<li><strong>与客栈的故事相关：</strong>迷你吧里最好出售与客栈故事有关的物品。客人可以“再次体验那种体验”，可以购买与在旅馆体验过的相同的东西，例如早餐时提供的果汁、在休息室喝的当地啤酒以及与客房内的设施相同的糖果。</li>
<li><strong>实惠的价格：</strong>迷你吧的价格应该是可以承受的。价格在500日元到800日元左右。如果太贵，客人会觉得贵而不会购买。通过使其价格实惠，我们鼓励客人尝试一下。</li>
</ol>

<h2>2. 把它放在哪里——在哪里以及如何看到它</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>将其放置在您可以看到的地方：</strong>将迷你吧放在可以看到的地方很重要。不要将其藏在冰箱中，而是将其放在显眼的地方，例如冰箱上的一件物品和桌子上的一件物品，以便客人知道有一个迷你吧。把它放在显眼的地方，购买的门槛就会降低。</li>
<li><strong>一次一项，保留一个空白：</strong>为迷你吧中的每件物品留出空间非常重要。打包太多物品会让您的客人感到混乱。通过为每个项目留出空间，每个项目都会脱颖而出。如何像一家很酷的咖啡馆一样呈现它：每件物品都放在木架子上，每件物品之间留有空白。</li>
<li><strong>添加价格标签和生产商的一行：</strong>迷你吧物品应包含价格标签和生产商的说明。诸如“这种啤酒是隔壁啤酒厂酿造的”或“这种果汁是用我们今天早上在田里采摘的水果制成的”这样的一行字，让客人“相信这个故事”。</li>
</ul>

<h2>3、运营——库存低，售完补货，看毛利</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>库存不足，售出后补货：</strong>迷你吧库存将会减少。库存过多会导致库存损失和粮食损失。库存有限，售完即会补充。通过与生产商签订“售后补货”合同可以降低库存风险。</li>
<li><strong>按毛利查看：</strong>迷你吧利润以毛利润而非销售额来衡量。有些产品的毛利率达到60-70%，所以看毛利就可以了解迷你吧的价值。每月计算一次毛利润并可视化迷你吧的价值。</li>
<li><strong>继续一件已售出的商品，替换一件未售出的商品：</strong>我们每月一次分析畅销产品和不畅销产品。一项已售出的商品将继续销售；一件未售出的商品将被替换。通过更换一件商品，总会有新的商品展示，回头客会想再看一眼。</li>
</ul>
